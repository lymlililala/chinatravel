China does not feature on most couples' shortlist for a honeymoon — which is precisely why it should. While the crowds flock to Bali and Maldives, honeymooners who choose China discover landscapes of extraordinary drama, ancient architecture that sets an atmosphere no purpose-built resort can replicate, and a private-feeling experience even at well-known destinations simply because most visitors don't stay long enough.

This guide covers where to go, when to go, and how to structure a China honeymoon that balances romance with practicality.

## Why China Works for a Honeymoon

A few things that make China genuinely appealing for couples:

**Scale and variety.** Within a few days' travel, you can move from a hypermodern city skyline to an ancient canal town to a misty mountain range. The scenery shifts dramatically.

**Exceptional accommodation.** China's luxury hotel scene has matured enormously. Amanfayun in Hangzhou. The Sukhothai Shanghai. Banyan Tree Lijiang. These properties rival the world's best, often at lower prices than equivalent properties in Europe or Southeast Asia.

**Intimacy at altitude.** Yunnan's ancient towns — Lijiang, Shangri-La, Dali — have a quiet, romantic quality at dawn and dusk that crowds cannot entirely destroy. A morning walk before the day-trippers arrive feels like the town belongs to you.

**Food as experience.** Sharing food is central to Chinese culture. A hot pot dinner for two, ordering dish after dish around a bubbling pot, is one of the great shared dining experiences in the world.

## Top Romantic Destinations in China

### 1. Guilin and Yangshuo — The Classic

The limestone karst landscape of Guilin has been inspiring poets and painters for over a thousand years. From the Li River, these peaks rise from flat valley floors in shapes so improbable that they look like brushstrokes on a scroll painting. The light here — particularly in the blue hour before sunset and after rain — is extraordinary.

**For couples:** Cruise the Li River from Guilin to Yangshuo on a private charter rather than the crowded ferry (arrange through your hotel or a local agency). Stay in Yangshuo rather than Guilin — the small-town atmosphere is far more romantic. The Moon Hill arch at golden hour. Cycling through rice paddies in the early morning.

**Where to stay:** Yangshuo Mountain Retreat (boutique, riverside) or Banyan Tree Yangshuo (luxury, remarkable valley views).

**Best time:** April–May and September–October for misty mornings and manageable temperatures.

### 2. Lijiang, Yunnan — Ancient Town Romance

Lijiang's old town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and, despite its popularity, still genuinely beautiful. Ancient wooden buildings line stone-paved canals. Snow-capped Jade Dragon Snow Mountain rises above the rooftops in the distance. In the evenings, the town glows with lantern light.

The key to romantic Lijiang is **avoiding the main tourist streets**. The lanes toward the Black Dragon Pool and the outskirts of Shuhe — the quieter satellite old town — give you the architecture and atmosphere without the crowds.

**For couples:** Hot springs at the resort hotels on the Jade Dragon Snow Mountain road. An early morning walk (6–7am) through the old town before vendors set up. Naxi culture performances in the evening. A two-night excursion to Shangri-La (Zhongdian) — higher, quieter, and stunningly beautiful in clear weather.

**Where to stay:** Banyan Tree Lijiang (iconic, surrounded by rice paddies) or The Linden Centre in Xizhou (a restored Bai manor house near Dali — unique and intimate).

### 3. Hangzhou — West Lake and Tea Country

Hangzhou's West Lake is where emperors came on holiday. The lake, surrounded by willow trees, pagodas, and pavilions, has been described in Chinese literature as the most beautiful place in the country. A boat trip to the lake's central islands at dusk, when the reflections multiply and the towers of the modern city disappear behind the tree line, is genuinely magical.

Beyond the lake, the nearby Longjing (Dragon Well) tea villages offer something more unusual for a honeymoon: walking through the world's most famous green tea terraces in spring, watching tea being picked and processed, and having it poured for you fresh.

**For couples:** Take the slow boat across West Lake rather than a speed boat. Hire bikes and cycle the full lake circuit (about 15km). Book a private tea ceremony at a heritage tea house in the Longjing area. Stay at the Amanfayun — a restored Hangzhou village converted into an extraordinary resort within walking distance of famous temples.

**Best time:** April (cherry blossoms) or October (clear skies, warm days).

### 4. Shanghai — Urban Glamour

For couples who want city energy as part of their honeymoon, Shanghai is the best Chinese city to base in. The Bund at night, a bar on a rooftop above the Pudong skyline, the intimacy of a cocktail bar in a restored colonial building in the French Concession — the city does romance on its own terms.

Shanghai is also one of the best places in China to eat well — internationally, with Japanese, French, and Italian restaurants at a standard that surprises visitors expecting only local cuisine.

**For couples:** The Waldorf Astoria Shanghai on the Bund for an anniversary dinner. Tianzifang, the French Concession gallery and café district, for afternoon wandering. A river cruise along the Huangpu at sunset. Day trip to Zhujiajiao water town — a Ming-dynasty canal town 45 minutes from central Shanghai that most couples find more intimate than the city centre.

### 5. Suzhou — The Garden City

If Shanghai is the honeymoon that goes out every night, Suzhou is the one that stays in. Located 30 minutes from Shanghai by high-speed train, Suzhou has more classical Chinese gardens than any other city — tranquil, walled landscapes designed for contemplation, with pavilions, ponds, and corridors of carved stone.

The **Humble Administrator's Garden** and the **Garden of the Master of the Nets** are the most celebrated. The Master of the Nets hosts traditional music performances on selected evenings — a genuinely moving experience in an intimate setting.

**Where to stay:** The Tongli Lakeview Hotel in nearby Tongli water town (Suzhou's quieter neighbour) or traditional courtyard guesthouses within the old city.

## Two Ready-Made Honeymoon Itineraries

### Itinerary 1: Romance and Landscape (10 Days)

This route prioritises scenic drama and outdoor atmosphere.

**Days 1–2: Guilin**
Arrive at Guilin Liangjiang International Airport. Check in to Yangshuo Mountain Retreat or Banyan Tree Yangshuo. Li River cruise on Day 2.

**Days 3–4: Yangshuo**
Cycling through the rice terraces and karst valleys. Moon Hill. Evening river bamboo raft. Hire a private guide for the full Yulong River cycling route.

**Days 5–7: Lijiang, Yunnan**
Fly from Guilin to Lijiang (approximately 1 hour 30 minutes). Old town exploration. Jade Dragon Snow Mountain. Shangri-La day trip. Hot spring evening.

**Days 8–9: Dali**
Bus or shared transfer from Lijiang to Dali (2 hours). Erhai Lake. Bai minority villages. Walking the old town at dawn.

**Day 10: Departure**
Fly from Dali or connect back through Kunming to international hub.

---

### Itinerary 2: Culture and Luxury (12 Days)

This route mixes architecture, history, and world-class hotels.

**Days 1–2: Beijing**
Great Wall at sunrise (hire a private car to Mutianyu; arrive before tour groups). Forbidden City with a licensed guide. Peking duck dinner at a top-tier restaurant.

**Days 3–5: Xi'an**
High-speed train from Beijing (4.5 hours). Terracotta Warriors with a private guide. Ancient city wall cycling at sunset. Muslim Quarter for evening street food.

**Days 6–8: Chengdu**
High-speed train from Xi'an (3.5 hours). Giant Panda Base (arrive at 8am for feeding time). Luxury resort base at the Chengdu IFS or the Waldorf Astoria Chengdu. Day trip to Leshan Giant Buddha. Hot pot dinner.

**Days 9–11: Shanghai**
Flight from Chengdu to Shanghai (2.5 hours). The Bund. French Concession. Zhujiajiao day trip. Farewell dinner at a rooftop restaurant above the Huangpu.

**Day 12: Departure**
Shanghai Pudong International Airport.

## When to Go on a China Honeymoon

| Season | Highlights | Drawbacks |
|---|---|---|
| March–May | Blooms, mild temperatures, Guilin at its best | Crowds at peak cherry blossom sites |
| June–August | Lush green landscapes, Yunnan at its most vivid | Summer heat in eastern China; rainy in south |
| September–October | Best all-round weather, clear skies, harvest colours | Golden Week (Oct 1–7) extremely crowded — avoid travelling then |
| November–February | Harbin ice festival (January), misty Guilin | Cold in north; Yunnan mild but shorter days |

The sweet spot for most China honeymoons is **late September through early October (avoiding the Golden Week itself)** or **April through mid-May**.

## Honeymoon Hotels Worth the Splurge

### Luxury (over USD 300/night)

**Amanfayun, Hangzhou** — A restored village within the hills above West Lake. Walking paths to ancient temples. Utterly private feeling despite the city below. The most romantic hotel in China for those who want tranquillity.

**Banyan Tree Lijiang** — Rice paddy setting with individual pavilion villas and private pools. Jade Dragon Snow Mountain views. Naxi minority experiences. A benchmark for luxury in Yunnan.

**The Sukhothai Shanghai** — The most peaceful luxury hotel in Shanghai's city centre. Tropical garden courtyards give it a resort feel in the middle of the former French Concession.

### Mid-Range (USD 120–300/night)

**Yangshuo Mountain Retreat** — Riverside location, international quality, extraordinary karst views.

**The Linden Centre, Xizhou, Yunnan** — A Bai manor house conversion 30 minutes from Dali. Cultural immersion at a genuinely high standard.

**West Lake Impression International Youth Hotel, Hangzhou** — Boutique quality at boutique prices, directly on West Lake.

## Practical Honeymoon Tips

**Book restaurants in advance.** The best Chinese restaurants — particularly in Shanghai, Beijing, and Chengdu — require reservations weeks ahead. Your hotel concierge is your most valuable resource for securing tables.

**Consider a private guide for one major experience.** A private guide at the Forbidden City or Terracotta Warriors transforms the visit from a crowded queue to an absorbing private lesson in history. Worth every yuan.

**Travel light between cities.** China's high-speed trains have luggage storage, not luggage handlers. Two medium-sized bags that you can lift overhead is the practical limit for comfortable train travel.

**Carry small-denomination cash.** Even in major cities, romantic spots — night markets, temple stalls, canal-side snack vendors — often prefer cash. Keep ¥300–500 in smaller bills at all times.
