China's fruit culture is as vast and varied as its landscapes. From the sun-drenched deserts of Ningxia, where watermelons grow in rocky fields, to the misty mountains of Zhejiang, home to giant lychee-like yangmei, each region offers a unique taste of sweetness. For travelers, exploring China through its fruits is a delicious way to connect with local geography, climate, and traditions. This guide takes you on a sweet tour, highlighting the best fruits across the country, where to find them, and practical tips for your journey.

## Top Fruits to Try by Region

### Ningxia: Se-sha Watermelon (硒砂瓜, Xī Shā Guā)
Grown in the rocky fields of Zhongwei (中卫, Zhōng Wèi), this watermelon is nicknamed "the Hermès of watermelons" for its exceptional sweetness and sandy texture. The secret lies in the "stone blanket" farming technique: locals cover the soil with gravel to reduce evaporation, increase temperature swings, and retain moisture. The result is a large, crimson-fleshed melon with a honey-like sweetness.

![A farmer holding a large seeded watermelon in a rocky field in Ningxia](https://images.pexels.com/photos/5054447/pexels-photo-5054447.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

- **Best time to visit:** July–September.
- **How to get there:** Take a high-speed train from Yinchuan (银川, Yín Chuān) to Zhongwei (about 1.5 hours). From Zhongwei, hire a taxi or join a tour to the melon fields along the scenic 66号公路 (Route 66).
- **Where to buy:** Local markets in Zhongwei or directly from farms. Prices vary, but expect around ¥2–5 per kg.

### Xinjiang: Hami Melon (哈密瓜, Hā Mì Guā)
Hami melon is so iconic that it shares its name with the city of Hami (哈密, Hā Mì) in eastern Xinjiang. With over 3,300 hours of annual sunshine and dramatic temperature swings, this melon develops a golden, honey-sweet flesh with a fragrant aroma. It has been cultivated for over 2,000 years and was once a tribute to imperial courts.

![A sliced Hami melon on a table in Xinjiang](https://images.pexels.com/photos/4772945/pexels-photo-4772945.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

- **Best time to visit:** June–October.
- **How to get there:** Fly to Hami from major cities like Beijing or Xi'an. Alternatively, take the high-speed train from Urumqi (about 2.5 hours).
- **Where to buy:** Hami's Nanhu Township (南湖乡, Nán Hú Xiāng) is the heart of melon cultivation. Visit local orchards or the city's fruit markets.

### Shandong: Peaches from Mengyin (蒙阴蜜桃, Méng Yīn Mì Táo)
Mengyin County (蒙阴, Méng Yīn) in Shandong is known as the "Honey Peach Capital." Two standout varieties are the Rainbow Flat Peach (彩虹蟠桃, Cǎi Hóng Pán Táo) and the Cream Flat Peach (奶油蟠桃, Nǎi Yóu Pán Táo). The Rainbow Peach has a crisp texture and sweetness reaching 16–20° Brix, while the Cream Peach offers a soft, creamy flesh with notes of vanilla.

![A basket of flat peaches from Mengyin, Shandong](https://images.pexels.com/photos/5550133/pexels-photo-5550133.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

- **Best time to visit:** June–August.
- **How to get there:** Take a high-speed train to Linyi (临沂, Lín Yí), then a bus to Mengyin (about 1 hour).
- **Where to buy:** Local orchards offer picking experiences. Peaches are also available in Linyi markets.

### Zhejiang: Xianju Dongkui Yangmei (仙居东魁杨梅, Xiān Jū Dōng Kuí Yáng Méi)
Yangmei, often called Chinese bayberry or waxberry, is a summer delicacy. The Dongkui variety from Xianju County (仙居, Xiān Jū) is the largest and sweetest, with fruits as big as ping-pong balls. Its flesh is juicy, with a perfect balance of sweetness and tartness. The region's mountainous terrain and strict no-pesticide policies ensure top quality.

![A handful of large red yangmei fruits in a basket](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1547981609-4b6bfe67ca0b?w=1200&q=85)

- **Best time to visit:** Mid-June to early July (short season!).
- **How to get there:** Take a high-speed train to Taizhou (台州, Tái Zhōu), then a bus to Xianju (about 1.5 hours).
- **Where to buy:** Local orchards offer picking. Fresh yangmei is also sold at Xianju markets. Note: the juice stains easily, so wear dark clothes.

### Guizhou: Fengtang Plum (蜂糖李, Fēng Táng Lǐ)
Hailing from Liuma Town (六马镇, Liù Mǎ Zhèn) in Zhenning County (镇宁, Zhèn Níng), the Fengtang plum is a "national treasure" of fruits. Its green skin hides an incredibly sweet (16–20° Brix) and crunchy flesh with a hint of tartness. The unique microclimate of the subtropical low-hot valley gives it a honey-like flavor.

![Green plums with a light bloom on a wooden table](https://images.pexels.com/photos/37295490/pexels-photo-37295490.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

- **Best time to visit:** Late June to July.
- **How to get there:** Fly to Guiyang (贵阳, Guì Yáng), then take a bus or drive to Zhenning (about 2 hours). From Zhenning, local taxis go to Liuma.
- **Where to buy:** Directly from orchards in Liuma. Look for plums with a slight yellow tinge for peak ripeness.

## Suggested Itinerary: A Fruit Lover's Road Trip

![West Lake Scenery, Hangzhou](/img/destinations/china-best-fruits-regional-guide/1.webp)

### Day 1–2: Xi'an & Shandong
Start in Xi'an (西安, Xī'ān) for Yanliang melons (阎良甜瓜, Yán Liáng Tián Guā) in May–June. Then take a high-speed train to Linyi (about 5 hours) to explore Mengyin's peach orchards.

### Day 3–4: Zhejiang
From Linyi, fly to Taizhou (about 2 hours) and head to Xianju for yangmei. Enjoy the misty mountains and fresh fruit.

### Day 5–6: Guizhou
Fly from Taizhou to Guiyang (about 2.5 hours), then travel to Zhenning for Fengtang plums. Combine with a visit to the Huangguoshu Waterfall (黄果树瀑布, Huáng Guǒ Shù Pù Bù).

### Day 7–8: Ningxia & Xinjiang
Fly from Guiyang to Yinchuan (about 2.5 hours), then take the high-speed train to Zhongwei for watermelons. Finally, fly to Hami (via Urumqi) for melons. Note: this leg requires more travel time; consider a separate trip to Xinjiang.

## Getting Around & Practical Tips

### Transportation
- **High-speed rail** connects most fruit-producing regions. Book tickets via the official 12306 app (English version available) or through travel agents.
- **Flights** are recommended for long distances (e.g., Shandong to Guizhou).
- **Local transport:** Buses and taxis are common. In rural areas, hiring a private driver is convenient.

### Payment & Booking
- **Alipay** and **WeChat Pay** are widely accepted. Set them up before your trip with an international credit card.
- **Tickets:** For popular attractions (e.g., Huangguoshu Waterfall), book online via Trip.com or Ctrip (English interface).

### Best Time to Visit
- **May–June:** Yanliang melons, Fengtang plums, yangmei.
- **July–September:** Peaches, watermelons, Hami melons.
- **October:** Late-season melons.

### Costs
- **Fruits:** ¥2–10 per kg at local markets; premium varieties (e.g., Fengtang plums) cost ¥30–60 per kg.
- **Transport:** High-speed rail from Xi'an to Linyi approx. ¥300 second class.
- **Accommodation:** Mid-range hotels ¥200–400 per night.

### Etiquette & Tips
- **Tasting:** It's common to sample before buying. Ask politely: "Kěyǐ cháng yīxià ma?" (可以尝一下吗？).
- **Bargaining:** Light haggling is acceptable at farmers' markets.
- **Stains:** Yangmei and plum juice stain fabrics; carry wet wipes.
- **Freshness:** Eat fruits soon after purchase. Many are perishable.

## FAQ

### What is the best time to try yangmei in Zhejiang?
Mid-June to early July is the peak season for Xianju Dongkui yangmei. The season lasts only about 15 days, so plan accordingly.

### Are these fruits available in supermarkets in big cities like Beijing or Shanghai?
Yes, many premium fruits are shipped to major cities, but they are often more expensive and less fresh than buying locally. For the best experience, visit the origin regions.

### How do I avoid scams when buying fruit at markets?
Stick to reputable vendors, compare prices, and avoid pre-packaged items that may hide low-quality fruit. Tasting before buying is standard.

### Can I bring fruits back home as souvenirs?
Fresh fruits may be restricted by customs. Check regulations for your home country. Dried or preserved fruits are safer options.

### Is it safe to eat raw fruits in China?
Yes, but wash them thoroughly. For fruits with thin skins (like yangmei), soak in salt water for 10 minutes to remove any residues.
