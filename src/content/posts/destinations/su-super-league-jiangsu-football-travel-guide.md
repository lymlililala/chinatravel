In 2025, a grassroots football phenomenon swept across Jiangsu province (江苏省, Jiāngsū Shěng). The Su Super League (苏超, Sū Chāo) – an amateur city football league – turned matchdays into province-wide travel events. With 85 matches, 2.43 million live spectators, and 22.2 billion online views in its debut season, the league generated ¥14.57 billion in cross-city tourism consumption. For every ¥1 spent on a ticket, ¥7.3 flowed into the local economy. This guide explains how to experience the Su Super League as a traveller: which cities to visit, what to eat, how to get tickets, and how to navigate matchday logistics.

## What is the Su Super League?

The Su Super League is a city-vs-city amateur football competition featuring all 13 prefecture-level cities of Jiangsu. Unlike China's professional leagues (中超, CSL), the Su Super League is built around local pride: players must hold local residency or have long-term local ties, and each team represents its city's culture, food, and identity. The league runs from May to November, with matches typically on weekends. Tickets cost ¥10–20, making it one of the most affordable live sports experiences in China.

## Top Cities & Their Rivalries

Each of Jiangsu's 13 cities has a distinct identity, and the Su Super League amplifies these differences. Here are the most iconic matchups:

### Nanjing vs. Suzhou: The Capital vs. The Economic Powerhouse
- **Nanjing (南京, Nánjīng)** – the provincial capital, nicknamed "Salted Duck Team" (盐水鸭队). Known for its imperial history and university culture.
- **Suzhou (苏州, Sūzhōu)** – the economic giant, famous for classical gardens and modern industry. Their rivalry reflects the tension between political capital and economic engine.

### Xuzhou vs. Suqian: The Chu-Han Derby
- **Xuzhou (徐州, Xúzhōu)** – ancient Pengcheng, home of Liu Bang, founder of the Han dynasty. Fans dress as "Han soldiers."
- **Suqian (宿迁, Sùqiān)** – hometown of Xiang Yu, the tragic hero of the Chu-Han Contention. Fans wear "Jiangdong Warriors" gear. The match is billed as a historical reenactment.

### Changzhou vs. Wuxi: The "Taihu Three Fools" Derby
- **Changzhou (常州, Chángzhōu)** – finished last in 2025, embracing the joke that they're "losing strokes." Their mascot is a pink dragon named "Changbao."
- **Wuxi (无锡, Wúxī)** – the "Peach Team" (水蜜桃队), known for its sweet peaches and manufacturing. Both cities sit on Lake Tai (太湖, Tài Hú), and their friendly banter is a highlight.

### Nantong vs. Taizhou: The Rise of the Underdogs
- **Nantong (南通, Nántōng)** – the 2025 champion, nicknamed "Nange" (Big Brother South). Their stadium, the "Southern Bird's Nest," holds over 32,000 fans.
- **Taizhou (泰州, Tàizhōu)** – the surprise dark horse, known for its morning tea culture. Their victory parade featured local delicacies.

## Matchday Experience: What to Expect

A Su Super League matchday is a festival. Arrive 2–3 hours early to explore the "Second Venue" (第二现场) – a fan zone outside the stadium with food stalls, live music, and games. Inside, expect:
- **Local food vendors**: From Nanjing salted duck to Yangzhou fried rice, each city showcases its cuisine.
- **Cultural performances**: Before kick-off, you might see traditional opera, dragon dances, or even actors dressed as historical figures (e.g., Sun Wukong in Huai'an).
- **Fan chants and tifos**: Each city has its own songs and banners. Don't be surprised to hear playful insults – it's all part of the fun.

## How to Get Tickets

Tickets are sold online via the official Su Super League mini-program on WeChat, and at the stadium box office on matchday. Prices are ¥10–20. For popular matches (e.g., Nanjing vs. Suzhou, or finals), buy at least a week in advance. Foreigners can purchase using Alipay or WeChat Pay – ensure your payment app is set up before arrival. Some cities offer "ticket + attraction" bundles; for example, a match ticket in Yangzhou grants free entry to all state-owned scenic spots on the day of the game.

## Getting There & Around

Jiangsu has one of China's best high-speed rail networks. All 13 cities are connected by HSR, with travel times between most cities under 2 hours. For matchday transport:
- **High-speed rail**: Book tickets via 12306 (English interface available). Many cities offer free shuttle buses from the train station to the stadium on matchdays.
- **Metro**: In Nanjing, Suzhou, Wuxi, and Xuzhou, the metro runs near the stadium. Check local transit apps.
- **Driving**: Stadiums have limited parking. Use the "P+R" (park and ride) system: park at designated lots (e.g., Lion Mountain Plaza in Suzhou) and take the metro one stop to the stadium.
- **Bike sharing**: Mobike and Hellobike are common. Stadiums usually have bike racks.

## Where to Stay

Book accommodation in the city centre near the stadium or a metro line. For a cultural experience, consider:
- **Nanjing**: Xinjiekou area – walking distance to Confucius Temple and the stadium.
- **Suzhou**: Guanqian Street – near classical gardens and the sports centre.
- **Yangzhou**: Slender West Lake area – combine matchday with a canal cruise.
- **Changzhou**: near Global Dinosaur City – a theme park that offers discounts with match tickets.

## Best Time to Visit

The Su Super League season runs from May to November. The best time to attend is September–October, when the weather is mild (20–25°C) and the league enters its knockout stages. Avoid July–August if you dislike heat and humidity; matches still happen but indoor stadiums have air conditioning. The final is usually in early November.

## Food: Eat Like a Local Fan

Each city's cuisine is a point of pride. Here's what to try at a match:
- **Nanjing**: Salted duck (盐水鸭, yánshuǐ yā) and duck blood vermicelli soup (鸭血粉丝汤, yāxuè fěnsī tāng).
- **Suzhou**: Sweet and sour spare ribs (糖醋排骨, tángcù páigǔ) and pine nut cake (松子糖, sōngzǐ táng).
- **Wuxi**: Spare ribs (无锡排骨, wúxī páigǔ) and peach-shaped buns (水蜜桃包, shuǐmìtáo bāo).
- **Yangzhou**: Fried rice (扬州炒饭, yángzhōu chǎofàn) and steamed crab meatballs (蟹粉狮子头, xièfěn shīzi tóu).
- **Xuzhou**: Spicy chicken (徐州辣子鸡, xúzhōu làzǐ jī) and donkey meat (驴肉, lǘròu).
- **Changzhou**: Radish and dried pork (常州萝卜干, chángzhōu luóbo gān) – often sold as a snack at the stadium.

## Practical Tips for Foreign Visitors

- **Payment**: Alipay and WeChat Pay are essential. Link your international credit card before arriving. Cash is rarely accepted at stadiums or food stalls.
- **Visa**: Most travellers need a tourist visa (L-visa). If you're transiting through Nanjing or Shanghai, check the 144-hour visa-free transit policy – it may cover your trip if you stay within Jiangsu.
- **Language**: Few stadium staff speak English. Download a translation app (e.g., Pleco) and have key phrases ready: "I want a ticket" (我要买票, wǒ yào mǎi piào), "Where is the stadium?" (体育场在哪里？tǐyùchǎng zài nǎlǐ?).
- **Etiquette**: Chinese fans are passionate but orderly. No flares or smoke bombs. Stand for the national anthem before kick-off. Avoid wearing rival colours in the home section.
- **Mobile data**: Buy a local SIM card at the airport or use an eSIM (e.g., Holafly). Free Wi-Fi is available in most stadiums.

## FAQ

### How do I buy Su Super League tickets as a foreigner?
Tickets are sold on the official WeChat mini-program (search "苏超" in Chinese). You'll need a WeChat account with WeChat Pay or Alipay. Alternatively, buy at the stadium box office on matchday – bring your passport. Prices are ¥10–20.

### Can I use a 144-hour visa-free transit to attend a match?
Yes, if you transit through Nanjing Lukou International Airport (NKG) and hold a passport from a eligible country (e.g., US, UK, Australia). You can stay in Jiangsu for 144 hours (6 days) without a visa. Confirm your eligibility with the airline before travel.

### What should I wear to a Su Super League match?
Wear your city's colours – if you don't have a team, pick one and buy a scarf at the stadium. Comfortable walking shoes are recommended. In summer, bring sunscreen and a hat; in autumn, a light jacket.

### Is it safe to travel between cities for matches?
Absolutely. Jiangsu has excellent public security. The high-speed rail network is efficient and safe. Stadiums have visible police presence. Keep your valuables secure in crowds.

### Can I bring my own food and drinks into the stadium?
Outside food and drinks are generally prohibited, but you can buy snacks and drinks inside. Stadiums have water stations. Alcoholic beverages are sold but may be limited to one per person.

### What if I don't speak Chinese?
While English is not widely spoken, stadium staff are used to helping foreigners. Use translation apps for communication. Many cities have English signage on metro lines. Consider joining a local expat football group – they often organise matchday meetups.
