Wuhan (武汉) sits at the confluence of the Yangtze and Han rivers — China's geographic centre, a natural crossroads that has made it a commercial hub for 3,000 years. Today it's a city of 11 million — China's seventh largest — with a university system that gives it the energy of a younger city than its history suggests.

The Yellow Crane Tower has been a pilgrimage site for Chinese poets since the Tang Dynasty. East Lake is 33 times the size of Hangzhou's West Lake. The Three Gorges downstream make Wuhan the jumping-off point for one of the world's great river journeys. This city deserves more international attention than it gets.

## Table of contents

## Yellow Crane Tower (黄鹤楼)

The single most celebrated building in the history of Chinese poetry — referenced in over 1,000 Tang and Song poems, the subject of Li Bai's famous admission that the Yellow Crane Tower left him with nothing to write. Located on Snake Hill (蛇山) overlooking the Yangtze River.

**The history:** The original wooden tower was built in 223 AD; it burned down and was rebuilt 27 times over 1,700 years. The current structure dates to 1985 — a reinforced concrete recreation of the Qing-era version. The lack of original fabric doesn't diminish the cultural resonance: this is the place Chinese people have been coming to look at the Yangtze and feel moved for 1,800 years.

**The view:** From the top (five stories, 51 metres), the panorama encompasses the Yangtze, the Han River confluence, the Wuhan Yangtze Bridge (武汉长江大桥, opened 1957 — the first bridge across the Yangtze), and both banks of the city. Best at golden hour.

**Ticket:** ¥80. Open 7:30 AM – 6:00 PM.

## East Lake (东湖)

Covering 87 km² — 33 times the size of Hangzhou's West Lake — East Lake is the largest urban lake in China. The shoreline is developed into a 100+ km cycling and walking path that winds through forests, wetlands, and botanical gardens.

**East Lake Greenway (东湖绿道):** A 101.98 km cycling/running loop around the full lake — one of China's best urban cycling routes. Bike rental from multiple points (¥5–20/hour). The full circuit takes a day; choose a section (Moshan South Road area is most scenic).

**Cherry Blossom at Wuhan University (武汉大学):** The university campus has one of China's most famous cherry blossom events — March–early April, when the sakura trees on the central campus hill bloom simultaneously. Free during off-peak; ticketed during peak bloom to manage the 30,000+ daily visitors.

## Hubei Provincial Museum (湖北省博物馆)

Free. One of China's finest provincial museums, housing the most important archaeological collection from the ancient Chu Kingdom (楚国, 770–223 BC) — the civilisation that produced some of the most sophisticated bronze work, silk textiles, and lacquerware in the ancient world.

**The Marquis Yi of Zeng's Tomb (曾侯乙墓):** The central exhibit — an intact 433 BC aristocrat's tomb containing 15,000 objects, including the famous **65-piece chime bell set (编钟)** — the most complex musical instrument from the ancient world. The bells, cast in bronze, produce two different notes when struck at different positions; performing on a 2,400-year-old musical instrument with modern museum acoustics is extraordinary to witness.

The museum also performs bell concerts (times posted at entrance). Free with museum admission.

## Hot Dry Noodles (热干面)

Wuhan's defining street food — wheat noodles alkaline enough to have a slight yellow tint, pre-cooked, tossed with sesame paste, sesame oil, and spring onion, eaten at room temperature. The texture is chewy; the sesame flavour rich and nutty. Available from 5 AM at every corner stall in the city for ¥8–12.

Not to be confused with cold noodles — hot dry noodles are served without broth or cold temperature; the sauce coats the noodles fully.

## Practical Tips

**Getting there:** Wuhan Tianhe Airport (WUH) — major hub with extensive domestic and international connections. High-speed rail: Beijing (5 hrs), Shanghai (5 hrs), Guangzhou (3.5 hrs), Chengdu (5 hrs).

**Three Gorges cruise:** Wuhan is the departure point for Yangtze River cruises through the Three Gorges to Chongqing — 3–5 days downstream, or 5–7 days upstream. A genuinely extraordinary river journey through one of China's most dramatic landscapes.

**Best season:** Spring (March–May, cherry blossoms and mild temperatures) or autumn (September–November).
