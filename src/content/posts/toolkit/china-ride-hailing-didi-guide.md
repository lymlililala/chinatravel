Didi (滴滴, Dīdī) is China's dominant ride-hailing platform — the equivalent of Uber, Lyft and Grab combined, with over 550 million registered users. For foreign tourists, it offers a massive advantage over hailing street taxis: you can show the destination on the app screen without any language exchange, the fare is pre-calculated, and the driver's route and license plate are tracked.

## Can Foreigners Use Didi?

Yes. Didi has an **international version** (DiDi International) available on the App Store and Google Play in most countries. This version:
- Accepts foreign phone numbers for registration
- Accepts international Visa, Mastercard and PayPal
- Has English language interface
- Shows estimated fare before booking
- Shares ride details for safety

**Versus the Chinese Didi app**: The international version has slightly less coverage in smaller cities. In major cities (Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Chengdu, Xi'an, Hangzhou, Chongqing) the international version works perfectly.

---

## Setup Instructions

1. **Download**: Search "DiDi" in the App Store or Google Play (not "滴滴" — find the English-language version)
2. **Register**: Use your foreign phone number. A verification SMS will be sent.
3. **Add payment**: Go to Profile → Payment → Add payment method. International Visa and Mastercard work. You may also link PayPal.
4. **Enable location**: The app needs location access to find you.
5. **Set language**: Should be English by default in the international version. If not, Settings → Language.

**Note**: If you have a Chinese SIM card or phone number (from a local SIM), you can also use the main Chinese Didi app, which has more ride types and better coverage in secondary cities.

## Booking a Ride

**Setting your destination**: Type or speak your destination in English or use the map. Didi's POI database is comprehensive — major hotels, attractions and landmarks are listed in English.

**Ride types**:
- **Express (快车 kuài chē)**: Standard ride at the lowest price. Most used option.
- **Premier / DiDi Plus**: Higher-quality vehicle; costs more.
- **DiDi Max (7-seater)**: Good for families or groups with luggage.
- **DiDi Bike**: E-bikes and regular bikes for short city trips.

**Fare estimate**: Shown before you confirm. Typical fares:
- Airport to city center (most major cities): ¥60–150
- Short city trip (3–5 km): ¥15–30
- Longer city trip (10–15 km): ¥35–60

**Surge pricing (高峰加价)**: Applied during peak hours (07:30–09:00, 17:30–19:30) and during rain. The app shows the multiplier (e.g., 1.5x) before you confirm. You can wait for the surcharge to drop or accept it.

## Payment

**In-app payment**: Charged automatically to your linked card after the ride. No cash, no tipping required.

**Cash rides**: Some drivers accept cash; this option appears at booking. Useful if your payment setup is incomplete.

**Receipt**: Automatically sent to your app history. Useful for expense reporting.

**Refund disputes**: If the driver took a significantly incorrect route, you can report it in the app for a fare adjustment.

---

## Safety Features

Didi added multiple safety features after a widely reported safety incident in 2018, including:
- **Real-time tracking**: Your ride is tracked by GPS and stored.
- **Emergency button**: In-app red button connects to Chinese emergency services (110/120) with your location.
- **Share ride**: Send a live link to someone who can track your journey.
- **Driver and vehicle verification**: Cross-check the driver name, car model and license plate shown in the app before entering.

**Best practice**: Always verify the car matches what the app shows before getting in. Ask "你叫什么名字？" (Nǐ jiào shénme míngzi? — What is your name?) or show your phone with the driver photo to confirm.

## Communicating with Your Driver

Most Didi drivers do not speak English. The app has a built-in chat with preset phrases, including:
- "I'm outside, waiting" (translated to Mandarin)
- "I'll be right down"
- "Can you wait?"

For more specific communication:
- Show the driver your destination on the map in the app
- Use Google Translate voice mode to explain any additional instructions
- Show hotel address on your phone screen

## When Didi Might Not Work

**Rural areas**: Didi coverage is good in cities with populations above 1 million. In small towns, villages and truly rural areas, drivers may be very scarce.

**During heavy demand**: During Spring Festival, National Day and heavy rain, wait times extend significantly. Have a backup plan.

**Technical issues**: Didi requires a working internet connection. If you're on a slow SIM or struggling with VPN settings, the app may load slowly. Keep your data connection strong.

---

## Alternatives to Didi

**Street taxis (出租车 chūzū chē)**: Still abundant and legal. Rules:
- Legitimate taxis have meters — insist it's turned on ("请打表" qǐng dǎ biǎo)
- Don't accept a "fixed price" offer from drivers who approach you at airports or train stations (these are unlicensed)
- Show your destination written in Chinese if possible

**Gaode Maps (高德地图)**: Amap's navigation app has ride-hailing built in, offering access to multiple services including Didi competitors.

**AutoNavi / Amap Taxi**: Similar to Didi, available in major cities.

**Hotel taxi arrangement**: Four and five-star hotels have doormen who arrange metered taxis and can communicate your destination to the driver — a good fallback for airport runs when you're exhausted.

---

## Didi for Intercity Transfer

Didi doesn't operate intercity rides (it's a city-based service). For transfers between cities, use:
- High-speed rail (recommended for most routes)
- Intercity bus (for routes without rail service)
- Domestic flights (for long distances)
- DiDi can take you from the city to the intercity bus station or train station

## Cost Comparison: Didi vs Alternatives

| Situation | Didi Express | Street Taxi | Metro |
|-----------|-------------|------------|-------|
| Airport to hotel (city center, 30km) | ¥80–130 | ¥90–140 | ¥25–35 |
| Hotel to attraction (5km) | ¥20–30 | ¥25–35 | ¥4–6 |
| Late night return (midnight) | ¥30–45 (+surge) | ¥35–45 | Not available |
| Rain (peak hours) | ¥40–60 (+1.5x surge) | ¥35–45 | ¥4–6 |

Didi is consistently cheaper than taxis (10–25% typically) because drivers don't have the same overhead. The metro is always cheapest but requires carrying luggage through stations.

Didi has transformed getting around Chinese cities for foreign tourists. The combination of English language interface, international card payment, in-app map communication and built-in safety features removes the single biggest practical challenge of urban China navigation — the language barrier at point of transport.
