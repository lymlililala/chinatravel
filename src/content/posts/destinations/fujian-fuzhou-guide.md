Fuzhou (福州) was one of the five original treaty ports opened in 1842 — a city of 600,000 at the time, already a millennium-old river port at the Min River mouth, the capital of Fujian Province. While Xiamen absorbed most subsequent development attention, Fuzhou maintained its historical fabric — particularly the extraordinary **Three Lanes and Seven Alleys (三坊七巷)**, one of the best-preserved historical residential districts in southeastern China.

## Three Lanes and Seven Alleys (三坊七巷)

The core historical district — approximately 40 hectares of Tang and Song Dynasty street layout with predominantly Ming and Qing residential buildings. The name refers to the three lanes (坊) running north-south and seven alleys (巷) running east-west that form the grid.

**Why this matters:** Unlike many "ancient street" developments that have been largely rebuilt, the Three Lanes and Seven Alleys retain genuine 300–800-year-old buildings in continuous residential use until very recently. Over 200 protected structures survive.

**Social context:** The district was traditionally the residence of Fuzhou's intellectual and official classes — the Min culture emphasis on Confucian scholarship meant that for 1,000 years, the families who produced Fujian's most famous officials, scholars, and reformers lived here.

**Famous residents:** Lin Zexu (林则徐, the official who destroyed the British opium) was born in the district; Yan Fu (严复, the translator who introduced Darwin and Spencer to China) lived here; Bing Xin (冰心, 20th-century novelist) grew up in these alleys.

**Ticket:** Free (outer lanes) or ¥120 for multiple house museums.

**What to look for:** The gabled roof silhouettes against the sky — the horseback walls (马背墙) curve differently here than the Huizhou style; the carved wooden screens visible through doorways; the private family gardens accessible through the house museums.

## Jasmine Tea (茉莉花茶) Heritage

Fuzhou has been the production centre of China's jasmine tea for over 700 years. The specific technique — scenting green tea with fresh jasmine flowers harvested at evening (when fragrance is highest) and blending overnight through multiple infusion cycles — was developed in the Fuzhou area.

**The process:** The freshest jasmine flowers are harvested in the evening, combined with green tea leaf, sealed overnight, then separated and re-combined with fresh flowers multiple times. Premium jasmine teas are scented 3–7 times; the very finest (特级) are scented up to 9 times.

**Where to buy:** Fuzhou's traditional tea district around **Zhongting Street (中亭街)** has multiple specialist tea dealers. The quality difference between standard jasmine tea (single scenting) and multi-scented premium grades is dramatic.

## Yongquan Temple (涌泉寺) on Gushan Mountain (鼓山)

12 km northeast of Fuzhou city — the most significant Buddhist monastery in Fujian, founded in 783 AD on the forested slopes of Gushan. The **Cliff Inscriptions (摩崖石刻)** covering a 2 km stretch of mountain path behind the monastery represent the largest collection of Buddhist stone calligraphy in southern China — over 480 inscriptions from the Song Dynasty onward.

**Ticket:** ¥10. Getting there: Bus or taxi from the city centre (45 minutes).

## Min River Delta (闽江)

The Min River forms a rich estuary landscape south of Fuzhou — historically one of the most important export ports for Chinese porcelain, tea, and lacquerware.

**Mawei Shipyard (马尾造船厂):** China's first modern shipyard, established in 1866 — the Fuzhou Ship Zhangming School (福建船政学堂) attached to it was China's first Western-curriculum educational institution, producing the first generation of Chinese naval officers, engineers, and translators.

## Practical Tips

**Getting to Fuzhou:** Fuzhou Changle Airport (FOC) — well-connected internationally (particularly to Southeast Asia) and domestically. High-speed rail from Xiamen (1 hr), Shanghai (3 hrs), Beijing (5 hrs).

**Combining with Xiamen:** Fuzhou + Xiamen makes a natural 5-day Fujian circuit — high-speed rail between them (1 hr), with options to add the tulou countryside.
