## Why Cycle in China?

China is the undisputed kingdom of cycling. The country has more bicycles than anywhere else on Earth, and the dockless bike-sharing revolution that started here has transformed urban mobility. In 2026, Chinese cities are dotted with millions of shared bikes — just scan a QR code and ride.

Cycling gives you a perspective that no other transport mode can match. You'll see neighbourhoods that tourists never visit, stumble upon street food stalls down side alleys, and experience the rhythm of Chinese urban life at ground level. Some of my most memorable China moments have been on two wheels — cycling the old city walls of Xi'an at sunset, weaving through Shanghai's French Concession on a lazy Sunday morning, and pedalling along Hangzhou's West Lake as the mist rolled in.

Plus, it's cheap. Most shared bike rides cost ¥1.5-2.5 ($0.21-0.35) for 30 minutes. A full day of cycling costs less than a cup of coffee.

## The Dockless Bike Revolution

### How It Started

China pioneered the dockless bike-sharing model. Companies like Mobike and Ofo launched in 2016-2017, flooding cities with brightly coloured bikes you could unlock with your phone and leave anywhere. The initial chaos — bikes piled up on sidewalks, blocking entrances and creating urban clutter — has been largely resolved through regulation and consolidation.

### The Current Landscape (2026)

The market has consolidated to three major players:

1. **HelloRide (哈啰出行)** — The market leader. Blue and white bikes. Best coverage in second and third-tier cities
2. **Meituan Bikes (美团单车)** — Yellow bikes. Integrated into the Meituan super-app. Strong in tier-1 cities
3. **DiDi Bikes (青桔单车)** — Green bikes. Part of the DiDi ride-hailing platform. Growing rapidly

All three offer standard pedal bikes and electric-assist bikes in most cities. The bikes are sturdy, simple, and designed for short urban trips (1-5km typically).

### Bike Quality

Modern shared bikes are much better than the early models:
- **3-speed or single-speed** gearing (adequate for flat cities)
- **Pneumatic tires** that don't go flat (self-sealing technology)
- **Basket or front rack** for bags
- **Built-in lock** — no need to find a docking station
- **GPS tracking** — you can find the nearest bike on the app
- **Adjustable seat** height on most models

The bikes aren't performance machines — they weigh 15-20kg and aren't built for speed. But for casual city riding, they're perfect.

## How to Use Shared Bikes

### Step-by-Step Guide

1. **Download the app:** Alipay and WeChat both have built-in bike-sharing mini-programs, or download the standalone apps
2. **Register:** You'll need your phone number and a payment method (Alipay or WeChat Pay)
3. **Find a bike:** Open the app to see nearby bikes on the map
4. **Unlock:** Scan the QR code on the bike's rear fender or lock. The bike unlocks automatically
5. **Ride:** The meter starts running
6. **Lock:** When you're done, push down the lock lever. The app automatically ends the ride and charges you

**For foreigners:** The registration process has become easier. Most apps now accept foreign phone numbers and passports. Alipay and WeChat Pay are essential — you can't use these services without them.

### Deposit vs No-Deposit

Most bike-sharing services have eliminated the deposit requirement. You pay per ride directly through Alipay or WeChat Pay. Some services offer monthly passes (¥10-20 / $1.40-2.80) for unlimited 30-minute rides, which is excellent value if you're staying for a while.

## Which Bike-Sharing App to Choose

### HelloRide (哈啰出行)

- **Best coverage** across China, especially in smaller cities
- **Bike types:** Standard, electric-assist
- **Pricing:** ¥1.5/15 min for standard, ¥2.5/15 min for electric
- **Monthly pass:** ¥12.8 ($1.80) for unlimited standard rides (30 min each)
- **Foreigner-friendly:** Yes, through Alipay mini-program

### Meituan Bikes (美团单车)

- **Best in tier-1 cities** (Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Shenzhen)
- **Bike types:** Standard, electric-assist
- **Pricing:** ¥1.5/15 min for standard, ¥2.5/15 min for electric
- **Monthly pass:** ¥14.8 ($2.10) for unlimited standard rides
- **Foreigner-friendly:** Yes, through the Meituan app or Alipay

### DiDi Bikes (青桔单车)

- **Growing network,** strong in cities where DiDi is dominant
- **Bike types:** Standard, electric-assist
- **Pricing:** ¥1.5/15 min for standard, ¥2/15 min for electric
- **Monthly pass:** ¥10 ($1.40) for unlimited standard rides
- **Foreigner-friendly:** Yes, through DiDi app

**My recommendation:** If you have Alipay, start with the Alipay bike-sharing mini-program — it aggregates bikes from multiple providers on one map. No need to download three separate apps.

## Traditional Bike Rental Shops

### When to Use Traditional Rental

Shared bikes are great for short trips, but they have limitations:
- No gears for hilly areas
- No child seats
- No panniers for touring
- Basic quality isn't suitable for long rides
- You need mobile payment

Traditional bike rental shops are worth seeking out for:
- **Day-long cycling tours** (shared bikes get expensive for full-day use)
- **Hilly or mountainous areas** where you need gears
- **Cycling with children** (child seats, tag-alongs)
- **Multi-day cycling trips**

### Where to Find Rental Shops

- **Tourist areas:** Often near popular cycling spots (West Lake in Hangzhou, City Wall in Xi'an)
- **Hostels and hotels:** Some rent bikes to guests or can recommend nearby shops
- **Search on Dianping** (大众点评) for 自行车出租 (bicycle rental)

### Typical Pricing

- **Standard city bike:** ¥30-50/day ($4.20-7)
- **Mountain bike:** ¥50-100/day ($7-14)
- **Electric bike:** ¥60-120/day ($8.40-16.80)
- **Tandem bike:** ¥80-150/day ($11.20-21)
- **Deposit:** ¥200-500 ($28-70), returned when you bring the bike back

### Notable Rental Locations

- **Xi'an City Wall:** Multiple rental shops near the South Gate. ¥45 ($6.30) for 2 hours, ¥90 ($12.60) for a full circuit
- **Hangzhou West Lake:** Rental shops along Nanshan Road and Hubin Road. ¥30-50/day ($4.20-7)
- **Guilin/Yangshuo:** Numerous rental shops for countryside cycling. ¥20-40/day ($2.80-5.60)
- **Gulangyu Island (Xiamen):** No cars allowed — cycling is the main transport. ¥30-60/day ($4.20-8.40)

## Electric Bikes and E-Scooters

### The E-Bike Phenomenon

China has over 300 million electric bikes and scooters. They're everywhere — silently zipping through traffic, carrying families, delivering food. For visitors, electric bikes offer an easy way to cover longer distances without breaking a sweat.

### Electric Shared Bikes

All three major bike-sharing companies offer electric-assist bikes. These have a small motor that provides pedal assistance up to 25km/h. They don't require a licence.

**How they differ from standard shared bikes:**
- Heavier and larger
- Small battery pack on the rear rack
- Motor assists when you pedal (you still need to pedal, it's not a scooter)
- More expensive per minute
- Fewer available than standard bikes

### Electric Scooter Rental

Proper electric scooters (no pedalling required) are available for rent in tourist areas, but there are important legal considerations:
- **E-scooters technically require a licence** in many cities
- **Riding without a licence** can result in a fine (¥50-200 / $7-28)
- **Many cities restrict e-scooters** in certain areas
- **Foreigners on e-scooters** attract police attention

**My advice:** Stick to electric-assist shared bikes or rent from a reputable shop that provides insurance and proper documentation. Avoid unlicensed e-scooter rental in tourist areas — it's not worth the hassle.

## Best Cycling Cities in China

### Tier 1: Excellent for Cycling

**Hangzhou** — China's most bicycle-friendly city. Dedicated bike lanes along West Lake, flat terrain, and the country's first bike-sharing system. A cyclist's paradise.

**Chengdu** — Flat, relaxed, and increasingly bike-friendly. The city has added dedicated cycling lanes along the Jin River and in the Tianfu New Area. Plus, cycling between food stops is the perfect way to eat your way through the city.

**Suzhou** — Flat, scenic, and compact. Cycling along the canals and between gardens is delightful. The old town is largely off-limits to cars.

**Xiamen** — The island city has a beautiful coastal cycling path (the Skyway), and Gulangyu Island is car-free.

### Tier 2: Good for Cycling

**Shanghai** — Extensive bike lanes in the French Concession and along the Huangpu River. Traffic can be intimidating, but the city has made major improvements in cycling infrastructure.

**Beijing** — Wide roads with bike lanes, but traffic is heavy and air quality can be poor. Best for cycling early morning or on dedicated paths.

**Nanjing** — Tree-lined boulevards, the Qinhuai River path, and Purple Mountain area make for excellent riding.

**Guilin/Yangshuo** — Stunning karst scenery on flat country roads. One of China's best cycling destinations for scenery.

### Tier 3: Possible but Challenging

**Guangzhou** — Hot, humid, and busy. Cycling infrastructure is improving but still limited in the city centre.

**Xi'an** — The city wall ride is legendary, but cycling in the old town streets is chaotic. Better to cycle outside the walls.

**Chongqing** — Forget it. This is a mountain city with steep hills everywhere. Cycling here is for the extremely dedicated.

## Top Urban Cycling Routes

### Beijing: Along the Tonghui River (通惠河)

- **Distance:** 12km one way
- **Difficulty:** Easy (flat, paved)
- **Highlights:** Historic canal, quiet green spaces, contrasting old and new Beijing
- **Start:** Jianguomen, near the CBD
- **End:** Tongzhou district

### Shanghai: West Bund to North Bund

- **Distance:** 15km one way
- **Difficulty:** Easy (flat, dedicated cycling path)
- **Highlights:** The Bund skyline, riverside parks, art galleries, spectacular night views
- **Start:** Long Museum (West Bund)
- **End:** North Bund Green Space

### Hangzhou: Complete West Lake Circuit

- **Distance:** 15km loop
- **Difficulty:** Easy (flat, well-maintained path)
- **Highlights:** Every famous West Lake viewpoint, pagodas, causeways, lotus ponds
- **Start/End:** Any point on the lake
- **Best time:** Early morning (before 8am) or after 6pm to avoid crowds

### Chengdu: Jin River Greenway (锦江绿道)

- **Distance:** 20km (select sections as desired)
- **Difficulty:** Easy
- **Highlights:** Riverside parks, traditional bridges, teahouse stops, city views
- **Start:** Hejiang Pavilion
- **End:** Sansheng Flower Town

### Xi'an: City Wall Circuit

- **Distance:** 13.7km loop
- **Difficulty:** Moderate (some uphill sections on the wall ramps)
- **Highlights:** 360-degree views of the old city, watchtowers, sunset over the pagodas
- **Cost:** Bike rental on the wall ¥45/2 hours ($6.30)
- **Best time:** Late afternoon for sunset

### Guilin to Yangshuo: Li River Countryside

- **Distance:** 60km (or shorter sections)
- **Difficulty:** Moderate (mostly flat, some gentle hills)
- **Highlights:** Karst peaks, rice paddies, village life, river views
- **Bike rental:** Available in both Guilin and Yangshuo, ¥30-50/day ($4.20-7)
- **Tip:** Ride from Yangshuo to Yulong River for the most scenic section

### Xiamen: Island Ring Road

- **Distance:** 43km loop (or shorter sections)
- **Difficulty:** Easy to moderate
- **Highlights:** Coastal views, beaches, university area, fishing harbours
- **Start:** Any point on Huandao Road

## Cycling Safety in China

### Traffic Culture

Chinese traffic culture is... different. Here's what you need to know:
- **Traffic signals are suggestions** for many cyclists and e-bike riders. Red lights are frequently ignored, especially for right turns
- **Right of way** belongs to whoever is biggest and most confident
- **E-bikes are everywhere** and often silent. They'll pass you closely from behind without warning
- **Pedestrians step into bike lanes** without looking
- **Delivery riders (外卖骑手)** are the most dangerous road users — they're rushing to meet deadlines and take risks

### Safety Tips

1. **Wear a helmet.** This is non-negotiable. Bike-sharing bikes don't provide them, so bring your own or buy one (¥50-100 / $7-14 at Decathlon stores)
2. **Ride defensively.** Assume nobody can see you and everyone might do something unexpected
3. **Use hand signals** — they're not common in China but they help
4. **Avoid peak hours** (7:30-9am, 5-7pm) when traffic is heaviest
5. **Stay in bike lanes** wherever they exist
6. **Be extra careful at intersections** — right-turning vehicles rarely yield
7. **Use lights at night** — shared bikes have reflectors but no lights. Buy a small clip-on light (¥15-30 / $2.10-4.20)

### What to Do in an Accident

1. **Call 122** (traffic police) if anyone is injured
2. **Call 120** (ambulance) if medical attention is needed
3. **Take photos** of the scene, your bike, and any injuries
4. **Exchange contact information** with the other party
5. **Contact your travel insurance** as soon as possible
6. **Don't admit fault** — let the police determine responsibility

## Cycling Laws and Regulations

### Key Rules

- **Cycling is not allowed** on expressways and some major roads
- **Cycling under the influence** of alcohol is illegal (fine ¥50-200 / $7-28)
- **Carrying passengers** on a standard bike is technically illegal but rarely enforced
- **Cycling while using a phone** is illegal in some cities (fine ¥20-50 / $2.80-7)
- **Reflective clothing** is required at night in some cities (rarely enforced for cyclists)

### Where You Can't Ride

- Elevated highways and expressways
- Some pedestrian-only streets and shopping areas
- Certain tunnels and bridges
- Gulangyu Island (Xiamen) — walking only in some areas
- Inside many parks (though this varies)

### Electric Bike Regulations

Electric bikes in China must:
- Have pedals (no scooter-style e-bikes in the shared system)
- Not exceed 25km/h motor-assisted speed
- Weigh less than 55kg
- Be registered in some cities (the rental companies handle this)

## Long-Distance Cycling in China

### Popular Long-Distance Routes

**Sichuan-Tibet Highway (G318):**
- **Distance:** 2,150km from Chengdu to Lhasa
- **Duration:** 25-35 days
- **Difficulty:** Extremely challenging (high altitude, mountain passes, remote areas)
- **Best time:** May-June or September-October
- **Organised tours:** ¥15,000-25,000 ($2,100-3,500) including support vehicle

**Hainan Island Circuit:**
- **Distance:** 800km loop
- **Duration:** 7-10 days
- **Difficulty:** Moderate (tropical heat is the main challenge)
- **Best time:** November-March

**Guangxi Karst Circuit:**
- **Distance:** 300-500km
- **Duration:** 4-6 days
- **Difficulty:** Moderate
- **Best time:** October-April

### Practical Considerations

- **Road conditions** vary enormously. Major highways have wide shoulders; minor roads can be rough
- **Accommodation** is available in every town — you don't need to camp
- **Food** is cheap and plentiful. ¥30-50 ($4.20-7) per meal at local restaurants
- **Navigation:** Use Baidu Maps — it has cycling-specific routing
- **Bike repair:** Every town has bike repair shops (修理铺). ¥5-20 ($0.70-2.80) for most repairs
- **Water:** Available everywhere. ¥2-3 ($0.28-0.42) per bottle

## What to Wear and Bring

### For Casual City Cycling

- Comfortable clothes and closed-toe shoes
- Helmet (bring your own or buy at Decathlon)
- Sunglasses and sunscreen
- Small backpack or crossbody bag
- Water bottle
- Phone with bike-sharing apps

### For Longer Rides

- Padded cycling shorts (seriously, your backside will thank you)
- Moisture-wicking top
- Cycling gloves
- Rain jacket (weather changes quickly)
- Bike lights (front and rear)
- Basic repair kit (tube, pump, tyre levers — available at Decathlon for ¥50-100 / $7-14)
- First aid supplies

### Where to Buy Cycling Gear in China

- **Decathlon (迪卡侬):** France-based sporting goods chain with stores in every major city. Excellent prices, decent quality. Helmets from ¥50 ($7), cycling shorts from ¥60 ($8.40)
- **JD.com:** Online delivery, wide selection, next-day delivery in cities
- **Taobao:** Best prices but requires more Chinese language ability to navigate

## Common Problems and Solutions

### Problem: Can't Find a Shared Bike

**Solution:** Walk a block or two — bikes cluster around metro stations and shopping areas. Use the app map to locate the nearest available bike. Early mornings can be scarce as commuters have taken them.

### Problem: Bike Is Broken

**Solution:** Report it in the app (there's a "faulty bike" button), lock it, and find another one. You won't be charged for the broken bike ride.

### Problem: Can't Lock the Bike

**Solution:** Make sure the bike is on flat ground and the lock mechanism isn't obstructed. Push down firmly. If it still won't lock, try restarting the app. As a last resort, contact customer service through the app.

### Problem: App Won't Unlock the Bike

**Solution:** Check that Bluetooth is enabled. Move closer to the bike. Try scanning the QR code again. If the bike has been reported faulty, it won't unlock.

### Problem: Overcharged

**Solution:** Check your ride history in the app. If you were charged for time you didn't ride (the bike wasn't locked properly), submit a dispute through the app. Refunds are usually processed within 24 hours.
