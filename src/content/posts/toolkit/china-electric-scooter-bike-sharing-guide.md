# China Bike & E-Scooter Sharing Guide 2026

China has the world's most extensive bike-sharing infrastructure — over 20 million shared bikes and e-scooters deployed across hundreds of cities, operated primarily by three platforms: **Meituan Bike (美团单车), Hello Bike (哈啰单车)**, and **Didi Bike (滴滴青桔)**. In virtually every city you visit, you can unlock a bike within 30 seconds and be cycling within a minute.

For foreign visitors, bike sharing offers something Didi cannot: spontaneous exploration at human speed. A 3-hour bike ride through Chengdu's old neighborhoods, along Beijing's hutongs, or beside Hangzhou's West Lake reveals a completely different city than the view from a taxi window.

## The Three Platforms Compared

| Platform | App | Best For | E-bike Option |
|----------|-----|---------|---------------|
| Meituan Bike (美团单车) | Meituan app | Beijing, Tier-2 cities | Yes (青骑) |
| Hello Bike (哈啰单车) | Hello app | Tier-2/3 cities, strong in south | Yes (哈啰电动) |
| Didi Bike (滴滴青桔) | Didi app | Cities where Didi dominates | Yes |

**Recommendation for visitors:** Install **Meituan** (already needed for food delivery) and **Hello Bike** (the most widely distributed outside major cities). This covers approximately 90% of situations.

## Setting Up: Foreign Phone Registration

**Step 1:** Download the app (Meituan or Hello Bike) from App Store or Google Play.

**Step 2:** Register with your foreign phone number (international numbers are accepted; you'll receive an SMS verification code).

**Step 3:** Link payment — WeChat Pay or Alipay (both accept foreign cards as of 2023).

**Step 4:** Deposit. Most platforms require a ¥99 refundable deposit; pay via the linked payment method. The deposit is refunded within 1–5 business days when you close your account.

**Alternative (no deposit required):** Users with **Sesame Credit (芝麻信用, Alipay's credit score)** above a certain threshold are exempt from deposits. Foreign card users typically don't have Sesame Credit scores, so the deposit is usually required.

## Riding Costs

**Regular bikes:** ¥0.5–1.5 per 30 minutes (varies by city and bike type). A full day of city cycling typically costs ¥5–15.

**E-bikes (electric assist):** ¥1.5–2.5 per 30 minutes. Well worth the additional cost for longer rides or hilly terrain — the electric assist is very effective.

**Monthly passes:** Available on all platforms (¥25–35/month for unlimited standard bike use); if you're visiting for 3+ weeks, this pays for itself quickly.

---

## Finding and Unlocking Bikes

**Via the app:** Open the app → enable location → bikes appear as dots on the map; select the nearest bike → scan the QR code on the bike's handlebars → bike unlocks.

**Walking to a clustered area:** In most Chinese cities, bikes are clustered near metro exits, shopping malls, and busy intersections. Finding a bike without the app is usually possible by walking toward any busy pedestrian area.

**Ending the ride:** Park in any designated bike parking area (painted white rectangles on the pavement) or in permitted zones (shown in the app). Do not park in red zones; you will be charged a penalty (¥5–20).

## E-Scooters (Electric Scooters)

Beyond the electric-assist bicycles, several cities also have **full electric scooter sharing** (not pedal-assist but throttle-controlled). These are subject to stricter regulations:

**Helmet requirement:** Chinese law requires helmets on electric scooters. In practice, enforcement varies by city; in some cities (Shanghai, Beijing) it is strictly enforced; in others, rarely. The platforms providing e-scooters typically have helmets attached to the vehicle or in a storage compartment.

**Speed:** Most shared e-scooters are limited to 25 km/h (the legal limit for Class 1 electric bicycles in China).

**License requirement:** In many cities, electric scooters above 25 km/h require a driving license; shared platforms stay within the 25 km/h legal limit to avoid this restriction.

## Best Bike Routes in Major Cities

**Beijing:** The **hutong network** around Gulou, Nanluoguxiang, and the Bell/Drum Tower area is the ideal cycling territory — narrow alleys that cars can't enter, historical architecture, and street food at every corner. Best morning route: start at Shichahai Lake, circle the lakes, enter the hutong network.

**Shanghai:** The **former French Concession** (法租界) is the city's best cycling neighborhood — plane tree-lined streets, pre-war architecture, garden restaurants. Route: Fuxing Park → Xintiandi → Tianzifang.

**Hangzhou:** The **West Lake circuit** (10 km around the full lake) is one of China's most pleasurable urban bike routes — no cars on the causeways, tea plantations visible on the hillsides, and lakeside pavilions to stop at.

**Chengdu:** The **Jincheng Greenway (锦城绿道)** is a 100-km circular greenway around the central city, largely separated from motor traffic; any section is excellent for cycling.

## Safety Notes

**Traffic rules:** Bikes use the dedicated bike lane (非机动车道) where present. Riding against traffic direction is common practice in China but significantly increases accident risk.

**Mobile while cycling:** Do not look at your phone while cycling in Chinese traffic; the traffic density and unpredictability requires full attention.

**Lock theft:** When not actively using the bike, always lock it to end the rental — bikes left running accumulate charges, and an unlocked running bike may be used by someone else at your expense.

*Bike sharing transformed Chinese cities — the first infrastructure innovation since the metro to make urban mobility both cheap and spontaneous. For visitors, it offers something irreplaceable: the ability to stop immediately when something interesting appears, to turn into an alley on impulse, and to cover more ground at walking-information density than any vehicle allows.*
