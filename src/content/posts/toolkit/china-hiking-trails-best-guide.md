China's terrain is jaw-dropping in its variety. Within a single country, you can hike through subtropical rainforest, high-altitude Tibetan plateau grassland, Gobi desert canyons, granite mountain peaks, and densely forested karst country. The trail infrastructure varies enormously — from stone-paved circuits with guesthouses every two hours to genuine wilderness routes where you might not see another soul for days.

This guide covers the best hiking trails in China organized by region and difficulty, with honest assessments of what you'll encounter, what you need to bring, and how to get there.

## Yunnan: China's Hiking Capital

### Tiger Leaping Gorge (虎跳峡, Hǔ Tiào Xiá)

The Tiger Leaping Gorge trek is China's most famous multi-day hike and deserves every bit of its reputation. The high trail — as distinct from the tourist road along the gorge floor — takes you along narrow paths carved into cliffs above the Jinsha River (upper Yangtze), with the Jade Dragon Snow Mountain on one side and Haba Snow Mountain on the other.

**Key details:**
- **Distance:** ~30km over 2 days (most popular format)
- **Difficulty:** Moderate-hard; several steep ascents and descents, highest point ~2,900m
- **Best season:** April-June and September-October; July-August is rainy season
- **Altitude sickness risk:** Low to moderate — start point (Qiaotou) is at ~1,800m; max elevation ~2,900m
- **Entry fee:** ¥65 (~$9) gorge entry
- **Guesthouses on trail:** Yes — Halfway Lodge and others offer basic dorms (¥40–¥80/night) and meals

**The route:** Most hikers start at Qiaotou village and follow the high trail clockwise, spending the night at one of several guesthouses before descending to the ferry point and catching transport back. The "28 Bends" ascent on day one is brutal but rewarding.

**Getting there:** From Lijiang, buses run to Qiaotou (2 hours, ¥35). From Shangri-La, similar duration in the opposite direction.

**Tip:** Book guesthouses in advance during peak seasons — Halfway Lodge fills up, and turning back in the dark is not an option.

### Meili Snow Mountain Circuit (梅里雪山)

The Meili (or Kawa Karpo) range is sacred to Tibetan Buddhism — no one has ever summited the highest peak, as all mountaineering attempts have been banned since a fatal Japanese expedition in 1991. The outer kora (circumambulation) circuit takes 12-16 days and passes through remote Yunnan-Tibet border country.

**Key details:**
- **Distance:** ~240km full outer kora
- **Difficulty:** Very hard; high altitude (up to 4,800m), remote, requires guide
- **Best season:** October-November, April-May
- **Permits:** Tibet Travel Permit required for portions crossing into Tibet
- **Guided tours:** Strongly recommended; several Deqin-based agencies offer full support, ¥5,000–¥15,000 per person depending on group size and support level

For those not ready for the full circuit, shorter day hikes and overnight trips around Mingyong Glacier and Yubeng village (the pilgrimage base) are spectacular and more accessible.

## Sichuan: Mountains and Plateaus

### Daocheng Yading Circuit (稻城亚丁)

The three sacred peaks of Yading — Chenrezig (Xiannairi), Jambeyang (Yangmaiyong), and Chanadorje (Xianuoduoji) — form one of China's most spectacular high-altitude landscapes. The main circuit connects the peaks via turquoise glacial lakes.

**Key details:**
- **Distance:** 30-60km depending on variant (day trip or 2-3 day)
- **Difficulty:** Moderate at lower elevations; hard at Milk Lake and Five-Color Lake (4,600m+)
- **Best season:** September-October (peak autumn colors), May-June
- **Entry + eco-bus:** ¥490 scenic area entry + eco-bus, required for access
- **Altitude warning:** Serious altitude sickness risk — acclimatize in Daocheng (3,700m) for 1-2 days before hiking

The sunrise on Chenrezig peak from the Five-Color Lake trail is one of the most memorable moments I've experienced in China — worth every meter of the climb.

### Siguniang Mountain (四姑娘山)

Four peaks ranging from 5,025m to 6,250m, surrounded by valleys with excellent day hiking and multi-day options that don't require technical mountaineering skills. The Shuangqiao Valley and Changping Valley offer beautiful trails through meadows and alpine forest with the peaks as backdrop.

**Key details:**
- **Day hikes:** Shuangqiao Valley entry ¥90; Changping Valley ¥110
- **Multi-day:** Rilong Town serves as a comfortable base
- **Best season:** May-October
- **Getting there:** Chengdu to Rilong ~5 hours by bus or hired car

## Huangshan and Eastern China

### Huangshan Complete Circuit (黄山, Yellow Mountain)

Huangshan in Anhui Province is one of China's most photographed landscapes — dramatically contorted pines clinging to granite peaks above clouds. The full circuit connecting the western and eastern cable car systems, with a night spent at a mountain summit hotel, takes 1-2 days.

**Key details:**
- **Distance:** Main circuit ~15-20km
- **Difficulty:** Moderate; well-paved stone steps, significant elevation change (~700m)
- **Entry fee:** ¥190 per person; cable cars ¥80-90 each way
- **Summit hotels:** ¥800–¥2,500/night (expensive for what you get, but the sunrise is worth it)
- **Best season:** Spring (April-May) and autumn (October-November); avoid Chinese public holidays when the mountain is extremely crowded

For serious hikers, the route from Tangkou at the base up through the Front Mountain (前山) to the summit, without using cable cars, is a full-day 15km vertical challenge.

### Taishan (泰山)

The most historically significant mountain in China — emperors performed ritual sacrifices here, Confucius climbed it and reflected on life, and the climb itself is considered a spiritual act. The main hiking route from Zhongtian Gate to the South Gate of Heaven involves ~6,000 stone steps.

**Key details:**
- **Distance:** 4.5km from Zhongtian Gate to summit (main route); full route from base is ~10km
- **Entry:** ¥125 per person
- **Cable car:** ¥100 up, ¥80 down (optional)
- **Best time:** Sunrise from summit — stay overnight in summit guesthouses (~¥300-500/night, book in advance)

## The Great Wall: Hidden Hiking Routes

Most visitors see the restored sections at Mutianyu or Badaling, which are more like paved promenades than hikes. The real Great Wall hiking experience is found on unrestored "wild" sections.

### Jiankou to Mutianyu

One of the most recommended Great Wall hikes for adventurous visitors. Jiankou is dramatic, steep, and genuinely dangerous in sections — crumbling towers, knife-edge ridges, and no guardrails. The trail connects to Mutianyu's restored section where you can take a toboggan down.

**Key details:**
- **Distance:** ~8km one way (Jiankou to Mutianyu)
- **Difficulty:** Hard; steep scrambles, unstable footing in sections
- **No entry fee** at Jiankou (officially unmanaged)
- **Guide recommended:** Local guides from Xizhazi village can be hired for ¥200-400
- **Getting there:** Bus from Beijing to Huairou, then local bus or taxi to Jiankou

### Gubeikou to Jinshanling

A gentler but equally beautiful unrestored-to-restored wall hike on the eastern side of Beijing.

**Distance:** ~14km; **Difficulty:** Moderate; **Entry:** ¥65 at Jinshanling section

## Xinjiang: Central Asian Landscapes

### Kalajun Grassland to Nalati Circuit (中天山)

In the Ili River valley of western Xinjiang, vast alpine grasslands backed by snow-capped Tianshan peaks offer some of China's most dramatic scenery at accessible altitudes (2,000-3,000m). Less developed than other regions, with genuine wilderness character.

**Key details:**
- **Best season:** June-August
- **Difficulty:** Moderate; mostly open grassland terrain
- **Permits:** Xinjiang travel is straightforward but check current travel advisories before planning
- **Base:** Narat (Nalati) Scenic Area or Kalajun Village

### Karakul Lake Area (喀拉库勒湖)

Near Kashgar on the Karakoram Highway, Karakul Lake sits at 3,600m with the 7,546m Muztagh Ata peak reflected in its waters. Day hikes around the lake and to the base of the scree fields are accessible from the yurt guesthouses on the shore.

**Key details:**
- Entry fee: ¥60
- Overnight yurt stays: ¥150-300 per person
- Getting there: Kashgar to Karakul, 3 hours by bus

## Tibet: The Roof of the World

### Mount Kailash Kora (冈仁波齐转山)

The most sacred pilgrimage circuit in Asia circumambulates Mount Kailash (6,638m), considered holy in Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Bon. The 52km circuit takes most people 3 days, crossing the Drolma Pass at 5,636m on day two.

**Key details:**
- **Altitude warning:** This is the hardest hike in this guide. The starting point at Darchen is 4,560m; the pass is 5,636m. Altitude sickness risk is very high — acclimatize in Lhasa (3,650m) for at least 3-5 days first
- **Permits:** Tibet Travel Permit + Alien Travel Permit + Mount Kailash area permit required. These must be arranged through a registered Tibet travel agency — independent travel to Tibet is not permitted for foreign visitors
- **Best season:** May-September; June-August most stable weather
- **Agency costs:** All-inclusive organized tours ¥6,000–¥15,000 per person depending on group size and comfort level

### Ganden to Samye Trek (甘丹-桑耶)

A 4-5 day high-altitude trek between two of Tibet's most important monasteries, crossing passes at 5,200m through remote countryside with stunning glacial scenery.

**Requirements:** Same permit requirements as Kailash. Less visited than Kailash, making it a more wilderness-like experience.

## Gear Essentials for China Hiking

Chinese mountains vary enormously in infrastructure and difficulty. Here's a realistic packing guide:

**Essential for all hikes:**
- Trekking boots (not trail runners for multi-day with heavy loads)
- Rain jacket and warm layers (weather changes rapidly at altitude)
- Sun protection — UV at high altitude is intense
- Headlamp (summit sunrises require early starts)
- Water purification tablets or filter (remote trails)
- Cash — remote guesthouses don't have card machines

**For high-altitude hikes (3,000m+):**
- Altitude sickness medication (Diamox/acetazolamide — consult your doctor before travel)
- Warm gloves and hat
- Trekking poles (genuinely useful on steep descents)

**For Tibet/Xinjiang:**
- Extra travel documents and permit copies
- Satellite communicator (for truly remote areas)

## Useful Apps and Resources

- **Baidu Maps (百度地图):** Works in China where Google Maps doesn't; good for trail access roads
- **2Bulu / Komoot:** Trail maps that work offline
- **Mafengwo (马蜂窝):** Chinese-language travel forum with detailed trail reports (use Google Translate)
- **China Hiking (中国登山协会):** Chinese Mountaineering Association for permit information

## Altitude Sickness: Know Before You Go

Any hike above 2,500m carries some altitude sickness risk, and China has many popular trails well above 3,000m. The condition ranges from mild headaches and nausea to potentially life-threatening High Altitude Pulmonary Edema (HAPE).

**Basic rules:**
- Ascend gradually — don't fly from sea level to a 3,700m base camp immediately
- Stay hydrated
- Descend if symptoms are severe (confusion, inability to walk straight, persistent cough with frothy sputum)
- Consider Diamox for known high-altitude routes — discuss with a doctor

China's hiking landscape is extraordinary, and the variety available in a single country is genuinely unparalleled. Whether you're looking for a well-organized half-day mountain circuit with guardrails and cable cars, or a week-long wilderness traverse with a guide, permits, and your sleeping bag, you'll find something here that matches your ambition and ability.
