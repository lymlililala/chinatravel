Southern China is a paradise for food lovers, where every region boasts its own unique culinary traditions. This guide takes you on a flavorful journey through Guangdong and Guangxi, from the bustling dim sum houses of Guangzhou to the rustic specialties of small towns like Kaiping, and even ventures into the fiery pickles of Hunan and the tangy sour soups of Guizhou. Whether you're a seasoned foodie or a curious traveler, you'll discover the rich tapestry of tastes that define this part of China.

## Table of contents

## Why Southern China is a Food Lover's Dream

Southern China's cuisine is as diverse as its landscapes. Guangdong is famous for its delicate Cantonese dishes, while Guangxi offers bold, sour flavors influenced by its mountainous terrain. The region's food culture is deeply rooted in local ingredients, from fresh river fish to aromatic herbs, and each city has its own signature dishes. For travelers, this means endless opportunities to taste authentic, regional specialties that rarely make it to international menus.

## Top Destinations for a Food Tour

![Baomo Garden, Guangdong, Guangzhou](/img/destinations/guangdong-guangxi-food-tour/1.webp)

### Guangzhou: The Heart of Cantonese Cuisine

Guangzhou, the capital of Guangdong, is a must-visit for any food tour. Start your day with **dim sum** (点心) at a traditional tea house, where you can savor delicate dumplings, steamed buns, and rice rolls. Don't miss classics like **har gow** (shrimp dumplings) and **siu mai** (pork and shrimp dumplings). For a more adventurous meal, try **chicken feet** (凤爪) or **turnip cake** (萝卜糕).

### Kaiping: A Hidden Gem of Wuyi Cuisine

Kaiping (开平), a county-level city in Jiangmen, is a treasure trove of local flavors. Known for its **Wuyi cuisine** (五邑菜), which combines ingredients from five nearby districts, Kaiping offers dishes like **claypot rice with eel** (黄鳝饭) and **Magan goose** (马冈鹅). The city is also famous for its **firewood porridge** (柴火粥), cooked over a traditional wood stove for a smoky flavor.

### Changsha: The Spicy Soul of Hunan

While technically in Hunan, Changsha's food scene is a must-try for those exploring southern China. The city is known for its **Xiangxi pickles** (湘西泡菜), a tangy, spicy mix of fermented vegetables that locals eat as a meal. These pickles are often served with a generous drizzle of chili oil and can include everything from radishes to fried blood tofu.

### Guizhou: Sour and Spicy Delights

Guizhou's cuisine is characterized by its **red sour soup** (红酸汤), a fermented tomato-based broth that's both tangy and spicy. It's commonly used as a hot pot base or to braise meats and vegetables. Pair it with a **dip** (蘸水) made from roasted chili flakes and Sichuan pepper for an extra kick.

## Must-Try Dishes

### Dim Sum (Guangzhou)

- **Har Gow**: Translucent shrimp dumplings with a delicate wrapper.
- **Siu Mai**: Open-topped pork and shrimp dumplings.
- **Char Siu Bao**: Steamed buns filled with sweet barbecue pork.

### Wuyi Cuisine (Kaiping)

- **Claypot Rice with Eel**: Rice cooked with shredded eel in a clay pot, resulting in a crispy bottom and fragrant top.
- **Magan Goose**: A local breed of goose, prepared roasted or braised.
- **Firewood Porridge**: Slow-cooked rice porridge with hand-made fish balls.

### Xiangxi Pickles (Changsha)

- **Pickled Radish**: Tangy and crunchy, often with a hint of sweetness.
- **Fried Blood Tofu**: A chewy, savory treat made from pig's blood and sticky rice.
- **Spicy Chili Oil**: The soul of the dish, made from local chilies and spices.

### Red Sour Soup (Guizhou)

- **Sour Soup Hot Pot**: A bubbling pot of tangy broth with beef, tofu, and vegetables.
- **Dip**: A mixture of roasted chili, garlic, and cilantro, perfect for dipping.

## Suggested Itinerary: 7-Day Food Tour

![Xingping, Guangxi, Yangshuo](/img/destinations/guangdong-guangxi-food-tour/2.webp)

### Day 1-2: Guangzhou
- **Morning**: Visit a traditional dim sum house like **Dian Dou De** (点都德) or **Tao Tao Ju** (陶陶居).
- **Afternoon**: Explore the **Shangxiajiu Pedestrian Street** (上下九步行街) for street food like **rice noodle rolls** (肠粉) and **stuffed tofu** (酿豆腐).
- **Evening**: Try **Cantonese roast goose** at a local restaurant.

### Day 3-4: Kaiping
- **Morning**: Head to **Hongxing Fish Ball Firewood Porridge** (宏兴鲮鱼丸柴火粥) for a traditional breakfast.
- **Afternoon**: Visit the **Kaiping Diaolou** (开平碉楼), UNESCO World Heritage sites, and try **claypot rice with eel** at **Xinghua** (兴华).
- **Evening**: Enjoy **Magan goose** at **Jintong Building** (金彤楼).

### Day 5-6: Changsha
- **Morning**: Start with a bowl of **rice noodles** (米粉) at a local shop.
- **Afternoon**: Visit **Jinhua Pickles** (金花泡菜) for authentic Xiangxi pickles.
- **Evening**: Try **stinky tofu** (臭豆腐) and **spicy crayfish** (口味虾).

### Day 7: Guizhou
- **All day**: Take a cooking class to learn how to make **red sour soup** and **dip**. Visit a local market for fresh ingredients.

## Getting There & Around

- **Guangzhou**: Fly into **Guangzhou Baiyun International Airport** (CAN). The city has an extensive metro system (Alipay/WeChat Pay accepted for tickets).
- **Kaiping**: Take a high-speed train from Guangzhou South Station to **Kaiping South Station** (about 1 hour). Local buses and taxis are available.
- **Changsha**: Fly into **Changsha Huanghua International Airport** (CSX) or take a high-speed train from Guangzhou (about 2.5 hours).
- **Guizhou**: Fly into **Guiyang Longdongbao International Airport** (KWE) or take a train from Changsha (about 3 hours).

## Where to Stay

- **Guangzhou**: Stay in the **Liwan District** (荔湾区) for easy access to food streets.
- **Kaiping**: Choose a hotel near **Chikan Ancient Town** (赤坎古镇) for a historic vibe.
- **Changsha**: Look for accommodations near **Wuyi Square** (五一广场) for nightlife and food.
- **Guizhou**: Stay in **Kaili** (凯里) for easy access to sour soup restaurants.

## Best Time to Visit

![Detianda Waterfall Scenery, Guangxi](/img/destinations/guangdong-guangxi-food-tour/3.webp)

The best time for a food tour is **spring (March-May)** and **autumn (September-November)**, when the weather is mild and ingredients are fresh. Summer can be hot and humid, but it's also the season for tropical fruits and refreshing pickles.

## Practical Tips

- **Mobile Payment**: Alipay and WeChat Pay are widely accepted. Carry some cash for small stalls.
- **Language**: Mandarin is common, but in rural areas, local dialects are spoken. Learn a few phrases like "how much?" (多少钱) and "delicious" (好吃).
- **Etiquette**: In Guangdong, it's polite to tap the table to thank someone for pouring tea. In Hunan and Guizhou, don't be shy about asking for less spice if you can't handle the heat.
- **Visa**: Most travelers need a tourist visa (L-visa). Check the latest requirements before travel.

## FAQ

**Q: Is it safe to eat street food in southern China?**
A: Yes, street food is generally safe if you choose busy stalls with high turnover. Look for places where locals eat.

**Q: Can I use credit cards?**
A: Credit cards are accepted in upscale restaurants and hotels, but for street food and small shops, you'll need cash or mobile payment.

**Q: How spicy is Hunan food?**
A: Very spicy! If you have a low tolerance, ask for "little spice" (少辣) or "no spice" (不辣).

**Q: What's the best way to learn about local food?**
A: Join a food tour or take a cooking class. Many cities offer half-day tours focused on street food.

**Q: Are there vegetarian options?**
A: Yes, especially in Guangdong where vegetable dishes are common. In Hunan and Guizhou, ask for no meat (不要肉).

**Q: What should I drink with the food?**
A: Try local teas like **Tieguanyin** (铁观音) in Guangdong or **osmanthus tea** (桂花茶) in Guizhou. Beer is also popular.

**Q: How do I find hidden food gems?**
A: Use food apps like **Dianping** (大众点评) or ask locals for recommendations. Many small towns have unmarked but excellent restaurants.
