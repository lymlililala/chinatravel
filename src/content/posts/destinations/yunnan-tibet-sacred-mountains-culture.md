From the holy peak of Mount Kailash (冈仁波齐, Gāngrén Bōqí) in Tibet to the hidden valleys of Yunnan's Shangri-La (香格里拉, Xiānggélǐlā), this guide takes you deep into the heart of Tibetan Buddhism and high-altitude wilderness. Whether you're a seasoned trekker seeking a multi-day pilgrimage or a cultural traveller craving authentic homestay experiences, the landscapes of Yunnan and Tibet offer an unforgettable journey. This article covers the best routes, practical logistics, and insider tips for navigating these sacred lands.

## Table of contents

## Top Sacred Mountains and Regions

### Mount Kailash (Gang Rinpoche)
Mount Kailash is the most sacred peak in Tibetan Buddhism, Hinduism, Jainism, and Bon. Located in Ngari Prefecture, Tibet, it stands at 6,638 m (21,778 ft) and is never climbed — pilgrims instead perform a 52 km (32 mi) **kora** (circumambulation) around the base. The full circuit typically takes 2-3 days, reaching an elevation of 5,630 m (18,471 ft) at the Dolma La pass. The best time is May to October, with July-September being the warmest.

### Meili Snow Mountain (Kawagebo)
In Yunnan's Diqing Prefecture, Meili Snow Mountain (梅里雪山, Méilǐ Xuěshān) is a sacred massif whose highest peak, Kawagebo (6,740 m / 22,112 ft), has never been summited. The **Yubeng Village** (雨崩村, Yǔbēng Cūn) trek offers close-up views and is a popular multi-day hike. The best season is October to November for clear skies, or May-June for rhododendron blooms.

### Geladandong and Amne Machin
Other notable sacred peaks include Geladandong (the source of the Yangtze River) and Amne Machin in Qinghai. These are less accessible but equally revered.

## Suggested Itineraries

### 10-Day Yunnan & Tibet Sacred Mountains Trek
This itinerary combines the cultural richness of Shangri-La with the spiritual power of Kailash. It's designed for experienced trekkers comfortable at altitudes above 4,000 m.

**Day 1-2: Arrival in Shangri-La (3,300 m)**
- Fly into Diqing Shangri-La Airport (DIG). Take it easy to acclimatise.
- Visit **Songzanlin Monastery** (松赞林寺, Sōngzànlín Sì), the largest Tibetan Buddhist monastery in Yunnan.
- Stay at **Saido Nomad** (赛朵游牧), a boutique homestay on the shores of Napahai Lake. The staff provides oxygen monitors and glucose upon arrival — a lifesaver for first-timers.

**Day 3: Napahai & Local Culture**
- Explore **Napahai Lake** (纳帕海, Nàpà Hǎi), a seasonal wetland that transforms from a lush meadow in summer to a bird-filled lake in winter.
- Participate in a **sang offering** (煨桑, wēi sāng) ceremony and learn to make butter tea. The homestay offers free Tibetan costume rental and guided photography.

**Day 4: Fly to Lhasa (3,650 m)**
- Morning flight to Lhasa (1.5 hours). Rest and acclimatise.
- Visit **Potala Palace** (布达拉宫, Bùdálā Gōng) and **Jokhang Temple** (大昭寺, Dàzhāo Sì).

**Day 5: Train to Shigatse**
- Take the Lhasa-Shigatse railway (2 hours, ¥64). Explore **Tashilhunpo Monastery** (扎什伦布寺, Zhāshílúnbù Sì).

**Day 6-8: Kailash Kora (3 days)**
- Bus from Shigatse to Darchen (塔尔钦, Tǎ'ěrqìn) — the base town (12 hours, ¥397). Buy your entry ticket (valid for 3 days) at a designated hotel; daily quota is 2,000 (500 for foreigners).
- **Day 6**: Start the kora from Darchen, trek to Dirapuk Monastery (5,210 m), 20 km, 8-10 hours.
- **Day 7**: Cross Dolma La pass (5,630 m) and descend to Zutulpuk Monastery (4,800 m), 18 km, 8-10 hours.
- **Day 8**: Return to Darchen, 12 km, 3-4 hours. Take bus back to Shigatse.

**Day 9-10: Return to Lhasa & Depart**
- Rest, visit **Sera Monastery** (色拉寺, Sèlā Sì) for the monks' debate at 3 PM.
- Depart from Lhasa.

### 5-Day Yubeng Village Trek (Alternative)
For those with less time or who prefer Yunnan's milder climate:
- **Day 1**: Fly to Shangri-La, drive to Feilai Temple (飞来寺, Fēilái Sì) for sunrise views of Meili Snow Mountain.
- **Day 2**: Trek from Xidang (西当, Xīdāng) to Yubeng Upper Village (3,200 m), 6-8 hours.
- **Day 3**: Hike to the **Ice Lake** (冰湖, Bīng Hú), 5-6 hours round trip.
- **Day 4**: Visit the **Sacred Waterfall** (神瀑, Shén Pù) and descend to Niru Village (尼汝, Nírú).
- **Day 5**: Return to Shangri-La.

## Getting There & Around

### To Shangri-La
- **By air**: Direct flights from major Chinese cities (Beijing, Shanghai, Chengdu) to Diqing Shangri-La Airport (DIG).
- **By train**: The Lijiang-Shangri-La railway opened in 2023; from Kunming, it's about 4 hours. The station is a 10-minute drive from Napahai.

### To Lhasa & Kailash
- **By air**: Fly into Lhasa Gonggar Airport (LXA) from Beijing, Chengdu, or Kathmandu.
- **By train**: The Qinghai-Tibet Railway connects Xining to Lhasa (20 hours, ¥200+).
- **To Darchen**: From Lhasa, take a bus or flight to Shigatse, then a long-distance bus to Darchen (12 hours, ¥397). Alternatively, fly to Ali Kunsha Airport (NGQ) and drive 3 hours.

### Getting Around
- **Taxis & ride-hailing**: DiDi operates in Shangri-La and Lhasa. In remote areas, negotiate with local drivers.
- **Tours**: For Kailash, join a group or hire a private driver (¥1,500-2,500 per day).
- **Mobile payment**: Alipay and WeChat Pay are widely accepted in cities and larger towns. Carry some cash for remote villages and bus tickets.

## Where to Stay

### Luxury/Eco-Lodges
- **Saido Nomad** (赛朵游牧): Located on Napahai Lake, this 34-room property blends Tibetan architecture with modern comforts. Rooms have underfloor heating, oxygen supply, and Le Labo toiletries. Rates from ¥1,200/night. Book via WeChat or travel platforms.
- **Songtsam Retreats**: A chain of boutique hotels across Yunnan and Tibet, offering cultural immersion and high-altitude wellness.

### Mid-Range
- **Tibet Homestays**: In Darchen, guesthouses like **Kailash Guesthouse** offer basic rooms (¥200-400/night).
- **Yubeng Village**: Family-run lodges (¥150-300/night) with shared bathrooms.

### Budget
- **Hostels**: In Lhasa, **Potala Youth Hostel** (¥60-100/bed).
- **Camping**: On the Kailash kora, tea houses provide dorm beds (¥100-300/night). Bring a sleeping bag.

## Food & Drink

Tibetan cuisine is hearty and warming. Must-tries:
- **Tsampa** (roasted barley flour) and **butter tea** (酥油茶, sūyóu chá)
- **Yak meat** (牦牛肉, máoniú ròu) — grilled, in soup, or dried as jerky
- **Momo** (dumplings) filled with yak or vegetables
- **Highland barley wine** (青稞酒, qīngkē jiǔ) — mild and sweet

At Saido Nomad, the restaurant serves Tibetan-style teppanyaki and hotpot featuring local mushrooms and yak. Breakfast runs from 8 AM to 1 PM — perfect for lazy mornings.

## Practical Tips

### Altitude Sickness
- Ascend gradually: spend 2 days in Lhasa or Shangri-La before going higher.
- Stay hydrated, avoid alcohol, and consider **Diamox** (acetazolamide) after consulting a doctor.
- Most hotels in Shangri-La (e.g., Saido Nomad) provide oxygen and pulse oximeters.

### Permits & Visas
- **China visa**: Required for most nationalities. Apply at your local Chinese embassy.
- **Tibet Travel Permit**: Needed for Lhasa and beyond. Book through a registered tour agency (budget ¥200-400).
- **Aliens' Travel Permit**: Required for Ngari (Kailash). Your agency can arrange it.
- **144-hour transit visa**: Available in Shanghai, Beijing, and other cities; does not cover Tibet.

### What to Pack
- **Trekking**: Waterproof boots, gaiters, trekking poles, warm layers (down jacket), sun hat, sunglasses, SPF 50+ sunscreen.
- **Altitude**: Lip balm, moisturiser, hydration tablets.
- **Electronics**: Power bank (limited charging on the kora); Chinese SIM card (China Mobile has best coverage in remote areas).
- **Documents**: Passport, visa, permits, travel insurance (mandatory for some treks).

### Etiquette
- **Temples**: Remove shoes, walk clockwise, avoid pointing at statues or monks.
- **Photography**: Ask permission before photographing people or inside monasteries.
- **Gift-giving**: Offer a **khata** (white scarf) as a sign of respect.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need a guide for the Kailash kora?**
A: Not strictly, but highly recommended for first-timers. The route is well-marked and has tea houses every 2-3 km, but altitude and weather can be challenging. Guides cost ¥300-500/day.

**Q: Can I use my credit card in remote areas?**
A: Rarely. Alipay and WeChat Pay are accepted in towns, but bring enough cash (¥1,000-2,000) for the kora and small villages.

**Q: Is it safe to trek alone?**
A: The Kailash kora is busy in peak season (July-August), so solo is fine. For remote treks like Yubeng or Geladandong, join a group or hire a guide.

**Q: What if I get altitude sickness on the kora?**
A: Every tea house has basic first aid; emergency evacuation by horse or vehicle is available (¥500-1,000). Carry Diamox and a pulse oximeter.

**Q: Can I do the kora in one day?**
A: Yes, but it's a 12-16 hour push. Most people take 2-3 days to acclimatise and enjoy the scenery. Start early (3-4 AM) and pack light.

**Q: Are there ATMs in Darchen?**
A: Yes, a few ATMs accept foreign cards, but they may run out of cash. Bring sufficient RMB from Lhasa or Shigatse.
