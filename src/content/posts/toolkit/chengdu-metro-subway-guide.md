Chengdu's metro has expanded rapidly over the past decade and now covers the main tourist and residential areas reasonably well. It's clean, inexpensive, and straightforward to navigate with English signage throughout. That said, Chengdu is also a city where DiDi fills real gaps — particularly for reaching attractions like the Panda Base, which until recently required a taxi or bus, and for navigating between areas that aren't on the same line.

## Table of contents

## Fares and Payment

Chengdu Metro uses distance-based pricing:
- **¥2** for the first 4 km
- Additional distance charged in increments
- Most central journeys cost **¥2–¥5**

**Alipay** transit code works on Chengdu Metro — open Alipay, find the transit code feature (乘车码), select Chengdu, activate, and scan at turnstiles. This is the most convenient payment method for visitors already using Alipay.

**WeChat Pay** also has a transit code for Chengdu Metro with the same process.

**Physical T-Union Card** (全国交通一卡通): Available at metro service windows, ¥20 deposit plus load funds. Works on Chengdu buses and metro.

**Single journey tokens**: From machines at all stations, English interface available.

## Key Lines for Visitors

**Line 1** is Chengdu's north-south spine and the most important for tourists. Running through the historic center, key stops include:
- **Tianfu Square (天府广场)** — the central plaza, close to the Sichuan Provincial Museum and Chengdu Museum
- **Chunxi Road (春熙路)** — Chengdu's main shopping district, full of food and fashion
- **Jinli** (accessible via a short walk from nearby stations)

**Line 2** connects the western residential areas to the center and Shuangliu Airport via Line 10.

**Line 3** reaches some outer residential and business areas:
- Useful for accessing the Jinli Ancient Street area more directly
- Also covers parts of southern Chengdu

**Line 4** (east-west connection) and **Line 9** (southeastern arc) are more residential but worth knowing for certain neighborhoods.

**Line 10** connects to **Shuangliu International Airport (CTU)** — the key airport line. From Shuangliu Airport Station, trains run to the city center (Taipingyuan station, then transfer to Line 3 for the central areas).

## Getting to the Giant Panda Base

This is the number-one question on Chengdu metro searches, and the answer has improved significantly. **Line 3 extended northward** to a station specifically serving the Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding (成都大熊猫繁育研究基地). 

The current route: Take **Line 3** to **Panda Avenue Station (熊猫大道)** or the dedicated Panda Base station (check current line extensions, as Line 3 was still being extended as of late 2025). From the nearest metro station, it's either a short walk or a brief taxi/bus ride to the panda base entrance.

**Practical tip**: Book your panda base tickets online in advance (the official website or WeChat mini-program) — ticket numbers are capped and popular time slots sell out, especially on weekends.

**Early arrival is critical**: The pandas are most active in the morning (feeding times 8–10am). Take the metro or DiDi to arrive at opening time (8am). By early afternoon, most pandas are napping and the crowds are larger.

## Getting to Wenshu Monastery and the Old Center

**Wenshu Monastery** (文殊院): Line 1 to Wenshu Monastery station (文殊院站). The Buddhist temple and surrounding street food market are right outside the exit.

**Jinli Ancient Street and Wuhou Shrine**: These are close together in the south-center of the city. Take Line 3 to Gaosheng Bridge (高升桥) station or Line 1 to some nearby transfer points, then a 10–15 minute walk or short taxi.

**People's Park (人民公园)**: Line 2 or 4 to People's Park station (人民公园站). Chengdu's beloved park with its famous teahouse culture — locals play mahjong and sip tea here all afternoon.

## Getting to Sichuan University and the Wenshu District

Chengdu's university culture is part of its identity — Sichuan University is one of China's top institutions, and the area around it has good food and bookshops. Take Line 1 to Sichuan University (四川大学) station.

## Where the Metro Doesn't Reach Well

**Qingcheng Mountain** (2 hours by car/bus from Chengdu): Take a train from Chengdu station or tourist buses — the metro doesn't extend this far west.

**Jinli-area restaurants** in the late evening: The area is accessible but parking and DiDi pickups around the hutong-style streets can be tricky. The metro is fine for arriving; for late-night departures after a long dinner, DiDi tends to work better.

**Sichuan University Jinjiang Campus and nearby areas**: Metro is close but not always at the door; a short taxi supplements nicely.

## Operating Hours and Frequency

Chengdu Metro operates from approximately **6:30am to 11:30pm** on most lines, with Line 10 running later for airport connections. Frequency during peak hours is every 3–5 minutes on busy lines, every 8–12 minutes off-peak.

## DiDi vs Metro in Chengdu

Chengdu traffic can be heavy, especially at rush hour on the Second and Third Ring Roads. For journeys along metro lines, the metro is faster and more predictable than a DiDi in traffic. For cross-city trips that require multiple metro transfers, DiDi can sometimes be faster and costs only ¥20–¥40.

**Best strategy**: Use the metro as your default for attractions within the Ring Road area, DiDi for the Panda Base (if Line 3 hasn't reached it yet), Wuhou Shrine area, and anywhere that requires two or more transfers.
