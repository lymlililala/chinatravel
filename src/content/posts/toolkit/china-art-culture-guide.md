The popular image of China as a cultural monolith is completely at odds with the reality. China has one of the world's most dynamic contemporary art scenes, driven by a generation of artists grappling with urbanisation, tradition, censorship, and global connectivity in ways that produce genuinely original work. The gallery districts in Beijing and Shanghai are legitimate world-class destinations for anyone who cares about contemporary art.

## Table of contents

## Beijing's 798 Art District

**798 Art District (798艺术区)** in northeast Beijing occupies a cluster of Bauhaus-style factory buildings originally constructed by East German engineers in the 1950s. In the early 2000s, artists moved in for the cheap industrial studio space; by 2010, it had become one of Asia's most significant contemporary art destinations. It remains that now, though the galleries have become more commercial and the real avant-garde has moved further east to Caochangdi and Songzhuang.

**What's worth seeing:**
- **Pace Gallery Beijing** — the prestigious New York gallery's Beijing outpost, regularly showing major international and Chinese artists
- **Ullens Center for Contemporary Art (UCCA)** — the anchor institution of 798, with serious exhibition programming and an excellent bookshop; admission ¥60-80 depending on exhibition
- **Galleria Continua** — Italian gallery with strong Chinese artist representation
- **Tang Contemporary Art** — one of the most important commercial galleries for Chinese contemporary art

The street-level outdoor sculpture and mural works throughout 798 are also excellent and free to walk around. The best time to visit is weekday mornings — weekends are significantly crowded with day-tripping families and photo-seekers rather than serious art visitors.

**Gallery hours:** Most 798 galleries are open 10:00-18:00, closed Monday. Admission to individual commercial galleries is free; institutional spaces like UCCA charge entry.

**Getting there:** Metro Line 14 to Wangjing South, then 15-minute walk or taxi (¥15-20 from Sanlitun area).

## Shanghai's Contemporary Art Scene

Shanghai has the strongest institutional art infrastructure in China, anchored by three major venues and a growing cluster of independent gallery districts.

### Power Station of Art (当代艺术博物馆, PSA)

Converted from a power station on the waterfront, the PSA is Shanghai's flagship public contemporary art museum and the only public contemporary art museum in mainland China. The building is spectacular — a massive industrial space with a 165m chimney that now serves as an architectural feature.

The PSA hosts major international exhibition loans (they've shown Pompidou Centre and Tate Modern exhibitions) as well as Chinese contemporary programming. Admission: ¥0 (free) for the permanent collection; temporary exhibitions ¥30-80. Open Tuesday-Sunday 11:00-19:00.

Getting there: Metro Line 4 to Luban Road station.

### M50 Creative Park (莫干山路50号)

M50 on Moganshan Road is Shanghai's equivalent of 798 — a complex of former textile factory buildings occupied by about 100 studios and galleries. Less slick than 798 but arguably more authentic; many working artists still have studios here.

**Key galleries at M50:**
- **ShanghART Gallery** — one of the oldest contemporary art galleries in China; major Chinese artist roster
- **Aike Dellarco** — shows experimental and conceptual work
- **Vanguard Gallery** — emerging Chinese artists

M50 is free to enter; individual galleries are free unless a ticketed event is running. Hours vary but roughly 10:00-18:00 Tuesday-Sunday.

### West Bund Cultural Corridor

The **West Bund** (西岸) area on the south bank of the Huangpu River has rapidly developed into Shanghai's premier cultural district — with significant investment producing a cluster of major cultural institutions within walking distance of each other:

- **Long Museum West Bund (龙美术馆西岸馆)** — one of China's largest and most impressive private art museums, housed in a brutalist waterfront building; admission ¥100; major international and Chinese art
- **Yuz Museum (余德耀美术馆)** — another private museum with exceptional international contemporary art; admission ¥100
- **Centre Pompidou x West Bund Museum Project** — joint programming with the Paris institution; world-class international contemporary art exhibitions

Walking the West Bund waterfront on a weekend afternoon and seeing three world-class exhibitions in an afternoon is a legitimate rival to anything in London or New York.

## Chengdu's Emerging Art Scene

Chengdu has developed rapidly as a creative centre, driven partly by its tech sector and partly by the city's historical culture of leisure and aesthetics.

**Key institutions:**
- **Chengdu Contemporary Art Center (成都当代艺术中心):** The main institutional venue for major contemporary shows; housed in a striking new building in the Tianfu New Area
- **A4 Arts Foundation:** Excellent mid-size institution focused on Chinese contemporary art; ¥0-50 depending on show
- **Anren Minsheng Museum (安仁民生现代美术馆):** About 40km from Chengdu in the Anren museum town — an extraordinary experiment in rural cultural tourism with multiple art and history museums; entry to the complex ¥80-150

## Key Chinese Artists to Know

Understanding a few names before visiting helps contextualize what you see:

- **Ai Weiwei:** The internationally best-known Chinese contemporary artist; political, conceptual, frequently provokes the Chinese state. His work appears in international collections everywhere.
- **Cai Guo-Qiang (蔡国强):** Known for gunpowder drawings and fireworks-based installations; major museum commissions globally
- **Liu Wei (刘炜):** Painter; raw, physically intense figurative work
- **Zeng Fanzhi (曾梵志):** Figurative painter known for "Mask" series; among the highest auction prices for contemporary Chinese art
- **Yang Fudong (杨福东):** Multi-channel video installation; shown at Venice Biennale multiple times

## Art Fairs and Events

**Art021 Shanghai Contemporary Art Fair** (November): One of Asia's most important commercial art fairs. Galleries from around the world show in the Shanghai Exhibition Centre. Open to the public on the final weekend; ¥80 entry.

**West Bund Art & Design Fair** (November, same week as Art021): Runs simultaneously, creating a concentrated week of major art events. The two fairs combined make Shanghai in November the best time for art tourism.

**Beijing Art Week** (autumn): Various gallery openings and institutional events across 798 and Caochangdi coincide in September-October.

## Practical Tips

Most commercial galleries in Beijing and Shanghai have at least some English wall text for major exhibitions. Institutional museums (UCCA, PSA, Long Museum) have comprehensive English interpretation.

Photography: Most Chinese galleries allow photography without flash for personal use. No tripods in most spaces. Check for "禁止拍照" (no photography) signs.

Purchasing: Chinese commercial galleries sell work through the gallery directly. Prices are generally not displayed on the walls (ask at reception). For collectors, many major galleries have online catalogues and can ship internationally.

The contemporary art scene in China is evolving extremely quickly — new institutions, new artists, and significant government and private investment mean what's excellent today will be different in three years. The best way to navigate it is to check the programming calendars of UCCA, PSA, and Long Museum before your trip and plan around what's showing.
