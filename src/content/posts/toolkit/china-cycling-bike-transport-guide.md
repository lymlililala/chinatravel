Cycling in China operates on two different scales that don't overlap much. On one end: the ubiquitous dockless bike-share systems covering every city, enabling quick point-to-point rides for ¥1–¥2. On the other: multi-day cycling tours through some of the most dramatic landscapes on the planet — the Tibetan plateau, the Yunnan-Guizhou border, the karst valleys around Yangshuo. Both are genuinely accessible to foreign visitors, though the setup requires a bit of preparation.

## Bike-Sharing Apps: The Urban Option

Three main operators dominate Chinese bike-share: **Hellobike (哈啰单车)**, **Meituan Bike (美团单车, formerly Mobike)**, and **DiDi Bike (青桔单车)**. You'll find their bikes — color-coded in yellow, orange/white, and green respectively — in virtually every major and mid-sized Chinese city.

**How they work**: Each system uses dockless GPS-tracked bikes. Scan the QR code on the bike's handlebars with the respective app, pay a small fee (¥1–¥2 per 30–60 minutes), and ride. Lock the bike anywhere when finished.

### Registering as a Foreigner

The good news is that all three apps have improved foreign access significantly:

**Hellobike**: Accessible through Alipay — if you have Alipay set up with your international payment details, Hellobike appears as a mini-program (小程序) within Alipay. No separate registration needed in many cases. This makes it the most accessible option for visitors who've set up Alipay.

**Meituan Bike**: Accessible through the main Meituan app. Requires WeChat Pay or Alipay linked as payment. Foreign phone numbers for registration have had mixed results — some international numbers work, others require Chinese verification. Try Alipay access first.

**DiDi Bike**: Integrated in the DiDi app. If you've set up DiDi for ride-hailing (see our DiDi guide), the bike option is in the same app.

**Deposit requirements**: Some apps require a refundable deposit (¥99–¥299) unless you have a high Sesame Credit (Alipay credit score). As a new foreign user, you may be asked for the deposit. It's refundable when you close the account or stop using the service.

### What You Get for Your ¥1–¥2

The bikes are heavy, single-speed (most models), with a basket. They're fine for urban riding on flat city streets — not suitable for hills or long distances. The electric assist versions (e-bikes, slightly higher price tier) are increasingly available and make urban cycling substantially more pleasant.

Lock them anywhere — bike share stations don't exist; you just leave the bike in a legal spot (not blocking doorways or pedestrian crossings, theoretically).

## Best Cities for Cycling

### Yangshuo — The Classic

**Yangshuo** in Guangxi province is China's best cycling destination for international visitors. The town sits in the middle of the Li River karst landscape, surrounded by rice paddies, rivers, and the pointed limestone mountains that look like a classical Chinese painting.

The **Yulong River valley** cycling route (15–25 km depending on how far you go) runs through this scenery on quiet country roads with minimal traffic. Rent a bike from any guesthouse or rental shop in Yangshuo (¥20–¥40/day for regular bikes, ¥80–¥150/day for good quality mountain bikes). No app required — these are traditional rental operations.

The route takes you past bamboo raft operators, small villages, old stone bridges, and rice fields. Most visitors do a 3–4 hour loop then return to town for late lunch.

### Chengdu — Flat City, Strong Cycling Culture

Chengdu is one of China's most bike-friendly major cities. It's mostly flat, has dedicated cycling lanes on many main roads, and a genuine cycling culture among residents. The **Jincheng Greenway** cycling path system extends through parks and along canals — a network of paths for leisure cycling.

Within the city, bike-share (all three apps operate here) is a practical transport option for short trips. The Chunxi Road/Jinli/Tianfu Square tourist triangle is compact enough to cover by bike.

### Shanghai — French Concession Cycling

**Shanghai's French Concession** (法租界) is one of the city's most pleasant cycling areas. The neighborhood is dense, relatively flat, with plane tree-lined streets, cafes, boutiques, and restaurants. Bike-share bikes cover the whole area. A casual 2-hour ride through the concession roads — Wukang Road, Anfu Road, Xinhua Road — is a genuinely enjoyable way to explore.

The city-wide bike-share is heavily used here, and the flat terrain makes it accessible. Just watch the traffic — Shanghai cycling requires comfort with urban density.

### Xi'an — Cycling the City Wall

Xi'an's ancient city wall is completely walkable and cyclable. You can rent bikes directly on the wall (a short stretch near the South Gate) and do the full **14 km circuit** of the top of the Ming-dynasty city wall. It takes about 1.5–2 hours by bike. Rental is ¥45 for 100 minutes, tandem bikes available. One of the best ways to understand the scale and layout of the old city.

## Multi-Day Cycling Routes

For serious cyclists, China has extraordinary touring routes:

**Yunnan Golden Triangle** (Lijiang–Shangri-La–Tiger Leaping Gorge): A classic multi-day route through high-altitude Yunnan. Challenging (Shangri-La is at 3,200m), but the scenery of the Hengduan Mountain ranges is outstanding. Rent touring bikes in Lijiang (gear rental shops near the Old Town).

**Sichuan-Tibet Highway (318 National Road)**: The most famous cycling route in China. Most cyclists start from Chengdu and take 20–30 days to reach Lhasa. The route crosses high-altitude passes, dozens of mountain ridges, and some of the most dramatic road scenery in the world. Not a casual trip — requires fitness, altitude preparation, and significant logistics.

**Yangshuo Extended**: Beyond the Yulong River valley day loop, you can cycle to **Xingping** (2–3 hours from Yangshuo) along the Li River — this village appears on the 20 yuan note.

## Practical Tips

**Helmets**: Not commonly used by Chinese bike-share cyclists. If you're doing serious rural riding, bring your own or rent from quality touring shops.

**Traffic**: Urban cycling in China requires attention and confidence. Cars do not default to yielding to cyclists at intersections. Watch, be predictable, and don't expect cars to stop.

**E-bikes**: Electric bikes (电动车) are pervasive in Chinese cities. You can rent them in tourist areas like Yangshuo. Easy to ride (no license required for under-50cc models), practically zero effort compared to pedaling. Popular among locals for medium distances.
