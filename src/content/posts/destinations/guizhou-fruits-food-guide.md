Guizhou (贵州, Guìzhōu) is a hidden gem for food lovers, offering a bounty of unique fruits and culinary traditions that are often overlooked by international travelers. From the legendary honey plum (蜂糖李, fēngtáng lǐ) to the delicate white phoenix peach (小白凤, xiǎo bái fèng) from neighboring Sichuan, this guide will take you on a flavorful journey through the region's summer harvests and preserved delicacies. Whether you're a fruit enthusiast or a curious foodie, Guizhou's vibrant food scene promises unforgettable tastes.

## Table of contents

## Why Guizhou Fruits Stand Out

Guizhou's mountainous terrain and subtropical climate create ideal conditions for growing intensely flavored fruits. The province is known for its high-altitude orchards, clean water sources, and traditional farming methods that prioritize taste over yield. Many fruits, like the honey plum, are grown in remote valleys where the combination of sunlight, temperature variation, and mineral-rich soil produces exceptional sweetness and aroma.

## Top Fruits to Try in Guizhou

### 1. Honey Plum (蜂糖李, Fēngtáng Lǐ)

Often called the "ceiling of plums," the honey plum is a must-try summer fruit. Grown primarily in **Liùmǎ Town** (六马镇), **Zhènníng County** (镇宁县), **Ānshùn City** (安顺市), this plum has a greenish-yellow skin that looks underripe but hides incredibly sweet (16-20° Brix), crisp flesh with a hint of tartness. It is a **national geographical indication product** since 2017.

- **Taste:** Sweet with a subtle tang, crunchy texture, and a clean pit that separates easily.
- **Best time:** Late June to July; the season lasts only about 3-4 weeks.
- **Where to buy:** Local markets in Anshun or direct from orchards. Some vendors ship nationwide via JD Logistics.
- **Tip:** Look for plums with a slight give when gently squeezed – they are at peak ripeness.

### 2. White Phoenix Peach (小白凤, Xiǎo Bái Fèng)

Though technically from **Longquanyi District** (龙泉驿区) in **Chengdu, Sichuan**, this peach is widely available in Guizhou markets during summer. It is a variety of the famous Longquanyi white peach, known for its fragrant aroma, thin skin, and juicy, melt-in-your-mouth flesh. Sweetness reaches 17° Brix, comparable to lychee.

- **Taste:** Aromatic, soft, and bursting with juice; not cloyingly sweet.
- **Best time:** Late June to early July; only about 15 days of peak season.
- **Where to buy:** Fruit shops or online platforms like JD.com. Expect to pay around ¥50-80 for a box of 12-15 peaches.
- **Tip:** Let firm peaches ripen at room temperature for 1-2 days for best flavor. Refrigerate after ripening.

## Beyond Fruits: Guizhou's Savory Food Culture

Guizhou cuisine is famous for its bold use of **sour and spicy** flavors, often from fermented ingredients like **sour soup** (酸汤, suān tāng) and **chili paste**. But there's also a refined tradition of **cold marinated delicacies** (糟货, zāo huò) influenced by neighboring Jiangnan region – a perfect companion to summer fruits.

### Cold Marinated Trio (糟三样, Zāo Sān Yàng)

This classic Jiangnan dish uses **fermented rice wine lees** (酒糟, jiǔ zāo) to marinate pork belly, tongue, and ear. The result is a refreshing, savory, and slightly alcoholic cold appetizer that cuts through summer heat.

- **What's included:** Sliced pork belly (肚尖), pork tongue (口条), and pork ear (耳片).
- **Flavor profile:** Savory with a subtle wine fragrance, tender yet crunchy texture.
- **Where to try:** Many restaurants in Guiyang serve it, or you can buy vacuum-packed versions from specialty stores.
- **Pairing:** Perfect with cold beer or as a side to congee.

## Best Time to Visit for Food

- **June to July:** Peak season for honey plums and white phoenix peaches. Also the start of summer fruit festivals.
- **August to September:** Try local grapes, kiwifruit, and the famous **Guizhou sour fish** (酸汤鱼).
- **October:** Chestnuts and persimmons.

## Getting There & Around

- **By air:** Fly into **Guiyang Longdongbao International Airport** (贵阳龙洞堡国际机场, KWE). Major cities have direct flights.
- **By train:** High-speed rail connects Guiyang to Chengdu (3h), Chongqing (2h), and Guangzhou (4.5h).
- **Local transport:** Use the metro in Guiyang, or rent a car/driver for countryside fruit-picking trips. Didi (Chinese Uber) works well.

## Where to Stay

- **Guiyang city center:** For easy access to markets and restaurants. Recommended areas: Yunyan District (云岩区) or Nanming District (南明区).
- **Anshun:** Closer to honey plum orchards. Look for guesthouses near Huangguoshu Waterfall.
- **Longquanyi (Chengdu):** If you're combining a peach trip, stay near the peach orchards on Longquan Mountain.

## Practical Tips for Travelers

- **Payment:** Alipay (支付宝) and WeChat Pay (微信支付) are widely accepted. Carry some cash for rural markets.
- **Language:** English is not common in small towns. Download a translation app like Pleco or Google Translate.
- **Etiquette:** When buying fruit, it's okay to ask for a taste (试吃, shì chī). Bargaining is expected at wet markets.
- **Visa:** Most travelers need a visa. Check if you qualify for **144-hour transit visa** in select cities like Chengdu.
- **Shipping:** If you fall in love with a fruit, many orchards can ship to your hotel or home via express delivery.

## Sample 3-Day Food Itinerary

### Day 1: Guiyang
- Morning: Visit **Erqi Road Food Market** (二七路小吃街) for sour soup fish and rice noodles.
- Afternoon: Explore **Qianling Park** (黔灵山公园) and try local snacks like **siwawa** (丝娃娃).
- Evening: Dinner at a restaurant serving **Guizhou-style hotpot**.

### Day 2: Anshun – Honey Plum Hunt
- Morning: Drive to Liùmǎ Town (1.5h from Anshun). Visit a honey plum orchard (book ahead).
- Lunch: Farmhouse meal with local specialties like **huangguoshu fish**.
- Afternoon: Visit **Huangguoshu Waterfall** (黄果树瀑布).
- Evening: Return to Anshun for street food.

### Day 3: Chengdu – Peach Paradise (Optional)
- Take a morning high-speed train to Chengdu (2h).
- Head to Longquanyi for peach picking (seasonal).
- Afternoon: Visit **Jinli Ancient Street** (锦里) for snacks.
- Evening: Enjoy a Sichuan hotpot before departure.

## FAQ

**Q: When is the best time to eat honey plums?**
A: The honey plum season is very short – typically from late June to mid-July. The peak is around the first two weeks of July. Outside this window, they are not available fresh.

**Q: Can I find honey plums outside Guizhou?**
A: Yes, they are increasingly shipped to other Chinese cities via e-commerce, but the flavor is best when eaten in the core production area of Liùmǎ Town. Imitations from other regions lack the same sweetness.

**Q: How do I know if a white phoenix peach is ripe?**
A: Gently press the peach near the stem. If it yields slightly, it's ready to eat. If it's hard, let it ripen at room temperature for 1-2 days. The skin should be easy to peel when ripe.

**Q: Is it safe to eat street food in Guizhou?**
A: Generally yes, but choose stalls with high turnover and clean conditions. Avoid raw or undercooked items if you have a sensitive stomach. Bottled water is recommended.

**Q: Do I need to speak Chinese to buy fruits?**
A: It helps, but you can point and use numbers. Many vendors use handheld calculators to show prices. Smile and be patient – it goes a long way.

**Q: Can I ship fruits home?**
A: Some orchards offer international shipping, but it's expensive and risky due to customs. It's better to enjoy them fresh during your trip. For domestic shipping within China, JD Logistics is reliable.
