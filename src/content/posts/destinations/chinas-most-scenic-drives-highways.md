China's vast and varied landscapes are best experienced on the open road. From the lush wetlands of Jilin to the high-altitude grasslands of Sichuan, scenic highways offer a front-row seat to nature's most vibrant displays. This guide takes you through two of China's most picturesque drives: the G334 National Highway in Jilin, a 895-kilometer tapestry of seven colors, and the Sichuan-Tibet Highway, which climbs to the 'Sky City' of Litang. Whether you're a seasoned road tripper or a first-time visitor, these routes promise unforgettable vistas and cultural encounters.

## Table of contents

## The G334 National Highway: Jilin's Color Palette

Stretching across the heart of Jilin Province, the G334 National Highway runs from Helong in the east to Dong Ujimqin Banner in Inner Mongolia, covering 895 kilometers within Jilin. Unlike the border-hugging G331, G334 cuts through the Songnen Plain and Baishan Mountains, weaving together rivers, lakes, wetlands, forests, farmland, and cultural relics into a colorful mosaic. The best time to drive this route is summer (June to September), when the landscapes are at their greenest and most vibrant.

![Lush green wetlands of Xianghai Nature Reserve in Jilin](https://images.pexels.com/photos/15528404/pexels-photo-15528404.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

### Xianghai Wetlands: The Green of Life

Start your journey in the west at Xianghai Wetlands, a national nature reserve and a Ramsar site. This vast expanse of marshes, grasslands, and lakes is a haven for birds, including over 200 red-crowned cranes—nearly 10% of the world's population. The endless green of the reeds and meadows is a soothing introduction to the drive. Entry is free, but guided tours are available. Bring binoculars for birdwatching.

### Qian'an Mud Forest: The Yellow of Another World

Heading east, you'll encounter the Qian'an Mud Forest, a unique erosional landscape of towering mud pillars and deep gullies. Locally called 'Wolf's Tooth Dam,' it resembles the surface of Mars or the moon. This site is also a paleontological treasure, with fossils of woolly rhinos and mammoths from the Late Pleistocene. A pair of complete woolly rhino fossils, dubbed 'the couple,' were discovered here—a rare find. The site is open year-round; tickets are around ¥50.

![Eerie mud pillars at Qian'an Mud Forest in Jilin](https://images.pexels.com/photos/15528406/pexels-photo-15528406.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

### Chagan Lake: The Blue of Serenity

Further east, Chagan Lake (查干湖, Chágān Hú) is a vast blue gem on the Songnen Plain. Covering up to 500 square kilometers, it's one of China's largest freshwater lakes. In summer, the lake is perfect for boating and fishing; in winter, the famous ice fishing festival draws crowds. The water ranges from deep cobalt to turquoise, creating a stunning gradient. Entry to the lake area is free, but boat rides cost around ¥100.

### Songhua River: The Jade of Three Cascades

The Songhua River (松花江, Sōnghuā Jiāng) flows like a jade ribbon through Jilin City and Songyuan. The river's upper reaches feature three man-made lakes—Songhua Lake, Hongshi Lake, and Baishan Lake—formed by hydroelectric dams. These lakes are nestled in forested valleys, offering opportunities for hiking and photography. The best views are from the winding roads along the river.

### Red Cultural Sites: The Crimson of History

Near Panshi, the Hongshilazi Anti-Japanese Base Area preserves the legacy of General Yang Jingyu, who led the Northeast Anti-Japanese United Army against Japanese occupation in the 1930s. The site includes over 3,300 archaeological features, such as living quarters and meeting grounds. It's a poignant stop for history buffs. Entry is free.

### 914 Factory: The Grey of Industry

Just 3 km off G334, the 914 Factory in Yansong Town is a living museum of China's Third Front Movement. Built in 1969, it produced military equipment and later transitioned to civilian steel pipes. Today, it operates as an industrial tourism site, where visitors can see vintage machinery and hear stories from retired workers. Tours are available on weekdays; check ahead.

### Golden Farmland: The Gold of Harvest

Jilin's black soil belt, between 40° and 45° north latitude, is one of the world's most fertile regions. In late summer, fields of corn and rice ripple like golden waves. Stop at a local farm to taste fresh corn or pick up some杂粮 (mixed grains). The countryside is a photographer's dream.

## The Sichuan-Tibet Highway: To the Sky City of Litang

From the lowlands of Jilin, we ascend to the high plateau of Sichuan. The Sichuan-Tibet Highway (G318) is one of China's most famous scenic routes, and a highlight is the detour to Litang County, known as the 'Sky City' at an elevation of over 4,000 meters. The best time to drive this route is May to October, when the weather is mild and the grasslands are green.

![Vast grasslands of Maoya Prairie in Litang, Sichuan](https://images.pexels.com/photos/37769783/pexels-photo-37769783.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

### Maoya Prairie: Green to the Horizon

West of Litang, Maoya Prairie (毛垭大草原, Máoyā Dà Cǎoyuán) stretches for tens of kilometers, a sea of green dotted with yaks and Tibetan tents. The Wuliang River meanders through, creating wetlands that attract wildlife. This is a perfect spot for a picnic or a short hike. The prairie is free to access; camping is allowed with permission.

### Litang: The Sky City

Litang town itself is a cultural gem. The Letong Ancient Town (勒通古镇, Lè Tōng Gǔ Zhèn) features over 4,000 traditional Tibetan houses, many converted into museums, guesthouses, and craft shops. The 4A-rated scenic area includes the Thousand-Household Tibetan Village, where you can experience Tibetan architecture, thangka painting, and butter tea. Entry to the old town is free; some museums charge ¥20-50.

![Traditional Tibetan houses in Letong Ancient Town, Litang](https://images.pexels.com/photos/31579502/pexels-photo-31579502.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

### Practical Tips for the Drive

- **Getting There**: Fly to Chengdu, then drive west on G318 to Litang (about 2 days). Alternatively, take a bus from Chengdu to Litang (10-12 hours). For G334, fly to Changchun and rent a car.
- **Car Rental**: International brands like Hertz and Avis are available in major cities. You'll need an International Driving Permit (IDP) plus a Chinese translation. Alternatively, hire a driver with a car.
- **Mobile Payment**: Alipay and WeChat Pay are widely accepted. Carry some cash for remote areas.
- **Altitude**: Litang is above 4,000m; acclimatize for a day before strenuous activity. Bring sunscreen and warm layers.
- **Accommodation**: In Jilin, stay in Songyuan or Jilin City (¥200-500/night). In Litang, choose from Tibetan guesthouses (¥150-300/night) or hotels.
- **Food**: Try Jilin's fish from Chagan Lake and Tibetan momos (dumplings) in Litang.

## Suggested Itinerary

### Day 1-3: Jilin's G334
- Day 1: Fly to Changchun, drive to Xianghai Wetlands (3 hours). Overnight in Tongyu.
- Day 2: Drive to Qian'an Mud Forest (2 hours), then to Chagan Lake (1.5 hours). Overnight at the lake.
- Day 3: Drive along Songhua River to Jilin City (2 hours), visit Songhua Lake. Overnight in Jilin City.

### Day 4-6: Sichuan's High Plateau
- Day 4: Fly to Chengdu, drive to Kangding (4 hours). Acclimatize.
- Day 5: Drive to Litang via Zheduo Mountain (6 hours). Explore Maoya Prairie.
- Day 6: Visit Letong Ancient Town, then return to Chengdu.

## Best Time to Visit
- **G334**: June to September for green landscapes; October for autumn colors.
- **Sichuan-Tibet Highway**: May to October; avoid July-August rainy season for landslides.

## Costs
- **Car rental**: ¥300-600/day
- **Fuel**: ¥0.8-1.0/km
- **Tolls**: G334 has few tolls; G318 has some.
- **Accommodation**: ¥200-500/night
- **Food**: ¥50-100/day per person

## FAQ

- **Q: Do I need a special permit to drive on these highways?**
  A: No special permit is needed for G334 or G318 to Litang, but you need a valid Chinese driver's license or IDP with translation.
- **Q: Is it safe to drive at high altitude?**
  A: Yes, but be cautious of altitude sickness. Drive slowly, stay hydrated, and avoid alcohol.
- **Q: Can I use my international credit card?**
  A: Major hotels and some restaurants accept cards, but cash and mobile payments are more common.
- **Q: Are there English signs?**
  A: On major highways, signs are bilingual. In remote areas, they may be only in Chinese. Use a GPS app like Baidu Maps.
- **Q: What should I pack?**
  A: Sunscreen, warm clothing, snacks, water, a first-aid kit, and a power bank.
