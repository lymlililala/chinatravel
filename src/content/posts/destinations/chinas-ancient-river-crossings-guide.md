China's great rivers—the Yellow River (黄河, Huáng Hé) and the Yangtze River (长江, Cháng Jiāng)—have long been arteries of civilization. But before bridges spanned their mighty currents, it was the ferry crossings (渡口, dùkǒu) that determined the fate of armies, the flow of trade, and the movement of people. These humble riverbanks were once the most strategic points in the empire, where dynasties rose and fell, where scholars embarked on imperial exams, and where merchants exchanged goods from north to south. Today, many of these ancient crossings have faded into obscurity, replaced by modern bridges and highways. Yet they remain powerful windows into China's past, offering travelers a chance to walk in the footsteps of emperors, poets, and generals.

## Why Ancient River Crossings Matter

Before the age of railways and highways, China's rivers were the superhighways of their time. Crossings were not just transportation hubs—they were choke points that controlled the movement of armies, grain, and information. A single ferry could decide the outcome of a war or the prosperity of a region. The most famous crossings became legendary, immortalized in poetry and historical records. For the modern traveler, visiting these sites offers a unique blend of history, culture, and scenic beauty.

## Top Ancient Crossings to Visit

### 1. Longmen Ferry (龙门渡, Lóngmén Dù) – The Throat of the Yellow River

Located at the border of Hejin in Shanxi (山西河津) and Hancheng in Shaanxi (陕西韩城), Longmen Ferry sits at a narrow gorge where the Yellow River is only 40 meters wide. The water roars through with thunderous force, earning it the nickname "Yellow River's Throat." This crossing was a key strategic point since the Spring and Autumn period (770–476 BCE). Qin armies crossed here to defeat Jin, and Tang Emperor Li Yuan used it to conquer the Guanzhong region. Today, a modern highway bridge spans the gorge, but the ancient site remains, offering dramatic views of the churning river.

**Getting there:** Take a high-speed train to Hejin East Station, then a taxi (about 40 minutes) to the Longmen scenic area. The site is free to explore.

### 2. Maojin Ferry (茅津渡, Máojīn Dù) – The Number One Ferry of Southern Shanxi

On the banks of the Yellow River in Pinglu County, Shanxi (山西平陆), facing Sanmenxia in Henan (河南三门峡), Maojin Ferry was one of the "Three Great Yellow River Ferries" along with Fengling and Dayu. Active since the Spring and Autumn period, it was both a military stronghold and a bustling commercial port. In 655 BCE, the state of Jin used this crossing to launch a surprise attack on Guo. During the Anti-Japanese War and the Chinese Civil War, it remained a vital military passage. The ferry ceased operations in 1993 after the Sanmenxia Dam and a highway bridge were built, but the site is now a scenic park with boardwalks and sunset viewing platforms.

**Best time to visit:** Late afternoon for the famous "Maojin Evening Crossing" (茅津晚渡), a local scenic spectacle.

### 3. Xijin Ferry (西津渡, Xījīn Dù) – A Thousand Years of History in One Street

Nestled at the foot of Yuntai Mountain in Zhenjiang, Jiangsu (江苏镇江), Xijin Ferry is often called "China's Ancient Ferry Museum." Unlike many crossings that have vanished, Xijin has preserved an entire historic street complex. Layers of road surfaces from the Tang, Song, Ming, and Qing dynasties are exposed in a 4.4-meter-deep excavation, vividly illustrating the passage of time. The site also features a Yuan Dynasty stone pagoda, a Song Dynasty Guanyin Cave, and a Qing Dynasty waiting pavilion. Xijin was a key crossing for northern immigrants during the Yongjia Rebellion (4th century), and poets like Li Bai and Wang Anshi left their verses here.

**How to explore:** The ancient street is pedestrian-only. Admission to the main area is free, but some individual attractions charge a small fee (around ¥20–30). Allow 2–3 hours.

### 4. Guazhou Ferry (瓜洲渡, Guāzhōu Dù) – The Poetic Crossing

Across the Yangtze from Xijin lies Guazhou Ferry in Yangzhou, Jiangsu (江苏扬州). This crossing rose to prominence after a canal was dug in the Tang Dynasty, connecting the Grand Canal to the Yangtze. It became a vital node for grain transport and a magnet for poets. Over 3,000 poems mention Guazhou, including Bai Juyi's "The waters of the Bian flow, the waters of the Si flow, flowing to the ancient ferry of Guazhou." The crossing is also the setting of the tragic story "Du Shiniang Sinks the Jewel Box in Anger," a popular Ming Dynasty tale. Today, Guazhou blends history with modern attractions like music festivals and art galleries.

**Getting there:** Take a bus from Yangzhou to Guazhou Town (about 40 minutes). The ferry still operates for passengers and vehicles.

### 5. Laza Ancient Ferry (拉鲊古渡, Lāzhǎ Gǔ Dù) – Zhuge Liang's Southern Crossing

On the Jinsha River in Panzhihua, Sichuan (四川攀枝花), Laza Ferry was the site where the legendary strategist Zhuge Liang crossed during his Southern Campaign in 225 CE. The river here is relatively wide and calm, making it a natural crossing point on the ancient Southern Silk Road. A stone inscription attributed to Zhuge Liang, reading "可以栖迟" (a place to rest), still survives on a nearby cliff. In the 1960s, the ferry was used by the first builders of the Panzhihua steel base. Today, it is a cultural site with walking trails and blooming kapok trees.

**Best time to visit:** Spring (March–April) when the kapok flowers are in full bloom.

### 6. Fengling Ferry (风陵渡, Fēnglíng Dù) – Where Three Provinces Meet

Located in Ruicheng County, Shanxi (山西芮城), at the corner where the Yellow River turns east, Fengling Ferry is the junction of Shanxi, Shaanxi, and Henan provinces. It was the busiest ferry on the Yellow River during the Tang Dynasty, handling grain, salt, and timber. Fengling also gained fame through Jin Yong's wuxia novel *The Return of the Condor Heroes*, where the heroine Guo Xiang first meets the protagonist Yang Guo. The ferry ceased operations after the Fenglingdu Yellow River Highway Bridge opened in 1994. The area now offers panoramic views of the river and the nearby Tongguan Fortress.

**How to visit:** The ferry site is accessible by bus from Ruicheng or Tongguan. The best view is from the Tongguan Ancient City wall.

## Practical Tips for Visiting Ancient Crossings

### Best Time to Visit
- **Spring (March–May)** and **autumn (September–November)** offer mild weather and clear skies, ideal for outdoor exploration.
- Summer can be hot and humid, especially along the Yangtze. Winter is cold but less crowded.

### Getting Around
- **High-speed rail** connects most major cities near these crossings (e.g., Xi'an, Zhengzhou, Nanjing, Shanghai). From the station, take a local bus or taxi.
- For remote crossings like Laza, consider renting a car or hiring a driver.
- **Mobile payment** (Alipay or WeChat Pay) is widely accepted, but carry some cash for small vendors.

### Tickets and Booking
- Most ferry sites are open-air and free to enter. Some, like Xijin Ferry's historic street, have paid sections for specific attractions.
- Check opening hours in advance, as some sites close early (around 5:00 PM).

### Where to Stay
- **Zhenjiang** (for Xijin Ferry): Boutique hotels near the ancient street.
- **Yangzhou** (for Guazhou Ferry): Traditional courtyard hotels in the old city.
- **Panzhihua** (for Laza): Budget hotels near the train station.

## Sample Itinerary: A Week of River Crossings

- **Day 1–2:** Arrive in Xi'an. Visit Longmen Ferry (half-day trip) and explore the Terracotta Warriors.
- **Day 3:** Take a high-speed train to Sanmenxia. Visit Maojin Ferry and Sanmenxia Dam.
- **Day 4–5:** Train to Zhenjiang. Spend a day at Xijin Ferry and another at Guazhou Ferry (ferry across the Yangtze).
- **Day 6:** Fly to Panzhihua (or take an overnight train). Visit Laza Ancient Ferry.
- **Day 7:** Return to Chengdu or Kunming for departure.

## Food to Try Near the Crossings

- **Shanxi** (Longmen, Maojin, Fengling): Knife-cut noodles (刀削面, dāoxiāomiàn) and braised pork with millet.
- **Jiangsu** (Xijin, Guazhou): Zhenjiang vinegar pork (镇江肴肉) and Yangzhou fried rice (扬州炒饭).
- **Sichuan** (Laza): Spicy hot pot and grilled fish from the Jinsha River.

## Conclusion

China's ancient river crossings are more than historical footnotes—they are places where the past feels palpable. Standing by the Yellow River at Longmen or walking the thousand-year-old street at Xijin, you can almost hear the echoes of soldiers, merchants, and poets. These sites offer a unique perspective on China's geography and history, far from the crowded tourist trails. Whether you're a history buff, a poetry lover, or simply a traveler seeking authentic experiences, these ancient crossings will leave a lasting impression.
