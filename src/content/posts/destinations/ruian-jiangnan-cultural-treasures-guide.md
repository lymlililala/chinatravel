Uncover the rich history and cultural heritage of lesser-known Jiangnan cities, from ancient trade to oracle bones. Ruian (瑞安, Ruì'ān), a small city in Zhejiang province, is a fascinating blend of academia and commerce. It was here that the first oracle bone script was deciphered, pushing back China's written history by a millennium. Today, Ruian is also a powerhouse of manufacturing, producing everything from auto parts to elastic bands. This guide takes you through its unique cultural landmarks, natural beauty, and practical travel tips.

## Why Ruian Matters: A City of Contrasts

![The Yuhai Library, a Qing-era library in Ruian where oracle bones were first deciphered](https://images.pexels.com/photos/8661757/pexels-photo-8661757.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

Ruian is a city of delightful contradictions. It's a top-30 county in China's economic rankings, churning out over 70% of small auto parts for domestic cars and 80% of the nation's elastic bands. Yet, it's also the birthplace of the first successful deciphering of **oracle bone script (甲骨文, jiǎgǔwén)**, the earliest known form of Chinese writing. This dual identity—shrewd businessman and meticulous scholar—defines Ruian's character.

### The Oracle Bone Breakthrough

In 1899, oracle bones—animal bones and turtle shells inscribed with ancient characters—were discovered in Henan. They were identified as divination records from the Shang dynasty (c. 1600–1046 BCE). The race to decode them began. In 1904, Sun Yirang (孙诒让, Sūn Yìràng), a reclusive scholar from Ruian, obtained a copy of the first oracle bone catalog, *Tieyun Canggui*. Locking himself in the family library, the **Yuhai Tower (玉海楼, Yùhǎi Lóu)**, he spent two months deciphering over 300 characters, publishing *Qiewen Juli* (契文举例). Although many of his interpretations were later corrected, his work laid the foundation for oracle bone studies. Today, Yuhai Tower stands as a testament to this intellectual feat.

## Top Sights in Ruian

![Gudianmingyuanliu Garden Scenery, Suzhou](/img/destinations/ruian-jiangnan-cultural-treasures-guide/1.webp)

### Yuhai Tower (玉海楼)

This Qing-dynasty library is one of Zhejiang's four great libraries. It houses rare books and manuscripts, and its quiet courtyard offers a glimpse into the life of a scholar-official. The building itself is a fine example of southern Zhejiang architecture, with whitewashed walls and dark wooden beams.

### Caocun Town (曹村镇)

Known as the "Number One Village of Imperial Examinations," Caocun produced 82 *jinshi* (进士, successful candidates in the highest imperial exam) over two centuries. Stroll through its ancient streets, visit ancestral halls, and learn about the region's Confucian heritage.

### Zhaoliao Stream (寨寮溪)

Nestled in the southern foothills of the Yandang Mountains, this scenic area is often called "Little Jiuzhaigou of Southern Zhejiang." Crystal-clear streams cascade through lush valleys, making it a perfect escape from summer heat. The poet Lu You once described boating here as "sailing in a mirror."

### Beiji Island (北麂岛)

For coastal scenery, take a ferry to Beiji Island, sometimes called "Ruian's Maldives." Its rocky shores, seafood markets, and lighthouse offer a relaxing day trip. Try local specialties like yellow croaker and spicy sea snails.

## Suggested Itinerary: 2 Days in Ruian

### Day 1: Cultural Immersion

- **Morning**: Visit **Yuhai Tower** and the adjacent Sun Yirang Memorial Hall. Spend an hour exploring the library and its exhibits on oracle bone studies.
- **Afternoon**: Head to **Caocun Town** for lunch (try local *maitieguo*, a crispy pancake). Explore the village's imperial examination culture.
- **Evening**: Stroll along the **Feiyun River** (飞云江) promenade. Watch the sunset over the river, a scene immortalized by poets.

### Day 2: Nature and Commerce

- **Morning**: Take a taxi or bus to **Zhaoliao Stream** (about 1 hour). Hike along the water, swim in natural pools, and enjoy a picnic.
- **Afternoon**: Return to the city and visit the **Ruian Auto Parts Market** (optional, for industrial tourism enthusiasts). Alternatively, relax in a local tea house.
- **Evening**: Feast on *zhuzangfen* (猪脏粉, rice noodles with pork offal) at a local eatery.

## Getting There & Around

![Shi Lake Scenery, Jiangsu, Suzhou](/img/destinations/ruian-jiangnan-cultural-treasures-guide/2.webp)

### By Air

The nearest major airport is **Wenzhou Longwan International Airport (温州龙湾国际机场, Wēnzhōu Lóngwān Guójì Jīchǎng)**, about 40 km north of Ruian. From the airport, take a taxi (approx. 1 hour, ¥150-200) or a direct bus to Ruian city center.

### By Train

**Ruian Railway Station (瑞安站, Ruì'ān Zhàn)** is on the Wenzhou-Fuzhou high-speed rail line. Trains from Shanghai (3 hours, ¥200-250) and Hangzhou (2 hours, ¥120-150) arrive frequently. From the station, city buses and taxis are available.

### Getting Around

Ruian's city center is walkable. For sights outside town, use **Didi** (China's Uber) or local buses. Taxis start at ¥8. Mobile payments (Alipay, WeChat Pay) are widely accepted.

## Food Highlights

![Zhuozheng Garden, Suzhou](/img/destinations/ruian-jiangnan-cultural-treasures-guide/3.webp)

Ruian's cuisine reflects its coastal and mountainous setting. Must-try dishes:

- **Zhuzangfen (猪脏粉)**: Rice noodles in a rich pork bone broth, topped with pig intestines and blood curd. A hearty breakfast.
- **Maiteguo (麦贴锅)**: A crispy wheat pancake, often stuffed with minced pork or vegetables.
- **Yangmei (杨梅)**: In June, the local water chestnut-shaped bayberries are sweet and fragrant.
- **Seafood**: Fresh from the East China Sea, try yellow croaker, mantis shrimp, and periwinkles.

## Best Time to Visit

**Spring (March-May)** and **autumn (September-November)** are ideal, with mild temperatures (15-25°C). Summer (June-August) is hot and humid but great for river activities. Winter (December-February) is chilly (5-10°C) but less crowded.

## Costs & Practical Tips

- **Daily budget**: ¥300-500 per person (mid-range), including accommodation, meals, and transport.
- **Tickets**: Yuhai Tower costs about ¥20; Zhaoliao Stream is free; Beiji Island ferry is ¥100 round trip.
- **Language**: Mandarin is standard; English is limited. Download a translation app.
- **Etiquette**: Remove shoes when entering temples or homes. Tipping is not customary.
- **Visa**: Most foreigners need a tourist visa (L visa). For 144-hour transit without visa, check eligibility at Shanghai or Hangzhou airports.

## FAQ

- **Is Ruian safe for solo travelers?** Yes, Ruian is very safe, even at night. Petty crime is rare.
- **Can I use credit cards?** International credit cards are accepted in major hotels but not in small shops. Use Alipay or WeChat Pay, or carry cash (¥500-1000).
- **How do I get to Beiji Island?** Take a ferry from Ruian Port (飞云江码头). Ferries depart at 9:00 AM daily (subject to weather). Book tickets at the port.
- **What is the significance of oracle bones?** Oracle bones are the earliest known Chinese writing, dating to the Shang dynasty. Their deciphering confirmed the Shang as a historical dynasty.
- **Are there English guides?** At Yuhai Tower, English audio guides are sometimes available. For other sites, hire a private guide through your hotel.
