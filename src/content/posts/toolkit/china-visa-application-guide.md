Despite the expansion of China's visa-free programs in recent years, most international visitors still need to apply for a tourist visa (L visa) before travel. The process is more straightforward than it used to be — the COVA online application system has simplified some steps — but it still requires attention to documentation detail. Getting it wrong means delays or refusal.

This guide walks through the complete application process with specific advice for common nationalities.

## Table of contents

## What Is the L Visa?

![Xingping, Guangxi, Yangshuo](/img/toolkit/china-visa-application-guide/1.webp)

The L visa (旅游签证, tourist visa) is the standard entry visa for tourism, visiting friends and family, and other non-business, non-work purposes. It's typically issued as:

- **Single entry:** Valid for one entry within 3 months of issue, stay up to 30-90 days depending on nationality
- **Double or multiple entry:** Available to citizens of some nationalities and for applicants with strong travel history to China; allows multiple entries within the validity period

The L visa does not permit working, studying for courses longer than 6 months, or conducting business activities that generate income in China.

## Documents Required

**Universal requirements (all nationalities):**

1. **Passport:** Valid for at least 6 months beyond your intended stay, with at least 2 blank pages

2. **Passport photo:** Recent (within 6 months), white background, 2 inches × 2 inches (51mm × 51mm) or as specified. Most embassies now accept digital photos in their online systems.

3. **Completed visa application form:** Available on the Chinese Embassy website for your country, or through the COVA online system. The form asks for: personal information, employment/study details, travel history, itinerary in China, emergency contact.

4. **Outbound flight booking:** A confirmed booking showing your departure from China (or round-trip booking). Most embassies accept a booking confirmation — you don't need to have paid for a fully non-refundable ticket, but it must be a real booking with a reference number.

5. **Hotel confirmation:** Confirmation for at least the first night, ideally all accommodation throughout your China visit. The hotel address(es) will be used to verify your stated itinerary.

**Additional documents required by many nationalities:**

6. **Bank statement:** 3 months of bank statements showing sufficient funds for your trip. The threshold varies by embassy — generally ¥3,000-5,000 per day of the intended stay is a rough guideline, but specific requirements vary. US and UK applicants typically don't need this; some developing-country applicants face stricter financial documentation requirements.

7. **Employment letter or proof of status:** A letter from your employer confirming employment and granting leave (for employed applicants), or proof of enrollment (for students), or proof of income (for self-employed or retired applicants).

## How to Apply

### Option 1: Apply Directly at the Chinese Embassy or Consulate

Most embassies and consulates offer in-person appointment-based applications. The process:

1. Complete the application form online or on paper
2. Book an appointment at the Chinese Embassy/Consulate website for your country
3. Attend the appointment with all original documents
4. Pay the visa fee (varies by nationality — see below)
5. Collect your passport with the visa (or receive it by mail if you paid for that service)

**This is the most direct option.** No agency fees, no middlemen. Processing times are typically 4-7 business days.

### Option 2: Apply Through a Visa Agency (CVASC or Private Agency)

China Visa Application Service Centres (CVASC) operate in many countries as authorised agents for the Chinese Embassy. In some countries, the Embassy has fully outsourced applications to these centres.

**Private visa agencies** (not affiliated with the Embassy) add an additional service fee (US$30-80 typically) but handle the submission on your behalf. Useful if:
- The nearest Embassy or CVASC is far from you
- You want the agency to review your documents for completeness before submission
- You need express processing that the agency can facilitate

### Option 3: COVA Online Application

The China Online Visa Application (COVA) system allows some nationalities to submit applications online and receive an electronic visa, bypassing the physical submission process.

**As of 2026:** COVA is available to citizens of many countries but not all. Check whether your nationality is eligible at the official China Online Visa website. If COVA is available to you, the process is:

1. Create an account at visaforchina.cn
2. Complete the online application form and upload required documents
3. Pay the fee online
4. Receive an e-Visa by email (typically within 4-7 business days)
5. Print the e-Visa and present it at the port of entry with your passport

The e-Visa process is significantly more convenient than embassy visits and is worth using if it's available to your nationality.

## Visa Fees

Visa fees vary by nationality due to reciprocity agreements. Approximate fees:

| Nationality | Single Entry | Double Entry | Multiple Entry |
|---|---|---|---|
| US citizens | US$185 | US$185 | US$185 |
| UK citizens | ~£175 | ~£175 | ~£175 |
| EU citizens (most) | ~€60-75 | ~€90-100 | ~€110-140 |
| Australian citizens | ~AU$90 | ~AU$135 | ~AU$180 |
| Canadian citizens | ~CAD$190 | ~CAD$190 | ~CAD$190 |

These fees reflect the reciprocity system — countries that charge Chinese citizens high visa fees see reciprocally high fees from China. US citizens pay significantly more than EU citizens for this reason.

## Processing Times

![Forbidden City Panorama Interior, Beijing](/img/toolkit/china-visa-application-guide/2.webp)

**Standard processing:** 4-7 business days after submission of complete documentation

**Express processing:** Some embassies offer 2-3 business day processing for an additional fee (typically US$20-30 extra). Not all embassies offer this; check availability.

**When to apply:** Apply no earlier than 3 months before your intended entry date (your visa validity starts from issue, not from your trip start, but most embassies won't accept applications too far in advance). **Apply at least 2 weeks before travel**, preferably 3-4 weeks to allow buffer for any documentation issues.

## Common Refusal Reasons and How to Avoid Them

**Incomplete or inconsistent documentation:**
The most common refusal cause. Check that your application form matches your supporting documents exactly — dates, names (spelled consistently), and addresses.

**Vague itinerary:**
The application asks for your itinerary in China. "Travelling around China" is not sufficient. List specific cities, dates, and at least some specific sites. You don't need to be rigid about it, but a plausible itinerary helps.

**Previous overstay or visa violation:**
If you've overstayed a Chinese visa in the past or had an entry denied, this will affect your current application. Disclose it honestly.

**Insufficient funds:**
If your bank statement shows insufficient balance for the intended stay, provide an explanation letter and additional financial documentation.

**Missing flight or hotel confirmation:**
The outbound flight and first hotel booking are essential. Don't submit without them.

## After You Receive Your Visa

**Check the visa details carefully:**
- Nationality spelling (matches passport?)
- Entry type (single/double/multiple)
- Validity dates (the dates within which you must enter)
- Duration of stay (the number of days you can stay per entry — often 30 or 90 days)

**The validity period vs. duration of stay:** A visa valid December 1, 2026 to December 1, 2027 with 60-day duration allows you to enter any time during that year, staying up to 60 days per entry. The duration of stay is separate from the validity period.

**China's registration requirement:** When you check in to any accommodation in China, the hotel registers your passport details with the local Public Security Bureau. This is standard procedure; your hotel handles it. If you stay with friends, they must register you at the local PSB within 24 hours.
