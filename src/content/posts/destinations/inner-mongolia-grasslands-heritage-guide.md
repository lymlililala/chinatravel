Imagine endless green waves rolling under a boundless blue sky, dotted with white yurts and grazing herds. This is Inner Mongolia (内蒙古, Nèi Měnggǔ) in summer — a land where nomadic traditions meet ancient history. From the pristine grasslands of Xilingol League to the Buddhist temples and ancient cities of Hohhot, this guide helps you experience the best of the steppe and its cultural treasures.

## Table of contents

## Best Time to Visit

The ideal time to explore Inner Mongolia's grasslands is from **June to September**, when the weather is warm and the grass is lush. July and August offer the greenest landscapes and the most cultural festivals, such as the **Nadam Fair** (traditional games including horse racing, wrestling, and archery). However, this is also peak tourist season, so expect higher prices and more crowds. May and September are quieter with pleasant temperatures, though the grass may not be as vibrant.

## Top Destinations

### Xilingol Grasslands (锡林郭勒草原, Xīlín Guōlè Cǎoyuán)

Xilingol League is home to some of Inner Mongolia's most pristine grasslands, including the **West Ujimqin Banner** (西乌珠穆沁旗, Xī Wūzhūmùqìn Qí), often called "Heavenly Grassland." Here you can experience authentic nomadic culture: stay in a traditional yurt (蒙古包, měnggǔ bāo), ride horses across the steppe, and watch herds of sheep and cattle move like clouds across the horizon. The area encompasses nine types of grassland ecosystems, offering diverse scenery.

### Hohhot (呼和浩特, Hūhéhàotè)

As the capital of Inner Mongolia, Hohhot blends urban convenience with rich history. The city boasts over a dozen national heritage sites (国保单位, guó bǎo dānwèi), including ancient temples, a princess's mansion, and remnants of the Great Wall. It's the perfect base for exploring both cultural and natural attractions.

## Top Sights in Hohhot

### Dazhao Temple (大召, Dà Zhāo)

Dazhao is the largest and most famous Tibetan Buddhist temple in Hohhot. Built in 1580, it features stunning murals, intricate carvings, and a giant silver Buddha statue. The temple is lively with pilgrims and tourists. **Admission:** around ¥35.

### Xilituzhao Temple (席力图召, Xílìtú Zhāo)

Just across the street from Dazhao, this quieter temple is known for its beautiful white pagoda (白塔, bái tǎ). **Admission:** about ¥25. It's a great alternative if you prefer fewer crowds.

### Wusutu Zhao (乌素图召, Wūsùtú Zhāo) & Zhao Great Wall

Located on the outskirts, this temple complex is less visited and offers a peaceful atmosphere. Don't miss the ancient bodhi tree and the remains of the Zhao Great Wall (赵长城, Zhào Chángchéng) behind the new white pagoda. Accessible by taxi from the city center.

### Five Pagoda Temple (五塔寺, Wǔ Tǎ Sì)

A hidden gem with exquisite pagodas and a small museum. It's free to enter and usually uncrowded, making it a pleasant stop.

### Zhaojun Tomb (昭君墓, Zhāojūn Mù)

Now part of the Zhaojun Museum, this site commemorates Wang Zhaojun, a Han dynasty lady who married a Xiongnu chieftain to promote peace. The museum's collection is modest but the story is fascinating.

### Princess's Mansion (公主府, Gōngzhǔ Fǔ)

Claimed to be the only well-preserved princess's mansion in China, this site offers insights into Qing dynasty royal life and the marriage alliances between the Manchu and Mongol nobility.

### General's Government Office (将军衙署, Jiāngjūn Yáshǔ)

This was the administrative center of the Qing and early Republic for southern Mongolia. The recently opened basement from the Republican era is worth exploring.

## Suggested Itinerary

### Day 1: Hohhot City Heritage
- Morning: Visit Dazhao Temple and Xilituzhao Temple (walking distance).
- Lunch: Try local lamb dishes near the temples.
- Afternoon: Explore Five Pagoda Temple, then cycle to the Catholic Church and the Great Mosque (清真大寺, Qīngzhēn Dà Sì). End at the nearby Kuan Xiangzi (宽巷子) food street for dinner.

### Day 2: Suburban Heritage
- Morning: Take metro to Batan station, then taxi to White Pagoda (白塔, Bái Tǎ) and Fengzhou Ancient City ruins. Visit the small museum there.
- Afternoon: Visit Zhaojun Tomb and museum.
- Evening: Return to city for dinner.

### Day 3: More City Sights
- Morning: Visit Princess's Mansion and General's Government Office.
- Afternoon: Cycle to the remaining Suiyuan City Wall (绥远城墙, Suíyuǎn Chéngqiáng), then visit the Inner Mongolia Museum (内蒙古博物院, Nèi Měnggǔ Bówùyuàn) — excellent exhibits on natural history and ethnic culture.

### Day 4-5: Xilingol Grasslands
- Travel from Hohhot to Xilingol (bus or train to Xilinhot, then local transport to grasslands).
- Stay in a yurt, ride horses, hike, and enjoy traditional Mongolian cuisine like roasted lamb and dairy products.
- Attend a Nadam festival if timing permits.

## Getting There & Around

- **To Hohhot:** Hohhot Baita International Airport (呼和浩特白塔国际机场) has flights from major Chinese cities and some international connections. High-speed trains from Beijing take about 2.5 hours.
- **To Xilingol:** Fly from Hohhot to Xilinhot Airport (锡林浩特机场) in 1 hour, or take a bus (about 6-8 hours).
- **Getting around:** In Hohhot, use the metro (Alipay accepted), taxis (metered), or shared bikes. For suburban sites, taxis are convenient but can be hard to find at remote spots like White Pagoda — consider hiring a driver for the day. For the grasslands, join a tour or hire a local guide with a vehicle.

## Where to Stay

- **Hohhot:** Choose a hotel near the city center (e.g., around Zhongshan Road) for easy access to temples and restaurants. International chains like Holiday Inn are available.
- **Grasslands:** Stay in a traditional yurt camp for an authentic experience. Many camps offer meals and activities. Book ahead in summer.

## Food

Inner Mongolian cuisine is meat and dairy-heavy. Must-tries include:
- **Roasted whole lamb** (烤全羊, kǎo quán yáng)
- **Milk tea** (奶茶, nǎi chá) — salty, not sweet
- **Hand-grabbed lamb** (手把肉, shǒu bǎ ròu)
- **Shaomai** (烧麦, shāomài) — lamb dumplings, a Hohhot specialty
- **Dairy products** like dried curds and yogurt

## Practical Tips

- **Visa:** Most foreign visitors need a visa to enter China. The 144-hour transit visa may apply if you are passing through certain cities. Check with your local Chinese embassy.
- **Mobile Payment:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are widely accepted. Set them up before your trip and link an international credit card.
- **Language:** English is not widely spoken outside tourist areas. Download a translation app like Pleco or Google Translate.
- **Etiquette:** When visiting temples, dress modestly, remove hats, and avoid pointing at statues. In yurts, do not step on the threshold.
- **Sun Protection:** The sun is strong on the grasslands. Bring sunscreen, a hat, and sunglasses.
- **Connectivity:** Buy a local SIM card at the airport or use a portable Wi-Fi device.

## Costs

- **Accommodation:** Budget hotels in Hohhot from ¥150/night; yurt camps from ¥300/night (including meals).
- **Food:** Street meals ¥20-50; restaurant dinners ¥80-150 per person.
- **Tickets:** Most temples ¥25-35; some sites free.
- **Transport:** Metro ¥2-6; taxi within city ¥10-30; bus to grasslands ¥100-200.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need to book tickets in advance for Hohhot's heritage sites?**
A: For most sites, you can buy tickets on the spot. During holidays, booking via WeChat mini-programs is recommended.

**Q: Can I use my international driver's license to drive in Inner Mongolia?**
A: No, China does not recognize international driver's licenses. You can hire a car with a driver or use public transport.

**Q: Is it safe to drink tap water?**
A: No, drink bottled or boiled water. Yurt camps usually provide bottled water.

**Q: What should I wear on the grasslands?**
A: Layers are key. Mornings and evenings can be cool even in summer. Bring a light jacket, long pants, and comfortable walking shoes.

**Q: Are there ATMs in the grasslands?**
A: Limited. Bring enough cash for remote areas, though mobile payment is common in towns.
