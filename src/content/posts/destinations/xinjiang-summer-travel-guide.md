Xinjiang (新疆, Xīnjiāng) is a vast region that rewards visitors in every season, but summer — roughly June to September — is when it truly shines. The snow melts, revealing emerald grasslands and wildflower carpets; seasonal roads like the legendary Duku Highway reopen; and the region's famous fruits reach peak sweetness. For international travelers, summer offers the most diverse landscapes, the richest cultural experiences, and the best weather for exploration. This guide covers everything you need to know: top sights, how to get around, where to stay, what to eat, and practical tips for a smooth trip.

## Top Summer Sights in Xinjiang

Xinjiang's summer landscapes are a feast for the eyes. From vast grasslands to flower-filled valleys, here are the must-see natural wonders.

### Grasslands: The Green Carpet Under Snowy Peaks

Xinjiang's grasslands are not flat plains but three-dimensional worlds where snow-capped mountains, forests, rivers, and gorges converge. The high altitude keeps them cool even in July, making them perfect summer retreats.

- **Nalati Grassland (那拉提草原, Nàlātí Cǎoyuán)**: One of the most famous alpine meadows, located in the Ili Kazakh Autonomous Prefecture. Rolling hills dotted with yurts and grazing livestock, with the Tianshan Mountains as a backdrop. Best visited June–August.
- **Karakul Lake (喀拉库勒湖, Kālākùlè Hú)**: Actually a lake, but the surrounding grasslands and pastures are stunning. Located on the Karakoram Highway, it offers views of Muztagh Ata peak.
- **Bayinbuluke Grassland (巴音布鲁克草原, Bāyīnbùlǔkè Cǎoyuán)**: Famous for its "Nine Bends of the Sun" — a river that meanders in nine curves, creating a spectacular sunset reflection. It's also home to the endangered swan species.
- **Xiate Ancient Trail (夏塔古道, Xiàtǎ Gǔdào)**: A historic pass through the Tianshan Mountains, offering a mix of grassland, forest, and glacier views. The trail is open only in summer.

### Flower Seas: Purple and Gold

Summer paints Xinjiang in vibrant colors. Two flower spectacles stand out:

- **Lavender Fields of Huocheng (霍城薰衣草, Huòchéng Xūnyīcǎo)**: Ili Valley is one of the world's three largest lavender producers, alongside Provence and Hokkaido. The purple blooms peak from mid-June to early July, with a smaller second bloom in August–September. The best place is **Lucaogou Town (芦草沟镇)** in Huocheng County.
- **Canola Flowers of Zhaosu (昭苏油菜花, Zhāosū Yóucàihuā)**: Zhaosu County is known as the "Hometown of Canola in China." The golden flowers bloom from July to August, creating a stunning contrast with the snow-capped Tianshan peaks.

![A vast field of purple lavender stretching to the horizon, with distant mountains](https://images.pexels.com/photos/4242688/pexels-photo-4242688.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

### Forests: A Cool Escape

Xinjiang is home to some of China's most pristine forests. In summer, they offer a refreshing respite from the heat.

- **Kuerdening (库尔德宁, Kù'ěrdéníng)**: Located in the Ili Valley, this is the world's most concentrated and best-preserved area of Schrenk's spruce (Picea schrenkiana). The valley is a UNESCO World Heritage site.
- **Kanas Lake (喀纳斯湖, Kānàsī Hú)**: Surrounded by dense taiga forests, this alpine lake is famous for its turquoise water and mysterious "lake monster" legends. The forests are a mix of Siberian larch, spruce, and birch.

## Seasonal Roads: Summer-Only Adventures

![Renwenjingguan, Xinjiang](/img/destinations/xinjiang-summer-travel-guide/1.webp)

Some of Xinjiang's most spectacular roads are only open for a few months each year, making summer the only time to experience them.

### Duku Highway (独库公路, Dúkù Gōnglù)

This 561-km road traverses the Tianshan Mountains from north to south, connecting Dushanzi (独山子) in the north to Kuche (库车) in the south. It typically opens in June and closes in October, depending on snow conditions. Along the way, you'll see snow-capped peaks, alpine meadows, deep gorges, and red sandstone cliffs. The road crosses four passes above 3,000 meters, offering a journey from northern to southern Xinjiang in a single day.

![A winding road cutting through a dramatic red-rock gorge, with a river below](https://images.pexels.com/photos/36157561/pexels-photo-36157561.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

### Yizhao Highway (伊昭公路, Yīzhāo Gōnglù)

Known as "Little Duku," this 122-km road connects Yining (伊宁) in the Ili Valley to Zhaosu. It crosses the Wusun Mountain at over 3,000 meters and is open only from June to September. The scenery includes lavender fields, alpine meadows, and dramatic weather changes — you might experience sun, rain, and hail in one day.

## Cultural Heritage: Silk Road Relics

Summer is also an excellent time to explore Xinjiang's rich history. The dry climate makes ancient sites accessible.

- **Jiaohe Ruins (交河故城, Jiāohé Gùchéng)**: A UNESCO World Heritage site near Turpan, this 2,000-year-old city was carved from a plateau of clay. It was an important stop on the Silk Road.
- **Kizil Thousand Buddha Caves (克孜尔千佛洞, Kèzī'ěr Qiānfódòng)**: Near Kuche, these are the oldest Buddhist cave temples in China, dating from the 3rd to 9th centuries. The murals show influences from India, Persia, and China.
- **Bezeklik Thousand Buddha Caves (柏孜克里克千佛洞, Bǎizīkèlǐkè Qiānfódòng)**: Located in the Flaming Mountains near Turpan, these caves feature exquisite murals from the 6th to 14th centuries.

## Practical Information for International Travelers

![Winter Beijiang Snow Scenery](/img/destinations/xinjiang-summer-travel-guide/2.webp)

### Best Time to Visit

- **June to September**: Ideal weather for most regions. Grasslands are green, flowers are in bloom, and seasonal roads are open.
- **July to August**: Peak season for lavender and canola flowers, but also the hottest and busiest. Expect crowds at major attractions.
- **September**: Harvest season for fruits, with cooler temperatures and fewer tourists.

### Getting There & Around

- **By Air**: Xinjiang has major international airports in Urumqi (URC), Kashgar (KHG), and Yining (YIN). Direct flights from Beijing, Shanghai, and other Chinese cities are frequent. International flights connect from Central Asia, Southeast Asia, and the Middle East.
- **By Train**: High-speed rail connects Urumqi to Beijing (about 12 hours), Shanghai (about 16 hours), and other cities. The Lanzhou-Xinjiang High-Speed Railway is a scenic route.
- **Within Xinjiang**: Domestic flights, trains, and long-distance buses connect major cities. For remote areas like Kanas, consider renting a car with a driver (available through hotels or travel agencies).

### Tickets & Booking

- **Major attractions**: Tickets can be bought online via platforms like Trip.com or at the gate. Some sites require advance booking (e.g., Kanas Lake, Kizil Caves).
- **Seasonal roads**: No tickets required, but check local announcements for opening dates. The Duku Highway may have traffic restrictions on weekends.

### Mobile Payment & Money

- **Alipay and WeChat Pay** are widely accepted in cities and tourist areas. Set up your account before arrival.
- **Cash** is still needed in remote villages and for small vendors. ATMs are available in cities.
- **Credit cards** are accepted at upscale hotels and some restaurants, but not everywhere.

### Visa & 144-Hour Transit

- Most international travelers need a visa to enter China. Check with your local Chinese embassy.
- **144-hour visa-free transit** is available at Urumqi Diwopu International Airport for citizens of certain countries. You must have a confirmed onward ticket to a third country.

### Etiquette & Tips

- **Dress modestly** in rural and religious areas. Women may want to cover their heads in mosques.
- **Respect local customs**: In Kazakh and Uyghur areas, remove shoes before entering homes. Avoid pointing your feet at people.
- **Photography**: Always ask permission before taking photos of locals. Some religious sites prohibit photography inside.
- **Language**: Mandarin is widely spoken, but English is limited. Download a translation app like Pleco or Google Translate.

## Where to Stay

- **Urumqi**: Offers the widest range of accommodations, from international chains (Hilton, Marriott) to budget hostels. The city center near Hongshan Park is convenient.
- **Ili Valley (Yining)**: Boutique hotels and guesthouses are available. For a unique experience, stay in a yurt on the grasslands.
- **Turpan**: Traditional Uyghur-style guesthouses with courtyards are popular. Many offer local cuisine and grape-picking experiences.
- **Kanas**: Lodges near the lake are expensive but offer stunning views. Book well in advance.

## Food: Summer's Bounty

Summer is the season for Xinjiang's famous fruits and street food.

### Fruits to Try

- **Hami melon (哈密瓜, Hāmì Guā)**: Sweet and juicy, available from June.
- **Turpan grapes (吐鲁番葡萄, Tǔlǔfān Pútao)**: Seedless and incredibly sweet, harvested from July.
- **Fig (无花果, Wúhuāguǒ)**: From Atush (阿图什), known as the "Fig Town." Eat them fresh in July–August.
- **Apricot (吊干杏, Diàogān Xìng)**: Dried on the tree, these are intensely sweet. Available in July.

### Night Markets

- **Hetian Night Market (和田夜市, Hétián Yèshì)**: Famous for lamb skewers (kawap), naan bread, and grilled fish. Open until late.
- **Urumqi Grand Bazaar (乌鲁木齐国际大巴扎, Wūlǔmùqí Guójì Dàbāzhā)**: A lively market with food stalls, souvenirs, and performances.

## Suggested Itinerary: 7 Days in Summer

**Day 1-2: Urumqi**
- Visit Xinjiang Museum (see the "Loulan Beauty" mummy).
- Explore the Grand Bazaar for souvenirs and street food.

**Day 3-4: Turpan**
- See Jiaohe Ruins, Bezeklik Caves, and the Flaming Mountains.
- Visit a local vineyard for grape tasting.

**Day 5-6: Ili Valley**
- Drive the Yizhao Highway (if open).
- Explore Nalati Grassland and the lavender fields of Huocheng.

**Day 7: Return to Urumqi**
- Fly out from Urumqi.

## Costs

- **Budget**: ¥300–500/day (hostels, street food, public transport).
- **Mid-range**: ¥800–1,200/day (3-star hotels, private car, nicer restaurants).
- **Luxury**: ¥1,500+/day (5-star hotels, guided tours, fine dining).

Major expenses: flights (¥1,000–3,000 from Beijing/Shanghai), accommodation (¥200–800/night), entrance fees (¥50–200 per site).

## Conclusion

Summer in Xinjiang is a sensory overload in the best way: the smell of lavender, the taste of sun-warmed grapes, the sight of endless green pastures, and the sound of a bazaar at dusk. Whether you're a nature lover, history buff, or foodie, this season delivers an unforgettable experience. Plan ahead, respect local customs, and prepare for a journey that will stay with you long after you leave.
