# China Visa Run & Border Crossing Guide 2026

A **visa run** — leaving China and re-entering on a new visa, or to reset a multiple-entry visa's validity — is a common reality for long-term visitors and expats. China's border infrastructure makes several convenient exit-and-entry options available, though the procedures have changed significantly in recent years.

---

## The Classic Routes

### Hong Kong (Port of Entry: Lo Wu / Lok Ma Chau / Shenzhen Bay)

Hong Kong is the most popular and convenient exit point for Shenzhen-based visitors and those in the Pearl River Delta.

**Three crossing points (Shenzhen–Hong Kong):**
- **Lo Wu (罗湖/罗虎):** The original and busiest crossing; connected by MTR on the Hong Kong side
- **Lok Ma Chau (落马洲):** Slightly less crowded; accessible from Hong Kong West Kowloon area
- **Shenzhen Bay (深圳湾):** Road crossing; good for those with vehicles or the Shenzhen Bay area

**Procedure:** Exit China through Shenzhen immigration; enter Hong Kong SAR; collect your passport exit stamp; do whatever you need in Hong Kong; re-enter China.

**What counts as leaving China:** Hong Kong and Macau are Special Administrative Regions — crossing into either resets standard tourist visa counts and is sufficient to trigger the "leaving China" effect for single-entry visas and visa-free stays. You are technically leaving "Mainland China" but not leaving Chinese sovereignty.

**Time needed for basic visa run:** Minimum 4–6 hours in Hong Kong; overnight gives you more flexibility and a pleasant Hong Kong night.

### Macau (Port of Entry: Cotai / Gongbei)

**Gongbei Border Gate (拱北口岸):** The primary crossing between Zhuhai and Macau; extremely busy; 24-hour crossing.

**Procedure:** Very similar to Hong Kong crossing. Macau is even more condensed — you can cross into Macau, have a coffee, and return within 2 hours if necessary. The Macau casino strip is the obvious extended stay option.

### Vietnam (Hekou–Lao Cai / Dongxing–Móng Cái)

For visitors in Yunnan or Guangxi, **land border crossings into Vietnam** offer a visa run with genuine travel experience attached.

**Hekou–Lao Cai (红河–老街):** The main Yunnan–Vietnam crossing; connects Hekou (Yunnan) to Lao Cai (Vietnam). A narrow-gauge railway once operated here; now road only. Lao Cai is the gateway to Sapa.

**Dongxing–Móng Cái (东兴–芒街):** The Guangxi crossing; connects Dongxing (Guangxi) to Móng Cái (Vietnam); a busy commercial crossing with less tourist infrastructure.

**Procedure:** Exit China with your Chinese visa/stamp; cross into Vietnam (a Vietnam e-visa is required for most nationalities); stay in Vietnam as desired; return to China.

### Kazakhstan (Alashankou / Khorgos)

For visitors in Xinjiang, **crossing into Kazakhstan** at **Khorgos (霍尔果斯)** or **Alashankou (阿拉山口)** provides a Central Asian visa run option with a genuinely different travel experience.

**Khorgos International Center:** A unique duty-free trade zone straddling the border; accessible without a Kazakhstan visa for shopping visits.

## The 144-Hour and 72-Hour Transit Programs

China offers **visa-free transit** for nationals of many countries passing through designated international airports:

**144-hour (6-day) transit:** Available at Beijing, Shanghai, Chengdu, Guangzhou, Wuhan, Zhengzhou, Shenyang, and several other major airports. Allows travel within a designated geographic area (not the entire country) during the transit period.

**72-hour transit:** Available at more airports; more restricted geographic area.

**How to use it:** On arrival at a designated airport, declare that you're using the transit program; the immigration officer stamps a transit entry valid for 72 or 144 hours. No visa required.

**What you can do:** Travel within the designated area (varies by city); exit through any designated airport within the time limit. You cannot change your exit airport for certain city programs.

**Who is eligible:** Citizens of approximately 50+ countries including most Western nations; check the current list as it is frequently updated.

## Current Visa Policy (2025–2026)

China has expanded **unilateral visa-free access** for multiple countries since late 2023:

**Visa-free 15–30 days (recent additions):** Many European countries, Australia, New Zealand, Canada, and several Southeast Asian nations now have 15–30 day visa-free access for tourism. The policy continues to expand.

**Check before you travel:** The visa-free policy list and terms (single entry, multiple entry, duration) change frequently. Always verify current requirements through your country's embassy or the official Chinese government immigration website before travel.

## Practical Notes for All Border Crossings

**Document check:** Carry your passport (with adequate blank pages), sufficient funds evidence (bank statement or credit card), return or onward travel booking, and your accommodation reservation.

**Health declaration:** Some border points may still require health documentation from the COVID-era protocols; check current requirements.

**Peak crossing times:** Chinese national holidays create extreme congestion at all crossing points; wait times of 2–5 hours are common at Hong Kong border crossings during Golden Week.

*Visa logistics are a less glamorous aspect of China travel but a genuinely significant one for anyone staying more than a short trip — understanding the exit options reduces anxiety and opens up travel patterns that would otherwise require more complex planning.*
