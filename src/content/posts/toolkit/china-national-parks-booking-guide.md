## Table of contents

China's approach to protected natural areas underwent a significant restructuring beginning in 2015 and formally enacted in 2021, when the country established its first five officially designated National Parks — a new tier above the existing scenic area (风景名胜区), nature reserve (自然保护区) and geological park (地质公园) system.

For visitors, the practical implication is that the most significant natural areas now operate with stricter daily visitor limits, advance booking requirements and conservation-focused management.

## China's First Five National Parks (2021)

### 1. Sanjiangyuan National Park (三江源国家公园)
**Location:** Southern Qinghai Province
**Area:** 190,700 sq km (largest in China)
**Why it matters:** The source region of the Yangtze, Yellow and Mekong rivers; one of the world's highest and largest plateau wetland ecosystems

**Visitor access:** Core zones are restricted; limited access via approved tour operators. Buffer zones around the source areas accessible with guides.

**Booking:** Through the Sanjiangyuan National Park administration WeChat account; limited approved tours.

### 2. Giant Panda National Park (大熊猫国家公园)
**Location:** Sichuan, Shaanxi and Gansu provinces
**Area:** 27,134 sq km
**Why it matters:** Covers the primary habitat of China's approximately 1,900 wild giant pandas; critical wildlife corridor between previously fragmented habitat patches

**Visitor access:** The park has several visitor zones including established tourist areas (Bifengxia, Wolong) within the broader park territory. Wild panda tracking experiences (very limited; ¥2,000–¥5,000 per person) available through the Wolong base.

**Key visitor centers:**
- Wolong Shenshuping Panda Center (卧龙神树坪熊猫中心): Chengdu-based giant panda facility within the national park
- Ya'an Bifengxia Base: See the Ya'an destination guide

### 3. Northeast Tiger and Leopard National Park (东北虎豹国家公园)
**Location:** Jilin and Heilongjiang provinces, on the Russian border
**Area:** 14,612 sq km
**Why it matters:** Primary recovery zone for Amur (Siberian) tigers and Amur leopards; critically endangered species recovering thanks to strict protection

**Visitor access:** Very limited. A small visitor center near Hunchun (珲春) provides information. No general public access to core zones; a small number of approved wildlife watching experiences available.

### 4. Hainan Tropical Rainforest National Park (海南热带雨林国家公园)
**Location:** Central Hainan Island
**Area:** 4,269 sq km
**Why it matters:** Last significant primary tropical rainforest in China; home to the Hainan gibbon (possibly the world's rarest primate, under 40 individuals)

**Visitor access:** Buffer zone accessible via several approved scenic areas. Yingge Ridge (鹦哥岭), Jianzifeng and Bawangling areas open for hiking. Core zone restricted.

**Booking:** Day hike permits for specific trails through the official park WeChat accounts.

### 5. Wuyishan National Park (武夷山国家公园)
**Location:** Northern Fujian Province
**Area:** 1,001 sq km
**Why it matters:** Double UNESCO World Heritage Site; exceptional biodiversity and cultural landscape

**Visitor access:** The main scenic area with Nine-Bend Stream rafting and Tianyou Peak is well-established tourist infrastructure. A larger buffer zone of primary forest has restricted access.

**Booking:** Day tickets through the official scenic area booking system; online advance booking required.

### High-Traffic UNESCO Natural Sites and Booking Requirements

**Jiuzhaigou (九寨沟):**
- Daily visitor limit: 20,000 (revised upward from 12,000 post-reconstruction)
- Advance booking: Mandatory; through the official 九寨沟景区 WeChat Mini Program
- Tickets: ¥169 April–November; ¥70 November–March
- Best booking lead time: 2–4 weeks ahead for peak season

**Zhangjiajie (张家界):**
- Daily visitor limit: 40,000 for the main scenic area
- Advance booking: Strongly recommended during holidays; online booking available
- Tickets: ¥245 for 4-day pass
- Best booking lead time: 1–2 weeks ahead; 4–6 weeks for Golden Week

**Huangshan (黄山):**
- Daily limit: 20,000 visitors
- Advance booking: Mandatory in peak season; through 黄山风景区 WeChat Mini Program
- Tickets: ¥190 April–November; ¥115 December–March
- Best lead time: 2–3 weeks ahead for peak periods

**Wuyishan:**
- Nine-Bend Stream raft requires advance booking particularly April–May and October
- Online booking through 武夷山景区 official account

## General Booking Process for Major Natural Sites

Most major Chinese scenic areas now operate through WeChat Mini Programs for advance ticket booking. The process:

1. Search for the scenic area name + "景区" in WeChat Mini Program search
2. Select your visit date
3. Select ticket type (adult, child, student, senior)
4. Enter passport/ID information
5. Pay via WeChat Pay (international cards have limited compatibility; use Alipay as alternative)
6. Receive QR code for admission

**Alternative booking platforms:**
- **Ctrip (携程):** English interface available; covers most major scenic areas. Slight convenience markup over direct booking.
- **Mafengwo (马蜂窝):** Good for combination packages
- **Official scenic area websites:** Most have English sections for major international sites

## Daily Visitor Limits and What They Mean

China's major scenic areas operate hard daily caps that prevent overselling. When the day is "full":
- The WeChat booking system simply shows no available tickets for that date
- Walk-up visitors at the gate are turned away
- No amount of negotiation changes this

**Practical implication:** For popular natural sites during peak season, booking 2–4 weeks ahead is not excessive. For Golden Week (October 1–7), 4–6 weeks ahead is prudent.

## Conservation Fees

Many protected areas include a conservation fee in their admission price or charge it separately. These fees (typically ¥5–¥50) are legitimate contributions to protected area management. Accept them as part of the cost of visiting.

The Giant Panda National Park "wild panda tracking" experience (¥2,000–¥5,000) directly funds anti-poaching patrols and habitat corridor restoration. If you have the budget and the opportunity, this is one of the most impactful wildlife tourism experiences available in Asia.

## Practical Tips

- **Download the official scenic area WeChat Mini Program** for each site before your visit date; this is the most reliable booking channel
- **Book accommodation in the area simultaneously** with site tickets; peak season accommodation near Jiuzhaigou, Huangshan and Zhangjiajie fills as quickly as the tickets
- **Check seasonal closures:** Several sites close or reduce services in winter (November–March) for ecological management. Jiuzhaigou's reconstruction affected opening for years; verify current status before booking.
- **Weather volatility:** China's mountain scenic areas can have dramatic weather changes. Check forecasts the morning of your visit; cloud can render a summit experience disappointing but often creates extraordinary photography.

## Final Word

China's national and protected scenic areas represent some of the most spectacular natural environments on earth — from the sandstone pillars of Zhangjiajie to the colorful mineral pools of Jiuzhaigou, from the Tibetan plateau wilderness of Sanjiangyuan to the tropical rainforests of Hainan. The booking systems, while requiring extra preparation, ensure that the visitor experience remains manageable at these extraordinary places. Plan ahead, book early, and the access is straightforward.
