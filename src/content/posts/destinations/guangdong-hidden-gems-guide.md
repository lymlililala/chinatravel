Guangdong is often associated with Guangzhou's dim sum and Shenzhen's skyscrapers, but venture beyond the usual tourist trail and you'll discover a province rich in hidden gems. From the 'Little Hong Kong' ceramic village of Shikan in the north to the culinary powerhouse of Jiangmen, these off-the-beaten-path destinations offer authentic experiences that most visitors miss. This guide takes you into the heart of rural Guangdong, where ancient kilns still burn, and where food tells stories of migration and tradition.

## Table of contents

## Shikan: Guangdong's Ceramic Village

Nestled in the mountains of Qingyuan (清远, Qīngyuǎn), about an hour's drive north of Guangzhou, lies the forgotten ceramic village of Shikan (石坎, Shíkǎn). Once known as 'Little Hong Kong' during its heyday in the 1980s, this sleepy town was a thriving hub of porcelain production, with nearly 300 dragon kilns lining the riverbanks. Today, thanks to the efforts of local artisans like Mai Zi, the village is experiencing a revival.

![A traditional dragon kiln nestled in green hills, with smoke rising from its chimney](https://images.pexels.com/photos/37932130/pexels-photo-37932130.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

### A History of Fire and Clay

Shikan's pottery history spans over 300 years. During the Qing dynasty, its stoneware was so renowned that it rivaled Jingdezhen's famous porcelain, earning the saying: 'Jingdezhen fine porcelain, Qingyuan earthen bowls.' At its peak in the 1980s, the state-owned Qingyuan Porcelain Factory employed hundreds and made millionaires of some locals. But as industrial ceramics declined, the kilns fell silent, and the craft was nearly lost.

### The Revival

Mai Zi, a third-generation pottery descendant, returned from Shenzhen in 2023 to revive her hometown's heritage. She and her mother documented elderly craftsmen, applied for provincial heritage status, and transformed abandoned kilns into a cultural hub. Today, visitors can explore the 'Tongxin Kiln' (同心窑) and other restored dragon kilns, now part of the Guangdong Industrial Heritage list. The site offers hands-on pottery workshops where you can try your hand at throwing clay on a wheel.

![A visitor shaping clay on a pottery wheel, with finished ceramic bowls in the background](https://images.pexels.com/photos/31875677/pexels-photo-31875677.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

### Getting There & Around

Shikan is in Longjing Town (龙颈镇, Lóngjǐng Zhèn), Qingxin District (清新区, Qīngxīn Qū). From Guangzhou, take a high-speed train to Qingyuan Station (about 30 minutes), then a taxi or bus (40 minutes). Driving is easiest if you have a rental car; the X370 road winds through scenic hills. Public buses are infrequent, so consider hiring a driver for the day.

### Practical Tips

- **Best time to visit**: October to April, when weather is cool and dry. Summers are hot and humid.
- **Tickets**: The pottery workshop costs around ¥100-200 per person; call ahead to book.
- **Mobile payment**: Alipay and WeChat Pay are widely accepted in Qingyuan, but bring some cash for small vendors in Shikan.
- **Language**: Mandarin is spoken, but Cantonese is common. Few locals speak English, so have a translation app ready.

## Jiangmen: The Culinary Capital of the Pearl River Delta

Two hours southwest of Guangzhou, Jiangmen (江门, Jiāngmén) is a sprawling city of five districts and four county-level cities, often overlooked by tourists. Yet this 'first overseas Chinese hometown' is a food lover's paradise, with a cuisine shaped by its coastal location and diaspora influences.

### Taishan: Rice and Seafood Heaven

Taishan (台山, Táishān), a coastal county-level city under Jiangmen, is known as the 'Land of Fish and Rice.' Its signature dish is **Taishan eel rice** (台山黄鳝饭, Táishān huángshàn fàn). Fresh local eels are steamed, deboned, and stir-fried with shredded meat, then cooked with premium rice in a claypot. The result is a fragrant, oily rice with crispy burnt bottom (guoba).

Another must-try is **Taishan eel rice's younger cousin**, **eel rice** (鳗鱼饭, mányú fàn). Taishan produces over 50,000 tons of eel annually, much of it exported to Japan. The fresh eel is grilled with a sweet soy glaze, yielding tender, flavorful meat.

![A claypot of Taishan eel rice, with eel pieces and green onions on top](https://images.pexels.com/photos/19981737/pexels-photo-19981737.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

### Xinhui: The Kingdom of Chenpi

Xinhui (新会, Xīnhuì) is famous for its aged tangerine peel (陈皮, chénpí), a prized ingredient in Cantonese cuisine. The peel is used in everything from soups to desserts. The most iconic dish is **Gujing roast goose** (古井烧鹅, Gǔjǐng shāo'é). The goose is marinated with Xinhui chenpi, then roasted over lychee wood, giving it a sweet, fragrant flavor.

Other chenpi specialties include **chenpi spare ribs** (陈皮骨, chénpí gǔ) and **chenpi red bean soup** (陈皮红豆沙, chénpí hóngdòu shā). For a unique treat, try **chenpi ice cream** at local shops.

### Other Jiangmen Delicacies

- **Kaiping** (开平, Kāipíng): Known for **Chikan tofu pudding** (赤坎豆腐角, Chìkǎn dòufu jiǎo) and **salted duck eggs**.
- **Heshan** (鹤山, Hèshān): **Gulao fish skin dumplings** (古劳鱼皮角, Gǔláo yúpí jiǎo) are delicate and flavorful.
- **Enping** (恩平, Ēnpíng): Try **lai fun** (濑粉, làifěn) and **bitter herb soup** (簕菜, lècài).

### Getting There & Around

Jiangmen is easily reached by high-speed rail from Guangzhou South Station (50 minutes to Jiangmen East Station). Once there, buses connect to Taishan, Xinhui, and other districts. For food tours, consider hiring a local guide or using ride-hailing apps like Didi.

### Practical Tips

- **Best time to visit**: October to December for cool weather and peak citrus season.
- **Food tours**: Join a local food tour to sample multiple dishes; prices start around ¥300 per person.
- **Visa**: Most travelers need a visa, but those transiting through Guangzhou can use the 144-hour visa-free policy if eligible.

## Suggested Itinerary: 3 Days in Rural Guangdong

### Day 1: Qingyuan – Pottery and Nature

- Morning: Take high-speed train to Qingyuan, then taxi to Shikan. Explore the dragon kilns and try a pottery workshop.
- Afternoon: Visit Fengweiwei Ancient Village (凤塱围, Fèngwéi Wéi), a well-preserved Qing dynasty fortified village.
- Evening: Dinner of Qingyuan chicken (白切鸡, báiqīe jī) at a local restaurant.

### Day 2: Jiangmen – Food Crawl

- Morning: Train to Jiangmen, then bus to Taishan. Lunch at a local restaurant for Taishan eel rice.
- Afternoon: Visit Xinhui's chenpi culture village or a chenpi factory.
- Evening: Dinner in Kaiping's Chikan Ancient Town, famous for roast goose and night scenery.

### Day 3: Nature and Departure

- Morning: Explore the **Kaiping Diaolou** (开平碉楼, Kāipíng diāolóu), UNESCO World Heritage fortified towers.
- Afternoon: Return to Guangzhou or Shenzhen.

## Where to Stay

- **Qingyuan**: Stay at a hot spring resort in Qingxin District for a relaxing soak after pottery.
- **Jiangmen**: Choose a hotel in the city center for easy access to food streets. For a unique experience, stay in a restored Diaolou in Kaiping.

## Best Time to Visit

Guangdong has a subtropical climate. The best months are October to April, avoiding the hot, rainy summer (May-September). Spring (March-April) is pleasant but can be humid.

## Costs

- **Transport**: High-speed train from Guangzhou to Qingyuan: ¥40-60; to Jiangmen: ¥50-80.
- **Food**: Street snacks ¥10-30; a full meal ¥50-100 per person.
- **Activities**: Pottery workshop ¥100-200; Diaolou entrance ¥80.

## Practical Tips for Foreigners

- **Mobile payment**: Set up Alipay or WeChat Pay before your trip. Most shops and restaurants accept them.
- **Language**: Learn a few Cantonese phrases like 'm̀h'gōi' (thank you) and 'néih hóu' (hello).
- **Etiquette**: When dining, don't stick chopsticks upright in a bowl of rice – it resembles incense at funerals.
- **Safety**: Rural Guangdong is safe, but watch for motorbikes on narrow roads.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need a visa to visit Guangdong's rural areas?**
A: Most foreign visitors need a tourist visa (L visa) for mainland China. However, if you're transiting through Guangzhou Baiyun International Airport, you may be eligible for the 144-hour visa-free policy, which allows you to stay in Guangzhou and surrounding areas (including Qingyuan and Jiangmen) for up to 6 days. Check eligibility before travel.

**Q: How do I get around without a car?**
A: High-speed trains connect major cities like Guangzhou, Qingyuan, and Jiangmen. For rural areas like Shikan, taxis or ride-hailing apps (Didi) are best. Buses are available but infrequent. Consider hiring a driver for a day (¥300-500).

**Q: Is English widely spoken in these areas?**
A: English is not commonly spoken in rural Guangdong. Learn basic Mandarin phrases or use a translation app like Google Translate or Pleco. In tourist spots, some staff may speak basic English.

**Q: What's the best way to experience local food?**
A: Join a food tour or visit local markets. In Jiangmen, head to the night market in Xinhui or Taishan for street food. Always ask for 'local specialties' (本地特色, běndì tèsè).

**Q: Can I pay with credit cards?**
A: Credit cards are rarely accepted in rural areas. Use Alipay or WeChat Pay (link a foreign credit card to these apps). Carry some cash (¥200-500) for small vendors.

**Q: Is it safe to travel alone in rural Guangdong?**
A: Yes, it's generally safe. Violent crime is rare, but watch out for pickpocketing in crowded markets. Stick to well-lit areas at night and keep valuables secure.
