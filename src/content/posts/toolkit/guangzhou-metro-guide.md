Guangzhou's metro is extensive, well-organized, and makes navigating this sprawling southern city genuinely manageable. With over 14 lines and 300+ stations, it covers tourist areas, business districts, and the Canton Fair complex. Unusually for a Chinese city, Guangzhou's metro also crosses into an adjacent city — Foshan — seamlessly, without any border formalities.

## Table of contents

## Fares and Payment

Guangzhou Metro uses distance-based pricing:
- **¥2** starting fare for short journeys
- Most tourist-area rides: **¥3–¥7**
- Airport connections (Baiyun): approximately **¥7–¥12** from central Guangzhou

**Alipay** transit code works throughout the system. Open Alipay, find the transit pass feature, select Guangzhou Metro, generate the QR code, and scan at turnstiles.

**WeChat Pay** also has a transit code for Guangzhou Metro.

**T-Union Card** (全国交通一卡通): Physical contactless card available at service windows. Works on Guangzhou Metro and buses, and in 300+ cities across China.

**Guangzhou Metro Card**: City-specific card available from metro service centers. ¥20 deposit plus loaded funds.

**Single journey tokens** from machines at all stations with English interface.

## Line 3 — The Backbone

**Line 3** is the single most important line for visitors to Guangzhou. It runs from Baiyun International Airport in the north through the city center and down to southern Panyu District. On this one line:

- **Baiyun Airport T1 and T2 stations** — direct airport access
- **Tianhe District stations** — Guangzhou's modern business and shopping hub
- **Tianhe Sports Center** — major stadium and shopping area
- **Sports Center (体育西路)** — transfer hub, Zhujiang New Town access
- **Ke Yun Nan** — connecting onward south

This makes Line 3 the first metro line most visitors will use: airport to hotel in Tianhe in about 45 minutes.

**Line 18** (newer, faster) also connects the airport to Zhujiang New Town (Pearl River New Town area) in about 30 minutes — slightly faster than Line 3 for the CBD.

## The Foshan Metro Connection

This is one of Guangzhou metro's most unusual features: **Line 2 and Line 7 extend into Foshan** — an adjacent city with its own identity and culture — without any ticket change or border formality. You board in Guangzhou and arrive in Foshan on the same ticket.

**Line 7** extends into the Foshan area. **Line 2** also has Foshan stations (Foshan Beizhan, Kuiqi Road).

Foshan is worth a visit for its **Ancestral Temple** (Zu Miao), its traditional Cantonese culture, and its famous ceramics district (Shiwan). Because it's a completely seamless metro connection, you can visit Foshan as easily as going across town — no buses, no taxis required.

The Foshan stations are marked on the metro map and the ticket system works the same way.

## Canton Fair Access

The **Canton Import and Export Fair** (广交会) is held at the China Import and Export Fair Complex in Haizhu District, twice a year (Phase 1/2/3 in late April-May and October-November). If you're visiting for the fair — or just curious to see one of the world's largest trade shows — the metro access is good.

**Line 8** has a **Pazhou station** (琶洲) right at the exhibition complex. From the city center (Tianhe or Yuexiu areas), transfer to Line 8 via several interchange stations.

**During fair season**: Trains can be crowded with exhibitors and buyers. Consider arriving early (fair opens around 9am) to avoid the mid-morning crush.

## Tourist Areas by Metro

**Shamian Island** (沙面): This colonial-era island district with European architecture is in Liwan District. Take **Line 1 to Huangsha station** (黄沙) then walk south about 15 minutes, or **Line 6 to Luban Road** station for a shorter walk. Shamian is a genuinely pleasant place to wander — French and British consular buildings, riverside promenades, and cafes.

**Yuexiu Park and Guangzhou Museum**: **Line 2 to Yuexiu Park station** (越秀公园). Yuexiu Park has the Five Rams Statue (Guangzhou's symbol), Ming-dynasty Zhenhai Tower, and the Guangzhou Museum covering the city's 2,000-year history.

**Chen Clan Academy** (陈家祠): **Line 1 to Chenjiaci station** (陈家祠). One of Guangzhou's best-preserved traditional buildings — intricate Lingnan architecture and folk art carvings throughout.

**Liwan Old Quarter and Xiguan Mansions**: Line 1 to Changshou Road (长寿路) or Huangsha. The old merchant neighborhood of Guangzhou, with traditional mansions and excellent dim sum restaurants.

**Beijing Road Pedestrian Area**: **Line 1 or Line 6 to Gongyuanqian station** (公园前). The main shopping and historical street.

## Practical Notes for Canton Fair Season

If you're visiting during Canton Fair (April-May or October-November), the metro will be noticeably busier than usual:
- **Morning peak extends** to 9:30–10am as exhibitors head to Pazhou
- **Line 8 to Pazhou** is heavily loaded during fair hours
- **Hotel options near transit hubs** fill up weeks in advance — book early
- The **Pazhou area has multiple exhibition halls** — know which one you need before you arrive

## Line 6 for Tourism

Line 6 is worth knowing for tourism in the old parts of the city:
- **Haizhuchang** (海珠广场) — historical center, Haizhu Square
- **Luban Road** — near Shamian Island
- **Shipai Bridge area** — restaurant and nightlife

## Operating Hours

Guangzhou Metro operates approximately **6am to midnight**, with some variation. Frequency on major lines during peak hours: every 3–5 minutes. Off-peak: every 6–10 minutes.
