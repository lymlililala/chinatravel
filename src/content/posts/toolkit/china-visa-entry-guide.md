Getting into China has become significantly easier for many nationalities in 2024–2025, thanks to expanded visa-free agreements and a well-established transit exemption scheme. This guide breaks down every option so you can choose the right entry route for your trip.

## Table of contents

## The Tourist Visa (L Visa)

For most travellers, the standard **L visa** (tourist visa) remains the most reliable way to visit China.

### How to apply

1. **Submit your application** at a Chinese embassy or consulate in your home country, or through an authorised visa agency.
2. **Required documents** typically include:
   - Valid passport (at least 6 months of remaining validity, two blank pages)
   - Completed visa application form (downloadable from the embassy website)
   - One recent passport-size photo
   - Round-trip flight itinerary
   - Hotel bookings or a letter of invitation from a host in China
   - Bank statement showing sufficient funds
3. **Processing time**: standard 4–7 business days; express services available at many consulates.
4. **Validity & duration**: a single-entry L visa typically allows a stay of up to 30 or 60 days; double- and multiple-entry options exist.

> **Tip:** Check the specific requirements of your nearest Chinese consulate — they can vary by country.

## The 144-Hour Transit Visa-Free Policy

If you are transiting through certain Chinese cities on your way to a third country, you may be able to enter visa-free for **up to 144 hours (6 days)** without any visa.

### Eligible ports of entry (as of 2025)

The 144-hour policy is available at a growing list of cities, including:

- **Beijing** (Capital International Airport & Daxing Airport)
- **Shanghai** (Pudong & Hongqiao Airports)
- **Guangzhou** (Baiyun Airport)
- **Chengdu** (Tianfu Airport)
- **Xi'an** (Xianyang Airport)
- **Hangzhou, Nanjing, Wuhan, Kunming, Harbin, Qingdao, Dalian, Shenyang** and more

*Always verify the current list on the official National Immigration Administration website before travelling.*

### Key conditions

| Condition | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Nationality | Most countries with diplomatic relations with China are eligible |
| Third-country requirement | You must hold a confirmed onward ticket to a **third country** (not the country you just flew from) |
| Movement | You must stay within the **designated visa-free zone** of the city (usually the entire municipality) |
| Re-entry | You cannot re-enter on the same exemption immediately after exiting |

### How to use it

1. Arrive in China on an international flight.
2. At immigration, go to the **transit lane** and present your onward ticket and passport.
3. Receive a stamp valid for up to 144 hours from midnight of the day of entry.
4. Depart to your third destination before the stamp expires.

## The 15-Day Visa-Free Bilateral Agreements

Since late 2023, China has signed 15-day (or longer) **mutual visa-free** agreements with a rapidly growing list of countries. As of mid-2025, nationals of the following countries can enter China visa-free for leisure or business stays of up to **15 days**:

- France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Netherlands, Switzerland, Ireland, Hungary, Austria, Belgium, Luxembourg (EU)
- Malaysia, Thailand, Singapore, Brunei (South-East Asia)
- Australia (pilot programme)
- *…and more being added regularly*

> **Check the latest list:** The National Immigration Administration of China publishes updated information at **www.nia.gov.cn** (Chinese; use a translation app).

### Conditions

- Passport must be valid for the duration of stay.
- Entry is for tourism, family visits, or short-term business — **not** for paid work or study.
- The 15-day clock starts from the date of entry, not the date of first landing.

## Port of Entry: What to Expect

### Arrival card

As of 2024, paper arrival cards have been **phased out** at most major airports. Immigration is handled electronically — you will be photographed and fingerprinted at the border.

### E-Gates

Many international airports now have **e-gates** for faster processing, though foreign passport holders are not always eligible. Follow signs to the correct queue.

### Customs

China's customs rules are similar to other countries:
- Up to USD 5,000 in cash (or equivalent) can be brought in without declaration.
- Declare items that exceed personal use quantities (electronics, expensive jewellery, medications).

## Practical Tips

- **Book your outbound ticket before you arrive** if using the transit exemption — immigration officers may ask to see it.
- **Register your accommodation within 24 hours** of arrival if staying outside a hotel (hotels do this automatically; Airbnb/private hosts may require you to register at a local police station).
- **Keep a photocopy of your passport** — or a photo on your phone — in case it is temporarily held by hotel staff.
- **Apply early.** Embassy appointment slots fill up months in advance in some countries.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I work on a tourist or transit visa?**
No. Working on an L visa or under the transit exemption is illegal and can result in deportation and a future entry ban.

**My country is not on the 15-day visa-free list yet. When will it be added?**
The Chinese government has stated an intention to expand the programme. Check the NIA website or your country's embassy for the latest.

**Can I extend my stay once I am in China?**
Tourist visas can sometimes be extended at a local Public Security Bureau (PSB) Exit-Entry Administration. Transit exemptions cannot be extended.
