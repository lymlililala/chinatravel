Summer in China is a fruit lover's paradise. While watermelon and grapes are common worldwide, China's seasonal treasures—lychee, bayberry (杨梅, yáng méi), honey plum (蜂糖李, fēng táng lǐ), and white peach (白凤桃, bái fèng táo)—offer unique flavors and textures that define the season. This guide explores these fruits, their origins, how to enjoy them, and practical tips for international travelers.

## The Stars of Chinese Summer Fruits

![Tianfuguosheyinger, Sichuan, Chengdu](/img/destinations/chinas-summer-fruits-guide/1.webp)

China's summer fruit lineup is diverse, but four stand out for their exceptional taste and cultural significance. Each has a short season, so timing is everything.

### Lychee (荔枝, lì zhī)

Lychee is perhaps the most iconic summer fruit in southern China. With its rough red shell and translucent, juicy flesh, it offers a sweet, floral flavor reminiscent of roses and grapes. The best lychees come from Guangdong and Hainan provinces, with peak season from May to July. Varieties like 'Feizixiao' (妃子笑) and 'Guiwei' (桂味) are prized for their fragrance and sweetness.

![Close-up of fresh lychees with red shells and white flesh on a wooden table](https://images.pexels.com/photos/8817557/pexels-photo-8817557.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

### Bayberry (杨梅, yáng méi) – Xianju Dongkui

Bayberry is a small, bumpy fruit that ranges from deep red to purple. The Dongkui variety from Xianju County (仙居县, Xiān Jū Xiàn) in Zhejiang Province is considered the "king of bayberries." These berries can grow as large as ping-pong balls, with a juicy, sweet-tart flavor that is incredibly refreshing. The season is short—only about two weeks in mid-to-late June.

![Hand holding a large, dark purple bayberry the size of a ping-pong ball](https://images.pexels.com/photos/6547669/pexels-photo-6547669.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

### Honey Plum (蜂糖李, fēng táng lǐ) – Guizhou Liuma

Honey plum, also known as "bee sugar plum," is a green-skinned plum with amber flesh that tastes intensely sweet (16–20° Brix) with a hint of tartness. It is grown in the mountains of Liuma Town (六马镇, Liù Mǎ Zhèn) in Guizhou Province. The flesh is crisp and separates cleanly from the pit. Peak season is late June to early July.

![Green honey plums with a slight yellow blush on a rustic wooden surface](https://images.pexels.com/photos/6208141/pexels-photo-6208141.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

### White Peach (白凤桃, bái fèng táo) – Longquanyi

White peaches from Longquanyi (龙泉驿区, Lóng Quán Yì Qū) in Chengdu, Sichuan, are renowned for their fragrant, melt-in-your-mouth flesh and high sweetness (up to 17° Brix). The 'Xiao Baifeng' (小白凤) variety is available for only about 15 days in late June to early July. These peaches are soft, juicy, and perfect for eating fresh or in desserts.

## Where to Find Them: Regional Origins

![Lugu Lake Ligeban Island Scenery, Sichuan](/img/destinations/chinas-summer-fruits-guide/2.webp)

Each fruit has a specific region where it thrives, thanks to unique microclimates and soil.

### Xianju, Zhejiang – Bayberry Capital

Xianju County is nestled in the mountains of eastern Zhejiang, with over 100 peaks above 1,000 meters. The area's "horseshoe" terrain and sandy soil provide excellent drainage, while the combination of sea moisture and high altitude creates ideal conditions for Dongkui bayberries. The fruit is so valued that local farmers hang banners warning against pesticide use, and many orchards use physical pest control methods.

### Liuma, Guizhou – Honey Plum Haven

Liuma Town lies in a subtropical low-hot valley climate zone, often called a "natural greenhouse." The average annual sunshine is about 1,200 hours, and the area's red soil and mountain spring irrigation contribute to the plum's intense sweetness. The fruit is hand-picked and undergoes strict selection—only plums with a diameter of about 40mm and a sugar content above 16° make the cut.

### Longquanyi, Sichuan – Peach Paradise

Longquanyi has a 400-year history of peach cultivation. The area's forest cover and mineral-rich soil produce peaches with exceptional aroma. Longquanyi peaches are a national geographical indication product and were even selected as a recommended fruit for the 2008 Beijing Olympics. They ripen about a month earlier than famous Jiangsu peaches, making them a more affordable option.

## How to Enjoy Them Like a Local

### Eat Fresh and Seasonal

The best way to enjoy these fruits is fresh, at peak ripeness. Locals often buy them from street vendors or directly from orchards. Look for fruits that are fragrant, slightly soft to the touch (for peaches and plums), and free of blemishes.

### Creative Ways to Use Them

- **Bayberry Soda**: Muddle 2-3 large bayberries in a glass, add ice, and top with soda water for a refreshing summer drink.
- **Lychee Cocktails**: Lychees pair well with gin or vodka. Simply muddle a few peeled lychees, add spirit, ice, and a splash of lime juice.
- **Peach Desserts**: White peaches are excellent in fruit salads, tarts, or simply chilled and eaten with a spoon.
- **Honey Plum Snacking**: Eat them out of hand—the skin is thin and edible, and the flesh is crisp and sweet.

### Storage Tips

- **Bayberries**: Highly perishable. Refrigerate and eat within 2-3 days. Do not wash until ready to eat.
- **Lychees**: Keep in a plastic bag in the fridge; they last about a week. The skin may darken but the flesh stays good.
- **Honey Plums**: Store at room temperature if eating within 2 days; refrigerate for longer storage. Let them ripen until slightly soft for maximum sweetness.
- **Peaches**: If firm, leave at room temperature for a day or two. Once ripe, refrigerate and eat within 3 days. The fuzz protects the skin—wash only before eating.

## Practical Tips for Travelers

![Shunanzhuhai Landscape](/img/destinations/chinas-summer-fruits-guide/3.webp)

### Best Time to Visit for Fruit

- **May-June**: Lychee season begins in the south.
- **Mid-to-late June**: Bayberry and honey plum peak.
- **Late June to early July**: White peach season.

Plan your trip to coincide with these windows if fruit is a priority. Many orchards offer pick-your-own experiences.

### How to Get to the Regions

- **Xianju**: Take a high-speed train from Shanghai or Hangzhou to Xianju Station (about 2 hours from Hangzhou). From there, local buses or taxis reach the orchards.
- **Liuma**: Fly to Guiyang, then take a bus or drive to Zhenning County (about 2 hours). Liuma is another hour by car.
- **Longquanyi**: Take Chengdu Metro Line 2 to Longquanyi Station, then a short taxi ride to the peach-growing areas.

### Buying and Payment

- At local markets, prices vary by quality. Bayberries might cost ¥30-60 per 500g; honey plums ¥40-80; peaches ¥20-40. Lychees are often ¥15-30 per 500g.
- Mobile payment (Alipay, WeChat Pay) is widely accepted. Carry some cash for small vendors.
- For online ordering, platforms like Taobao and JD.com deliver fresh fruit nationwide. Look for stores with high ratings and "origin direct delivery" (产地直发).

### Etiquette and Tips

- When buying from street vendors, it's acceptable to ask to taste one piece before purchasing.
- Avoid squeezing fruits too hard; they bruise easily.
- If visiting an orchard, ask permission before picking. Many charge a small fee for entry and per kilogram picked.
