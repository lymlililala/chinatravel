Nanluoguxiang (南锣鼓巷) is Beijing's most visited hutong lane — a 800-metre north-south alley lined with craft shops, ice cream vendors, independent cafes, and foreign tourists. It's simultaneously one of the best entry points to hutong culture and a victim of its own success.

The trick is to use Nanluoguxiang as a starting point rather than a destination, and to leave the main lane for the side alleys that branch off it — where Beijing actually lives.

## Nanluoguxiang Itself

**The main lane**: Lined with a mix of original hutong architecture (1-2 story grey tile buildings on both sides) and modern commercial shopfronts. The street is closed to vehicles and becomes very crowded from 10am onwards on weekends and holidays.

**Best time on the main lane**: 7:30–9am. The stalls setting up, the local residents getting breakfast, the lane quiet before the tourist tide.

**The food on the main lane**: Mostly tourist-oriented snacks (fried dough sticks, ice cream, fruit sticks). The few genuine Beijing street breakfast vendors (jianbing 煎饼 stalls, dou jiang (豆浆) soy milk shops) that open early morning are the most interesting.

## The Side Alleys (胡同横街)

Eight traditional hutong lanes branch off each side of Nanluoguxiang, most running east-west. These are where the neighbourhood life is:

**Mao'er Hutong (帽儿胡同)**: Running east from the northern section. The best preserved courtyard architecture of the Nanluoguxiang network — several residences with original Qing dynasty gate carving visible. The end of the lane connects to the Shichahai lake area.

**Jing'a Hutong (景儿胡同)**: Quieter than Mao'er; more residential. The lane has a number of original courtyard gates (四合院大门) with different architectural grades visible — the number and size of gate stones indicating the historical social status of the resident.

**Qiangu'an Hutong (前鼓角胡同)**: Running west toward the Drum Tower — longer lane with mix of original residents and artists' studios.

## The History

Nanluoguxiang is located in the eastern part of what was the inner city during the Ming and Qing dynasties. The northern half of the lane, near the Drum Tower, was a military garrison area. The southern sections were mixed residential.

**The Yuan dynasty layout**: The lane itself follows the Yuan dynasty (13th century) street grid that remains the foundation of Beijing's inner city street pattern. Walking Nanluoguxiang is walking a 750-year-old urban plan.

## Drum Tower to Shichahai via Side Lanes

The most satisfying hutong walk:

Start at the **Drum Tower** (Gulou) → walk south on any of the western lanes → pass through the Shichahai lake area (Houhai Bar Lake visible between buildings) → continue south through the Mao'er Hutong section → arrive at Nanluoguxiang south entrance → walk north on Nanluoguxiang to the start point.

Total distance: 3–4km. Time: 2–3 hours with stops.
