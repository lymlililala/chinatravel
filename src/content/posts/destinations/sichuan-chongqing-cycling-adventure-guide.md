Imagine swapping the crowded tourist buses for two wheels, winding through misty mountain passes, stopping for steaming bowls of hand-pulled noodles, and feeling the raw pulse of China's heartland. This cycling adventure from Xi'an (西安) to Chongqing (重庆) via the Qinling Mountains (秦岭) is not just a physical challenge—it's a deep dive into the culinary and cultural soul of Shaanxi and Sichuan. Whether you're a seasoned cyclist or a curious traveler looking for an authentic experience, this guide covers everything from route planning and gear to must-try local dishes and practical tips for foreigners.

## Table of contents

## Why Cycle the Qinling?
The Qinling range is China's geographical north-south divide. Pedaling through it means crossing from wheat-loving, noodle-centric Shaanxi into rice-and-spice Sichuan. The route follows the Mei-Tai Highway (眉太线) and the G210国道, offering moderate climbs (most under 2,000m), well-paved roads, and frequent villages for supplies. You'll witness terraced fields, misty peaks, and the legacy of ancient trade routes like the Baoxie Plank Road (褒斜古道).

## Best Time to Go
Spring (April-May) and autumn (September-October) are ideal. Summer can be rainy and humid; winter brings snow and ice at higher elevations. The rider in our reference started in May and encountered rain, so pack waterproofs regardless of season.

## Getting There

![Baodingshan Stone Carving, Chongqing](/img/destinations/sichuan-chongqing-cycling-adventure-guide/1.webp)
Fly into Xi'an Xianyang International Airport (XIY). Budget airlines like China Eastern often have fares under ¥700. Bicycles fly as checked luggage (20kg included). At Xi'an airport, you cannot assemble your bike inside the terminal. Take bus #802 to Konggang International Business Center (空港国际商务中心) for ¥2, then assemble on the sidewalk. Alternatively, take a taxi to a nearby park.

## Route Overview: Xi'an to Chongqing (approx. 700km, 7-10 days)

### Day 1: Xi'an → Yinge Town (鹦鸽镇) — 125km
- **Terrain**: Mostly flat to rolling along the northern foothills of Qinling.
- **Highlights**: Start in Xi'an, cycle through Xianyang (咸阳), Xingping (兴平), Wugong (武功), and Yangling (杨凌).
- **Food stop**: Yangling's "Belt Noodles" (杨凌蘸水面) at a local shop. Order 4 noodles (¥3 each) plus a vegetable pancake (¥10). The noodles are as long as your belt!
- **Accommodation**: Huiquan Supermarket Guesthouse (汇全批发超市) in Yinge Town, ¥60/night. Basic but clean, with shared bathroom. Call ahead: the owner's number is on the door.
- **Dinner**: Dequan Restaurant (德泉酒家) for spicy fish hotpot (¥40).

### Day 2: Yinge Town → Taibai County (太白县) — 45km
- **Terrain**: Steady climb into the mountains.
- **Highlights**: Ride through misty forests, pass Qingfeng Gorge National Forest Park (青峰峡国家森林公园), and catch views of Aoshan (鳌山, 3,476m).
- **Food**: Breakfast at a local noodle shop (¥9 for a bowl of saozi noodles). Lunch is scarce—pack snacks.
- **Accommodation**: Several hotels in Taibai County, around ¥80-120.

### Day 3: Taibai County → Jiangkou Town (江口镇) — 80km
- **Terrain**: Descent then rolling hills.
- **Highlights**: Follow the Baohe River, pass ancient plank road remnants, and enjoy views of the Han River valley.
- **Food**: Try local cold rice noodles (凉皮) and roujiamo (肉夹馍).
- **Accommodation**: Simple guesthouses in Jiangkou, ¥50-80.

### Day 4: Jiangkou → Hanzhong (汉中) — 60km
- **Terrain**: Mostly flat through the Hanzhong Basin.
- **Highlights**: Explore Hanzhong's Han Dynasty history, visit the Hanzhong Museum or the Baijiang Terrace (拜将坛).
- **Food**: Hanzhong is famous for its hot dry noodles (热干面) and fish dishes.
- **Accommodation**: Mid-range hotels in Hanzhong, ¥100-150.

### Day 5: Hanzhong → Guangyuan (广元) — 120km
- **Terrain**: Rolling hills, crossing into Sichuan.
- **Highlights**: Enter Sichuan Province, pass through the Jianmen Pass (剑门关) area—a dramatic gorge with a historic pass.
- **Food**: Sichuan-style hotpot or chuanchuanxiang (串串香) in Guangyuan.
- **Accommodation**: Budget hotels, ¥80-120.

### Day 6: Guangyuan → Nanchong (南充) — 150km
- **Terrain**: Flat to rolling, following the Jialing River.
- **Highlights**: Ride through the Sichuan countryside, see terraced fields and traditional farmhouses.
- **Food**: Nanchong's famous "Zhu Bajie" braised pork or local street snacks.
- **Accommodation**: Hotels in Nanchong, ¥100-150.

### Day 7: Nanchong → Chongqing — 150km
- **Terrain**: Rolling hills, then a long descent into the Chongqing basin.
- **Highlights**: Arrive in the mountainous megacity of Chongqing. Celebrate with a hotpot feast!
- **Food**: Chongqing hotpot (重庆火锅) is a must—spicy, numbing, and unforgettable.
- **Accommodation**: Hostels or budget hotels in Chongqing's Yuzhong District, ¥80-150.

## Getting Around on the Bike
- **Navigation**: Use Alipay's Amap (高德地图) or Baidu Maps (百度地图) in English mode. Download offline maps.
- **Roads**: G108 and G210 are the main highways. They are well-paved but can have heavy truck traffic. Use a rear-view mirror and bright lights.
- **Supplies**: Villages every 10-20km have small shops for water, snacks, and basic repair tools. Stock up on food before long climbs.
- **Accommodation**: Small towns have guesthouses (招待所) or hotels (酒店). Prices range ¥50-150. Always ask to see the room first.

## Food Along the Route

![Jinyun Mountain Scenery, Chongqing](/img/destinations/sichuan-chongqing-cycling-adventure-guide/2.webp)
This trip is a gastronomic adventure. Key dishes to try:
- **Yangling Belt Noodles (杨凌蘸水面)**: Thick, chewy noodles dipped in a tomato-egg-chili sauce.
- **Saozi Noodles (臊子面)**: Minced pork, tofu, and vegetables in a spicy broth—a Shaanxi classic.
- **Roujiamo (肉夹馍)**: Chinese "hamburger"—braised pork in a crispy bun.
- **Chongqing Hotpot (重庆火锅)**: The ultimate Sichuan experience. Order tripe, beef, and vegetables. Be prepared for the numbing spice of Sichuan peppercorns.
- **Local snacks**: Cherry tomatoes (¥8/jin), cold noodles, and street-side grilled skewers.

## Where to Stay
- **Budget**: Guesthouses (¥50-80) are basic but safe. The Huiquan Supermarket Guesthouse in Yinge Town is a good example.
- **Mid-range**: Hotels in county towns like Taibai or Hanzhong (¥100-150) offer private bathrooms and WiFi.
- **Luxury**: In Xi'an and Chongqing, international chains like Holiday Inn or local boutique hotels (¥300+).

## Practical Tips for Foreigners
- **Visa**: Most nationalities need a tourist visa (L visa). If you're transiting through a major city like Xi'an or Chongqing, check if you qualify for 144-hour visa-free transit.
- **Mobile Payment**: Alipay and WeChat Pay are essential. Link your international credit card (Visa/Mastercard) before departure. Carry ¥500-1000 cash for small shops.
- **SIM Card**: Buy a local SIM at the airport (China Unicom or China Mobile) with data. A VPN is necessary for Google, Facebook, etc.
- **Language**: Learn basic phrases: "Nǐ hǎo" (hello), "Xièxiè" (thanks), "Duōshao qián?" (how much?), "Zhège hěn hǎochī" (this is delicious). Use Google Translate offline.
- **Safety**: Roads can be narrow with trucks. Wear a helmet, use lights, and ride defensively. In rain, watch for falling rocks on mountain roads.
- **Health**: Altitude is not an issue (max ~2,000m). Carry a basic first-aid kit and sunblock. Drink bottled water.

## Is This Route for You?
This trip is ideal for intermediate cyclists comfortable with 60-150km days and moderate climbs. You don't need to be a pro, but a good fitness level helps. The route is well-supported with villages and guesthouses, so you can adjust daily distances. If you want a shorter taste, cycle Xi'an to Hanzhong (3-4 days) and take a bus back.
