China's vast geography and diverse climates produce an incredible array of fruits, each with its own loyal following and peak season. From the succulent, ping-pong-sized bayberries of Zhejiang to the honey-sweet plums of Guizhou, the juicy peaches of Shaanxi, and the historic apricots of Shandong, these regional gems offer a taste of local terroir. This guide explores four of China's most celebrated fruits, where and when to find them, and how to enjoy them like a local.

## Table of contents

## Xianju Dongkui Bayberry (仙居东魁杨梅)

![A close-up of large, dark red bayberries on a rustic wooden table, with one cut open to show juicy flesh](https://images.pexels.com/photos/1118331/pexels-photo-1118331.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

Xianju County in Zhejiang Province is the undisputed king of bayberries (杨梅, yáng méi), specifically the Dongkui variety. These berries are famed for their impressive size—often compared to ping-pong balls—and their explosive juiciness. The local government strictly regulates pesticide use, and many orchards use organic methods, making them safe to eat straight from the tree. The flavor is a perfect balance of sweet and tart, with a distinct, aromatic fragrance.

**Best time to visit:** Mid-to-late June to early July. The season is short—only about two weeks.

**Where to buy:** The best bayberries come from high-altitude orchards (over 100m) in villages like Xiaoyang Village (肖垟村). Local markets in Xianju sell them, but for the freshest experience, visit a farm or order directly from growers. Prices range from ¥80-100 per jin (500g) at local markets, with premium gift boxes costing more.

**How to eat:** Rinse gently and eat fresh. Many locals eat them without soaking. For a treat, freeze them for a natural sorbet or make bayberry sparkling water. They are highly perishable—consume within 1-2 days of purchase.

## Shaanxi Xiameng Peach (陕西夏梦水蜜桃)

![A ripe, pink-blushed peach on a tree branch with green leaves, sunlight filtering through](https://images.pexels.com/photos/31737462/pexels-photo-31737462.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

Shaanxi Province, once China's top fruit-producing region, offers the Xiameng peach (夏梦水蜜桃, xià mèng shuǐ mì táo) in late June to July. Grown on the fertile Guanzhong Plain (关中平原) and irrigated by the Wei River, these peaches are known for their intense, old-fashioned peach flavor, thin skin, and juicy, melting flesh. They are harvested at 80% ripeness and arrive with a balance of sweetness and a hint of acidity.

**Best time to visit:** Late June to July.

**Where to buy:** Direct from orchards in the Guanzhong region or online. A 2.5-jin (1.25kg) box can cost as little as ¥40, making them an excellent value compared to more famous peach varieties.

**How to eat:** Let them ripen at room temperature for 2-3 days for peak sweetness. Eat fresh, or use in desserts. The skin is edible.

## Guizhou Fengtang Li Plum (贵州蜂糖李)

![A pile of greenish-yellow plums with a waxy bloom, one cut in half showing the pit](https://images.pexels.com/photos/8914340/pexels-photo-8914340.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

Hailing from the Liuma Township (六马镇) in Zhenning County, Guizhou Province, the Fengtang Li (蜂糖李, fēng táng lǐ) is often called the "ceiling of plums." Its greenish-yellow skin and crisp, juicy flesh offer an extraordinary sweetness (16-20° Brix) with just a hint of tartness. It is a clingstone variety that separates cleanly from the pit. The unique microclimate of the subtropical valley—with ample sunshine and mountain spring water irrigation—creates its exceptional flavor.

**Best time to visit:** Late June to July. The season is short, so plan accordingly.

**Where to buy:** The best quality comes from the core production area of Liuma. Look for plums with a slight yellow tinge and a firm-yet-springy feel. Premium fruit is often sold in gift boxes with individual compartments. Prices are high due to limited supply; expect ¥100+ per jin.

**How to eat:** Eat fresh. Let slightly green plums ripen for 2-3 days at room temperature. They are perfect for snacking and make a refreshing summer treat.

## Qingdao Shaoshan Red Apricot (青岛少山红杏)

![A basket of red-and-yellow apricots with a rustic background, one split open to show the pit](https://images.pexels.com/photos/9399898/pexels-photo-9399898.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

Grown at the foot of Laoshan Mountain in Qingdao, Shandong Province, the Shaoshan Red Apricot (少山红杏, shǎo shān hóng xìng) has a history dating back to the Ming Dynasty. Legend says it was named "Guanyelian" (关爷脸) by Emperor Wanli after its red-and-yellow complexion resembles the face of the warrior Guan Yu. These apricots are sweet (15° Brix or higher) with negligible acidity, a melting texture, and abundant juice. They are freestone and have a rich, aromatic flavor.

**Best time to visit:** Mid-to-late June. The season lasts less than two weeks.

**Where to buy:** Direct from orchards in the Shaoshan area of Chengyang District. Many farms offer on-site picking or shipping. A 3-jin box costs around ¥88, while a 4-jin box is about ¥108.

**How to eat:** Eat fresh when the fruit is half-yellow, half-red. The skin has a slight tartness; peel if desired. They are excellent for jams or drying.

## Practical Tips for Fruit Lovers in China

### Best Time to Visit for Fruit

| Fruit | Peak Season | Region |
|-------|-------------|--------|
| Xianju Bayberry | Mid-June to early July | Zhejiang |
| Shaanxi Peach | Late June to July | Shaanxi |
| Guizhou Fengtang Li | Late June to July | Guizhou |
| Qingdao Apricot | Mid-to-late June | Shandong |

### How to Get There

- **Xianju:** Take a high-speed train to Xianju South Station from Shanghai (about 2 hours) or Hangzhou (1 hour). Local buses or taxis reach the orchards.
- **Shaanxi peach region:** Fly or take a train to Xi'an, then drive to the Guanzhong Plain orchards (about 1 hour).
- **Guizhou Liuma:** Fly to Guiyang, then take a bus or drive to Zhenning County (about 2 hours). Liuma is a further 1-hour drive.
- **Qingdao Shaoshan:** Fly or take a train to Qingdao, then take metro Line 1 to Chengyang and a taxi to Shaoshan (30 minutes).

### Booking and Payment

- **Mobile payment:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are widely accepted. International visitors can link their foreign credit cards to these apps (see our [practical tips](/tags/culture) guide).
- **Tickets:** For farm visits or picking experiences, it's best to contact the farm in advance via WeChat or phone. Many farms accept walk-ins but may have limited stock.
- **Visa:** Most travelers need a tourist visa (L visa). Citizens of certain countries can use the 144-hour transit visa in cities like Shanghai, Xi'an, and Qingdao.

### Where to Stay

- **Xianju:** Stay in Xianju County town for easy access to orchards. Budget hotels start at ¥200/night; mid-range options around ¥400.
- **Shaanxi:** Base yourself in Xi'an and take day trips. Xi'an has a wide range of accommodations.
- **Guizhou:** Stay in Zhenning County or Anshun City for proximity to Liuma. Anshun has more options.
- **Qingdao:** Stay in Qingdao city center (e.g., Shinan District) and take a short trip to Shaoshan.

### What to Eat

Beyond the fruits themselves, each region has local specialties:

- **Xianju:** Try bayberry wine or bayberry juice.
- **Shaanxi:** Sample the famous Biang Biang noodles and lamb paomo.
- **Guizhou:** Don't miss sour fish soup (suān tāng yú) and spicy hotpot.
- **Qingdao:** Enjoy fresh seafood and Tsingtao beer.

### Costs

| Item | Approximate Cost (per person) |
|------|-------------------------------|
| High-speed train (Shanghai-Xianju) | ¥200-300 |
| Local taxi (10km) | ¥30-50 |
| Fruit (per jin) | ¥40-150 (varies by fruit and quality) |
| Farm visit/picking | ¥50-100 (often includes fruit) |
| Budget hotel | ¥200-400/night |

### Etiquette and Tips

- **Tipping:** Not customary in China.
- **Bargaining:** Acceptable at local markets but not at established farms or stores.
- **Language:** English is not widely spoken in rural areas. Use translation apps like Pleco or Google Translate.
- **Freshness:** Always ask when the fruit was picked. Fresher is better.
- **Storage:** Most of these fruits are highly perishable. Eat within 1-3 days or refrigerate.
