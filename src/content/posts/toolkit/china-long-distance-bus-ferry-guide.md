China's high-speed rail network is impressive, but it reaches fewer than 400 cities. Beyond those hubs lies a vast network of long-distance buses, scenic ferry routes, and rural minibuses that are essential for reaching some of the country's most rewarding destinations. This guide covers everything you need to know to use them comfortably as a foreigner.

## Long-Distance Buses (长途汽车)

![Landscape Scenery, Zhangjiajie](/img/toolkit/china-long-distance-bus-ferry-guide/1.webp)

### When buses are the right choice

Use long-distance buses when:
- The destination has no railway station (common in mountainous areas: Zhangjiajie old town, rural Guizhou, many parts of Yunnan)
- The bus route is more scenic than the train alternative
- The bus is significantly faster due to a more direct road route
- You're connecting between smaller cities not directly linked by train

### Types of long-distance buses

**Seated coaches (坐席客车)**: standard coaches for journeys under 4 hours. Reclining seats, air conditioning, sometimes on-board TV. Cost: ¥30–¥150 for most routes.

**Sleeper buses (卧铺客车)**: for overnight journeys over 6 hours. Narrow bunk beds arranged in rows (lower and upper berths). Mattress, pillow, blanket provided. Suited for night travel to avoid losing a day to travel. Cost: ¥80–¥250.

**Luxury coaches**: higher-spec vehicles with wider seats, more legroom, USB charging. Used on popular intercity routes. Prices slightly higher than standard.

## How to Find and Book Bus Tickets

### Bus stations

Every Chinese city has at least one long-distance bus station (汽车客运站 or 长途汽车站). Cities often have multiple stations serving different directions — confirm which station serves your destination before going.

**Finding the right station:**
- Search your starting city + "汽车站" in Amap to see all stations
- Ask your hotel — they usually know which station serves popular tourist destinations

### Buying tickets

**At the station in person**: most reliable for day-of or next-day travel. Show your passport and destination on a map (counters are usually marked in Chinese). Tickets are printed with your name and passport number.

**Via apps:**
- **12306 app** handles some long-distance bus routes in addition to trains
- **Fliggy** and **Trip.com** have limited bus booking
- **Ba Shi Yu Ding (巴士预订)**: a dedicated long-distance bus booking mini-program within WeChat

**Practical note**: For remote destinations in Yunnan, Guizhou, or Sichuan, booking in person at the station on the day of travel is often the only option — don't worry about pre-booking.

### What to bring on the bus

- Passport (required for boarding and ID check)
- Small day bag only (large luggage goes in the hold — ensure you get a baggage tag)
- Snacks and water for journeys over 3 hours (stops are brief and roadside options are limited)
- Motion sickness medication if needed (mountain roads in Southwest China are very winding)

## Bus Safety and Comfort Tips

![Shilihualang Scenery, Zhangjiajie, Hunan](/img/toolkit/china-long-distance-bus-ferry-guide/2.webp)

**Seats 1–5 in the front**: avoid on mountain roads — the front offers the most dramatic view of precipitous drops. If you're prone to anxiety on mountain roads, mid-bus or rear seats feel more stable.

**Luggage in the hold**: confirm your large bags are loaded and get the stub. At the destination, your bags are unloaded to the pavement — collect them promptly.

**Sleeper buses**: upper bunks rock more but are less disturbed by fellow passengers getting on and off. Lower bunks are easier to access for toilet trips (stop approximately every 3–4 hours).

## Ferry Services in China

### Cross-river and lake ferries

China's geography means ferries are often the quickest connection:

**Yangtze River ferries**: scheduled passenger ferries operate along the Yangtze between Chongqing and Yichang, passing through the Three Gorges. This is both a practical transport option and one of China's iconic scenic journeys (3 days downstream, 4 days upstream).

**Li River boats (Guilin–Yangshuo)**: the famous 4.5-hour cruise from Guilin to Yangshuo is technically a tourist ferry but genuinely the most practical way to reach Yangshuo from Guilin while seeing the karst landscape.

**Qiandao Lake and Erhai Lake**: scenic ferries connect islands and lakeshore towns in Zhejiang and Yunnan respectively.

### Island ferries

**Putuoshan (普陀山)**: the sacred Buddhist island near Zhoushan is reached by ferry from Zhoushan port (mainland) or Shenjiamen. Ferries run regularly and take 30–60 minutes.

**Hainan connections**: fast ferries connect Hainan Island to mainland Guangdong (Zhanjiang Xuwen port to Haikou). Journey: 1.5 hours. An alternative to flying or driving on the bridge.

**Zhoushan Archipelago**: ferry network connects dozens of islands off Zhejiang coast.

**Xiamen–Gulangyu**: short ferry (10 minutes, ¥35–¥50) between Xiamen city and Gulangyu Island. This is a required crossing as no vehicles are allowed on the island.

### Yangtze Three Gorges Cruise: Practical Details

For travellers wanting to experience the Three Gorges:

| Route | Duration | Best season |
|-------|---------|------------|
| Chongqing → Yichang (downstream) | 3 days | April–November |
| Yichang → Chongqing (upstream) | 4 days | April–November |

**Booking**: Victoria Cruises, President Cruises, and Century Cruises offer English-language services. Chinese state ferry companies also operate, at much lower cost, but are Chinese-language only.

**What's included**: cabin, meals on board, guided excursions to the Three Gorges dam, Shibao Rock, and other sites.

## Rural Transport: Minibuses and Shared Taxis

In rural China and minority village areas, transport often means:

**Minibuses (面包车/小巴)**: 12-seat vans running on informal routes between villages and town centres. They leave when full, have no fixed schedule, and cost ¥5–¥20 for short routes. Essential for reaching Miao villages in Guizhou, Tibetan villages in Sichuan, and similar.

**Shared taxis (拼车)**: drivers outside long-distance bus stations offer shared rides to nearby destinations — particularly useful when the last bus has gone. Negotiate the price (per person, not the whole car) before getting in.

**Tourist shuttles**: popular tourist destinations (Zhangjiajie, Yellow Mountain, Jiuzhaigou, Guilin's countryside) run official tourist buses on set routes. These are often the most convenient option and accept foreign visitors without language issues.
